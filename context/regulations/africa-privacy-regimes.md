## What it is

This pack covers the data protection regimes an organisation most often meets when it has customers, staff, vendors or infrastructure in Anglophone West and East Africa and North Africa. There is no single "African GDPR": each state legislates separately, the AU Malabo Convention sets only high-level obligations for the states that ratify it, and regional economic communities (ECOWAS, EAC, SADC) add non-binding or partially binding model laws. The practical consequence is a patchwork of similar principles with very different administrative mechanics — registration, licensing, audit filings and permits — which is where most enforcement actually happens.

Two generations of law coexist. The newer statutes (Nigeria 2023, Kenya 2019, Rwanda 2021, Tanzania 2022, Uganda 2019, Egypt 2020) borrow GDPR concepts — lawful bases, DPOs, DPIAs, breach notification with fixed clocks, turnover-linked fines — while retaining a regulator-registration model inherited from the 1998 UK Act lineage. The older statutes (Ghana 2012, Morocco 2009) are declaration-and-notification regimes without DPIAs or breach clocks; both countries are now replacing or reforming them.

### Country notes

**Nigeria.** The NDPA replaced the NDPR 2019 and created the NDPC. Detail lives in the GAID (NDPC/NDP ACT-GAID/01/2025, issued 20 March 2025; reported effective 19 September 2025, secondary), which fixes the DCPMI classification, the Compliance Audit Return (CAR) regime (late filing adds 50% of the filing fee; non-filing exposes the entity to s. 48 penalties), semi-annual internal DPO reports, DPIA and legitimate-interest templates, and the adequacy method in Schedule 5. The 2025 CAR deadline was extended to 30 May 2026 (secondary). Breach notifications must describe circumstances, timing, data involved and mitigation (GAID Art. 33(5)).

**Kenya.** The DPA commenced 25 November 2019; the Registration, General and Complaints-Handling Regulations of 2021 took effect six months after publication. Registration is the compliance hook: the certificate lasts 24 months and lapsing while processing is an offence. The ODPC actively issues enforcement and penalty notices and compensation orders; a draft Data Protection (Amendment) Bill 2025 (AI, cross-border and penalty provisions) has circulated but is not enacted as of September 2026 (verify).

**Ghana.** Act 843 was gazetted 18 May 2012 and commenced 16 October 2012 (regulator website, secondary). It is a registration-and-renewal regime with penalty-unit fines and no breach clock. The **Data Protection Bill 2025** — consulted October–November 2025 and still being prepared for Parliament as of March 2026 — would repeal Act 843 (cl. 123), create a Data Protection Authority, impose a 72-hour breach notification with administrative penalties of 2,000–100,000 penalty units for missing it (cl. 51), penalise failure to register (cl. 82) and regulate cross-border transfers (cl. 96). Treat it as pending; do not cite it as law.

**Egypt.** Law 151/2020 was dormant until the Executive Regulations (Prime Ministerial / MCIT Decree 816 of 2025, issued 1 November 2025; secondary) activated licensing, DPO registration, breach procedures and transfer permits. The Law gives addressees one year from issuance of the Executive Regulations to comply (Art. 6, promulgating law), so full enforcement is expected from 1 November 2026. The PDPC's licensing portal was expected mid-2026 (secondary). Breach notification content under Art. 7 mirrors GDPR Art. 33 (nature, DPO contact, consequences, remediation).

**Morocco.** Law 09-08 (Dahir 1-09-15 of 18 February 2009, BO 5714) is a CNDP declaration/authorisation regime with criminal-style sanctions and no DPIA, accountability or breach-notification duties. Reform to align with GDPR has been under discussion for years; no replacement text had been adopted as of mid-2026 (secondary). Morocco has not signed the Malabo Convention. Processor contracts must bind the processor to controller instructions and security measures (Art. 23).

**Rwanda.** Law 058/2021 (gazetted and in force 15 October 2021; two-year transition ended 15 October 2023) is the most GDPR-like regime here, with the addition of mandatory registration, mandatory personal-data logging (collection, alteration, access, disclosure, erasure), records of processing, and a default data-localisation rule. Enforcement moved into an accountability phase from 2024–2026 (secondary).

**Uganda.** The DPPA (assented 25 February 2019) is enforced by the PDPO created under the 2021 Regulations. Its breach rule is the strictest wording on the continent ("immediately") but the Authority controls whether data subjects are told. Corporate offences carry a turnover-based fine on top of currency-point fines.

**Tanzania.** The PDPA (Act No. 11 of 2022) commenced 1 May 2023 (secondary), with the Personal Data Collection and Processing Regulations (GN 449C of 2023) adding the transfer-permit procedure and fee bands. After repeated extensions the PDPC set 8 April 2026 as the final voluntary registration date and began enforcement on 9 April 2026 (secondary; regulator site not reachable at review).

**AU Malabo Convention.** Adopted 27 June 2014; entered into force 8 June 2023, thirty days after the fifteenth ratification (Art. 36). Chapter II (Arts. 8–23) obliges State Parties to legislate a data protection framework with an independent national authority (Art. 11), preliminary declaration/authorisation formalities (Art. 10), GDPR-like principles (Art. 13), sensitive-data prohibitions (Art. 14) and confidentiality, security, retention and sustainability duties (Arts. 20–23). It sets no breach clock or penalty levels — those are national. Chapter III covers national cybersecurity frameworks and cybercrime offences.

## Enforcement and penalties

- **Registration is enforced first.** Nigeria's NDPC issued compliance notices to over 1,300 organisations in 2025 and has imposed nine-figure naira fines (secondary); Tanzania and Kenya both run public registers and treat lapsed registration as an offence. Expect the first regulator question to be "show your certificate", not "show your DPIA".
- **Turnover-linked fines** exist in Nigeria (2% gross revenue, no cap), Kenya (1%, capped at KES 5 m and *whichever is lower*), Rwanda (1% administrative / 5% criminal) and Uganda (2% corporate). Egypt and Morocco rely on fixed-band fines plus imprisonment; Ghana on penalty units.
- **Criminal exposure for officers** is real in Uganda (s. 38, officers who knowingly permit the contravention), Rwanda (Arts. 56–61), Egypt (Economic Courts) and Morocco (Arts. 54–63, with equipment seizure and data erasure as ancillary orders).
- **Audit and reporting filings** are recurring obligations, not one-offs: Nigeria's annual CAR (31 March), Kenya's 24-month renewal, Ghana's two-year renewal with gap-analysis reports (secondary), Tanzania's five-year renewal.
- Appeals: Kenya s. 64 (administrative action), Rwanda Art. 54 (court application), Uganda s. 34 (30 days).

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Map establishment and targeting per country** — Nigeria, Kenya and Rwanda reach foreign controllers that process data of people located there; Uganda reaches processing of Ugandan citizens' data anywhere. Record the applicability decision per entity. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Register before you process** — Nigeria (if DCPMI), Kenya, Ghana, Rwanda, Uganda and Tanzania all require a certificate; Egypt requires a licence/permit from 2026; Morocco requires a declaration or authorisation. Track renewal dates (24 months Kenya, 2 years Ghana, 5 years Tanzania) and Nigeria's annual CAR in the compliance calendar.
3. **Wire the breach clocks into incident response** — 48 h Rwanda, 72 h Nigeria/Kenya/Egypt, "immediately" Uganda; log every notification in [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md) and follow [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md). Note the threshold differences: Kenya requires a "real risk of harm"; Nigeria "likely to result in a risk to rights and freedoms".
4. **Treat transfers as permit work, not paperwork** — Egypt, Tanzania and Rwanda require a regulator permit/authorisation per transfer; Rwanda and (by Cabinet Secretary order) Kenya can require local storage. Build these into vendor onboarding via [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
5. **Appoint and register DPOs** where mandatory (Nigeria DCPMI, Rwanda, Egypt) and file the Nigerian semi-annual internal DPO report; Ghana's DPC expects a certified data protection supervisor for large controllers (secondary).
6. **Run DPIAs** for high-risk processing in Nigeria, Kenya and Rwanda using [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md); Nigeria's GAID Schedule 4 template is the local format.
7. **Keep processing records and logs** — Rwanda mandates per-operation logging; Nigeria's CAR and Ghana's gap-analysis renewal both require evidence of processing inventories and policies. See [../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md](../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md).
8. **Track pending change** — Ghana Bill 2025, Egypt's 1 November 2026 enforcement, Kenya amendment proposals, Morocco reform — through [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md); log unavoidable gaps (e.g. localisation conflicts with group architecture) as exceptions under [../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md](../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR:** none of these countries holds an EU adequacy decision; Morocco is working towards one (secondary). EU-origin data flowing into Africa needs SCCs or another Chapter V mechanism, while African-origin data flowing to the EU needs the African regulator's permit or adequacy finding — plan both directions. See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md).
- **Malabo Convention vs national law:** the Convention binds only ratifying states and sets floors, not clocks or fines. Ghana and Rwanda are parties; Nigeria signed in 2024 but has not ratified; Kenya, Egypt, Morocco, Uganda and Tanzania are not parties. Do not rely on it as an operative obligation outside ratifying states.
- **Sectoral overlays:** Egypt carves banking data out to Central Bank rules; Nigeria's GAID Art. 2–3 addresses double regulation with sector regulators; Kenya s. 50 lets the Cabinet Secretary impose localisation for strategic-interest processing; Ghana's credit-reporting and electronic-communications laws add their own disclosure offences.
- **Breach timelines elsewhere:** compare with the 72-hour GDPR baseline and shorter regimes in [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md); Rwanda's 48-hour clock and Uganda's "immediately" are among the tightest globally.
- **Other non-EU regimes:** for South Africa's POPIA and the rest of the non-EU landscape see [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md).
