## What it is

This pack covers the data protection regimes an organisation most often meets when it has customers, staff, vendors or infrastructure in Anglophone West and East Africa and North Africa. There is no single "African GDPR": each state legislates separately, the AU Malabo Convention sets only high-level obligations for the states that ratify it, and regional economic communities (ECOWAS, EAC, SADC) add non-binding or partially binding model laws. The practical consequence is a patchwork of similar principles with very different administrative mechanics — registration, licensing, audit filings and permits — which is where most enforcement actually happens.

Two generations of law coexist. The newer statutes (Nigeria 2023, Kenya 2019, Rwanda 2021, Tanzania 2022, Uganda 2019, Egypt 2020) borrow GDPR concepts — lawful bases, DPOs, DPIAs, breach notification with fixed clocks, turnover-linked fines — while retaining a regulator-registration model inherited from the 1998 UK Act lineage. The older statutes (Ghana 2012, Morocco 2009) are declaration-and-notification regimes without DPIAs or breach clocks; both countries are now replacing or reforming them.

### Country notes

**Nigeria.** The NDPA replaced the NDPR 2019 and created the NDPC. Detail lives in the GAID (NDPC/NDP ACT-GAID/01/2025, issued March 2025), which fixes the DCPMI classification and registration fees, the Compliance Audit Return (CAR) regime, DPIA and legitimate-interest templates, and the adequacy method in Schedule 5. Entities of major importance established before 12 June 2023 file CAR by 31 March each year; those established later file within fifteen months of establishment (GAID Art. 10(7)–(8)). Late filing adds an administrative penalty of 50% of the filing fee (Art. 10(9)), and UHL and EHL entities must file through a licensed Data Protection Compliance Organisation (Art. 10(14)). Breach notifications must describe circumstances, timing, data involved and mitigation (GAID Art. 33(5)).

**Kenya.** The DPA commenced 25 November 2019; the Registration, General and Complaints-Handling Regulations of 2021 took effect six months after publication. Registration is the compliance hook: the certificate lasts 24 months and lapsing while processing is an offence. The ODPC actively issues enforcement and penalty notices and compensation orders; amendment proposals covering AI, cross-border transfers and penalties have circulated, but the ODPC's regulatory-framework page still lists only the 2019 Act and the 2021 Regulations as of September 2026, so treat any amendment as pending (verify).

**Ghana.** Act 843 carries a gazette date of 18 May 2012 on the Business Regulatory Reforms portal; the commencement date is commonly given as October 2012 (verify — the Data Protection Commission's own site was unreachable at review). It is a registration-and-renewal regime with penalty-unit fines and no fixed breach clock. A **Data Protection Bill** to repeal and replace Act 843 — creating a new authority, a fixed breach-notification clock and express cross-border transfer rules — was consulted on in late 2025 but had not been passed as of September 2026; the leading practitioner surveys still record Act 843 as Ghana's primary data protection legislation in March 2026. Treat the Bill as pending; do not cite it as law.

**Egypt.** Law 151/2020 entered into force three months after publication (Art. 7, promulgating law) but was dormant until the Executive Regulations (No. 816/2025, issued by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology on 1 November 2025; secondary) activated licensing, DPO registration, breach procedures and transfer permits. The Law gives addressees one year from issuance of the Executive Regulations to comply (Art. 6, promulgating law), so the grace period ends 31 October 2026 and enforcement is expected from 1 November 2026. Breach notification content under Art. 7 mirrors GDPR Art. 33 (nature, DPO contact, consequences, remediation).

**Morocco.** Law 09-08 (Dahir 1-09-15 of 18 February 2009, BO 5714) is a CNDP declaration/authorisation regime with criminal-style sanctions and no DPIA, accountability or breach-notification duties. Reform to align with GDPR has been under discussion for years; the CNDP was still administering Law 09-08 declarations, prior authorisations and transfer requests in September 2026, and no replacement text has been adopted. Morocco has not signed the Malabo Convention. Processor contracts must bind the processor to controller instructions and security measures (Art. 23).

**Rwanda.** Law No. 058/2021 of 13 October 2021 (gazetted 15 October 2021; two-year transition under Art. 67 ended 15 October 2023) is the most GDPR-like regime here, with the addition of mandatory registration, mandatory personal-data logging (collection, alteration, access, disclosure, erasure), records of processing, and a default data-localisation rule. The supervisory authority is the National Cyber Security Authority, which launched its Data Protection and Privacy Office on 31 March 2022; that office now runs registration, transfer and storage authorisations and breach reporting.

**Uganda.** The DPPA (assented 25 February 2019, gazetted and commenced 3 May 2019) is enforced by the PDPO created under the 2021 Regulations. Its breach rule is the strictest wording on the continent ("immediately") but the Authority controls whether data subjects are told. Corporate offences carry a turnover-based fine on top of currency-point fines.

**Tanzania.** The PDPA (Act No. 11 of 2022, now Chapter 44) was published in English as Government Notice 395B on 13 June 2023, with the Personal Data Collection and Processing Regulations (GN 449C of 2023) adding the transfer-permit procedure, DPIA duty and fee bands; the Act's own commencement date is not stated in the published text (verify). On 28 August 2026 the PDPC announced the start of on-site compliance inspections covering registration, DPO duties, cross-border transfers under ss. 31–32 and security measures under s. 27(1), with penalties for entities found non-compliant.

**AU Malabo Convention.** Adopted 27 June 2014; entered into force 8 June 2023, thirty days after the deposit of the fifteenth instrument of ratification (Mauritania, deposited 9 May 2023) (Art. 36). Chapter II (Arts. 8–23) obliges State Parties to legislate a data protection framework with an independent national authority (Art. 11), preliminary declaration/authorisation formalities (Art. 10), GDPR-like principles (Art. 13), sensitive-data prohibitions (Art. 14) and confidentiality, security, retention and sustainability duties (Arts. 20–23). It sets no breach clock or penalty levels — those are national. Chapter III covers national cybersecurity frameworks and cybercrime offences.

## Enforcement and penalties

- **Registration is enforced first.** Tanzania's PDPC opened an inspection campaign on 28 August 2026 whose first checkpoint is whether the entity appears on the Commission's register; Kenya makes acting as an unregistered controller or processor unlawful (s. 18(1)); Nigeria makes registration and the annual CAR the gateway obligations for entities of major importance. Expect the first regulator question to be "show your certificate", not "show your DPIA".
- **Turnover-linked fines** exist in Nigeria (2% of annual gross revenue, or NGN 10 m for DCPMI / NGN 2 m for others, whichever is greater), Kenya (KES 5 m or 1% of preceding-year turnover, whichever is lower), Rwanda (1% administrative / 5% on corporate conviction) and Uganda (2% of corporate annual gross turnover). Egypt and Morocco rely on fixed-band fines plus imprisonment; Ghana on penalty units; Tanzania mixes a TZS 100 m administrative ceiling with criminal fines up to TZS 5 bn.
- **Criminal exposure for officers** is real in Uganda (s. 38, officers who knowingly and wilfully authorise or permit the contravention), Rwanda (Arts. 56–61, up to ten years), Egypt (Economic Courts, Art. 5 of the promulgating law) and Morocco (Arts. 54–63; Art. 61 allows seizure of the equipment used and erasure of the data, and Art. 64 doubles fines for legal persons).
- **Audit and reporting filings** are recurring obligations, not one-offs: Nigeria's annual CAR (31 March), Kenya's 24-month renewal, Uganda's 12-month registration validity (Regs 2021, reg. 20), Ghana's two-year renewal with gap-analysis reports (secondary), Tanzania's five-year renewal.
- Appeals: Kenya s. 64 (to the High Court against any administrative action), Rwanda Art. 54 (application to the competent court), Uganda s. 34 (to the Minister within 30 days).

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Map establishment and targeting per country** — Nigeria, Kenya and Rwanda reach foreign controllers that process data of people located there; Uganda reaches processing of Ugandan citizens' data anywhere. Record the applicability decision per entity. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Register before you process** — Nigeria (if DCPMI), Kenya, Ghana, Rwanda, Uganda and Tanzania all require a certificate; Egypt requires a licence/permit from 2026; Morocco requires a declaration or authorisation. Track renewal dates (24 months Kenya, 12 months Uganda, 2 years Ghana, 5 years Tanzania) and Nigeria's annual CAR (31 March) in the compliance calendar.
3. **Wire the breach clocks into incident response** — 48 h Rwanda, 72 h Nigeria/Kenya/Egypt, "immediately" Uganda; log every notification in [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md) and follow [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md). Note the threshold differences: Kenya requires a "real risk of harm"; Nigeria "likely to result in a risk to rights and freedoms".
4. **Treat transfers as permit work, not paperwork** — Egypt, Tanzania and Rwanda require a regulator permit/authorisation per transfer; Rwanda and (by Cabinet Secretary order) Kenya can require local storage. Build these into vendor onboarding via [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
5. **Appoint and register DPOs** where mandatory — Nigeria (NDPA s. 32 as applied by GAID Art. 11), Rwanda (Art. 40), Egypt (Art. 8, with entry in the Centre's DPO record), Tanzania (s. 27(3)) and Uganda (Regs 2021, reg. 47); Ghana's DPC expects a certified data protection supervisor for large controllers (secondary).
6. **Run DPIAs** for high-risk processing — a statutory duty in Nigeria, Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania, and in Rwanda a duty the DPO must advise on and monitor (Art. 41) — using [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md); Nigeria's GAID Schedule 4 template is the local format, and Tanzania's Regs 2023 reg. 33 lists the triggering processing types.
7. **Keep processing records and logs** — Rwanda mandates per-operation logging; Nigeria's CAR and Ghana's gap-analysis renewal both require evidence of processing inventories and policies. See [../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md](../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md).
8. **Track pending change** — the Ghana Bill, Egypt's enforcement date of 1 November 2026, Kenya amendment proposals, Morocco reform — through [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md); log unavoidable gaps (e.g. localisation conflicts with group architecture) as exceptions under [../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md](../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR:** none of these countries holds an EU adequacy decision. EU-origin data flowing into Africa needs SCCs or another Chapter V mechanism, while African-origin data flowing to the EU needs the African regulator's permit or adequacy finding — plan both directions. See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md).
- **Malabo Convention vs national law:** the Convention binds only ratifying states and sets floors, not clocks or fines. Ghana (ratified 2019) and Rwanda (ratified 2019) are parties; Nigeria signed on 23 January 2024 but has not ratified; Kenya, Egypt, Morocco, Uganda and Tanzania have neither signed nor ratified. Do not rely on it as an operative obligation outside ratifying states.
- **Sectoral overlays:** Egypt carves banking data out to Central Bank rules; Nigeria's GAID Art. 2–3 addresses double regulation with sector regulators; Kenya s. 50 lets the Cabinet Secretary impose localisation for strategic-interest processing; Ghana's credit-reporting and electronic-communications laws add their own disclosure offences.
- **Breach timelines elsewhere:** compare with the 72-hour GDPR baseline and shorter regimes in [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md); Rwanda's 48-hour clock and Uganda's "immediately" are among the tightest globally.
- **Other non-EU regimes:** for South Africa's POPIA see [south-africa-popia.md](south-africa-popia.md); for the wider non-EU landscape see [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md). For the EU side of any outbound transfer see [eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md](eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md).
