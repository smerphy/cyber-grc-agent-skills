# China: Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law and Personal Information Protection Law (CSL / DSL / PIPL)

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | Cybersecurity Law (CSL, NPCSC 7 Nov 2016, effective 1 June 2017; amended 28 Oct 2025, amendments effective 1 Jan 2026); Data Security Law (DSL, effective 1 Sept 2021); Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL, effective 1 Nov 2021). Three national laws plus a thick layer of State Council regulations and CAC departmental rules |
| Lead regulator | Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC, national and provincial offices) coordinates; Ministry of Public Security (MPS) leads CII protection and MLPS; MIIT, sector regulators and "protection work departments" for CII sectors |
| Status (Sept 2026) | All three laws in force. Amended CSL applied from 1 Jan 2026. Network Data Security Management Regulations in force since 1 Jan 2025. 2025–2026 saw a wave of implementing rules: incident reporting (1 Nov 2025), PI compliance audits (1 May 2025), PI export certification (1 Jan 2026), network data risk assessment (20 Aug 2026), small-processor simplifications (1 Sept 2026); large-processor rules still in draft |
| Who is covered | CSL: "network operators" (owners, managers and network service providers) in China, with a heavier tier for critical information infrastructure operators (CIIOs). DSL: all data handling in mainland China, plus extraterritorial reach for activities harming national security or public interest. PIPL: handling of personal information (PI) in China and, extraterritorially, handling of PI of individuals in China to offer them products/services or analyse their behaviour (Art. 3) |
| Structure | CSL: network operation security (MLPS), CII, network information security, monitoring/emergency response, penalties. DSL: categorised and graded data protection (general / important / core data), security obligations, export control and blocking provisions. PIPL: legal bases, sensitive PI, individual rights, handler duties, cross-border transfer mechanisms |
| Cross-border transfers | Three mechanisms under PIPL Art. 38 (CAC security assessment, standard contract, certification), gated by volume thresholds set in the CAC Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (22 Mar 2024). Important data always needs a CAC security assessment; CIIOs must localise PI and important data (CSL Art. 39 as renumbered) |
| Breach / incident clocks | PIPL Art. 57: "immediately" notify regulator and individuals. CAC incident measures (from 1 Nov 2025): report "relatively major" or above incidents within 1 hour (CII), 2 hours (central/state organs) or 4 hours (all other operators); summary report within 30 days |
| Penalties | PIPL Art. 66: up to RMB 50 million or 5% of the previous year's turnover for grave violations, plus personal fines up to RMB 1 million and role bans. DSL Art. 45: up to RMB 10 million for core-data violations. Amended CSL Art. 61: up to RMB 10 million where CII loses its main functions |
| Certifiable? | No general certification of compliance. Formal schemes: MLPS grading/assessment (GB/T 22239-2019), PI protection certification (an export mechanism, certificate valid 3 years), voluntary certification of data-security risk-assessment providers |
| Neighbours | Sits alongside the Civil Code (personality rights) and the Criminal Law; PIPL is China's GDPR analogue but with a state-security spine (DSL/CSL) that GDPR lacks |

## What it is

China's data regime is a triad. The **CSL (2017)** created the foundational duties for anyone operating a network: the Multi-Level Protection Scheme (MLPS), incident emergency plans, real-name rules, and a protected tier for critical information infrastructure (CII) with data localisation and procurement security review. The **DSL (2021)** extended state supervision from networks to data itself: a national categorised-and-graded system with "important data" catalogues and a stricter "core data" tier, mandatory risk assessments for important-data handlers, export controls and a blocking rule against handing data to foreign judicial or law-enforcement bodies. The **PIPL (2021)** is the comprehensive personal-information statute: legal bases, separate consent for sensitive PI and transfers, impact assessments, extraterritorial reach, a local-representative requirement for foreign handlers, and turnover-based fines.

The **2025 CSL amendment** (adopted 28 Oct 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026) was the first revision since 2017. It added an artificial-intelligence article (state support for AI research, compute and training-data infrastructure, ethics norms, risk monitoring and safety supervision, and use of AI to improve cybersecurity), aligned CSL personal-information provisions with the PIPL and Civil Code, restructured the penalty chapter with a new two-tier "serious / particularly serious consequence" regime, and renumbered most articles (old Art. 21 MLPS is now Art. 23; old Art. 37 CII localisation is now Art. 39). Cite the amended numbering from 2026 onward.

Below the laws sit the **State Council regulations** — Critical Information Infrastructure Security Protection Regulations (Decree 745, effective 1 Sept 2021) and Network Data Security Management Regulations (Decree 790, effective 1 Jan 2025) — and a fast-growing set of **CAC departmental rules** that carry the operative thresholds and clocks. Most practical compliance questions (do we need a security assessment? how fast must we report?) are answered in that secondary layer, not in the laws.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Test | Detail |
|---|---|
| CSL "network operator" | Owners and managers of networks and network service providers (Art. 78 as renumbered). Any company running IT systems in mainland China qualifies |
| CIIO | Operators of important network facilities/systems in public communications and information services, energy, transport, water, finance, public services, e-government, defence science and industry, and any other system whose destruction, loss of function or data leak could seriously endanger national security, the economy, people's livelihood or the public interest (CII Regs Art. 2). Sector "protection work departments" issue identification rules, identify CII and notify the operator (Arts. 8–10); until notified, treat CII status as a risk, not a fact |
| DSL | Data handling activities and their security regulation within mainland China (Art. 2); "data" is any record of information in electronic or other form (Art. 3). Extraterritorial reach for handling outside China that harms national security, public interest or citizens' rights |
| PIPL | PI handling within China (Art. 3(1)). Extraterritorial (Art. 3(2)) where the purpose is to provide products or services to individuals in China, or to analyse or assess their behaviour. Such foreign handlers must establish a dedicated entity or appoint a representative in China and report it to the regulator (Art. 53); the Network Data Regs Art. 26 route that filing to the city-level CAC office |
| Sensitive PI | Biometrics, religious belief, specially designated status, medical health, financial accounts, location tracking, and PI of minors under 14 (PIPL Art. 28); separate consent required (Art. 29), guardian consent for under-14s (Art. 31) |
| Important data | Data in specific fields, groups or regions, or of a certain precision and scale, whose compromise could directly endanger national security, economic operation, social stability, public health and safety (Network Data Regs Art. 62(4)). Identified through sector/regional catalogues; a handler need not treat data as important for export purposes unless notified or the category is published (Art. 29; Cross-Border Provisions Art. 2) |
| Size tiers (PI volume) | < 100,000 individuals: "small processor" simplified regime (from 1 Sept 2026). > 1 million: must designate a PI protection officer responsible for audits (Audit Measures Art. 12). > 10 million: audit at least every two years (Audit Measures Art. 4) and important-data-processor governance duties (Network Data Regs Art. 28); draft "large processor" designation regime (consultation to 7 Sept 2026) |
| Large network platforms | ≥ 50 million registered users or ≥ 10 million monthly active users with complex business (Network Data Regs Art. 62(8)); annual PI protection social-responsibility report (Art. 44); PIPL Art. 58 duties (independent oversight body, platform rules, public reports) |

## Core obligations

### CSL (article numbers as amended, effective 1 Jan 2026)

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Art. 20 (new) | State support for AI: basic research and algorithms, training-data and compute infrastructure, ethics norms, risk monitoring/assessment and safety supervision; use of AI to raise cybersecurity protection |
| Art. 23 (old 21) | MLPS duties for all network operators: internal security systems and responsible persons, technical measures against attacks/intrusions, monitoring and logging with network logs retained at least six months, data classification, backup and encryption of important data |
| Art. 27 (old 25) | Cybersecurity incident emergency plans; prompt handling of vulnerabilities, malware, attacks and intrusions; activate the plan on an incident, take remedial measures and report to competent departments as provided |
| Art. 33 (old 31) | CII sectors and definition; State Council sets scope and protection measures |
| Art. 37 (old 35) | CIIO procurement of network products/services that may affect national security must pass national security review organised by CAC with State Council departments |
| Art. 39 (old 37) | CIIOs store PI and important data collected/generated in China domestically; export only after a security assessment under CAC-led measures |
| Art. 40 (old 38) | CIIOs: at least annual security testing and risk assessment, self-performed or by a cybersecurity service organisation, with reports to the protection work department |
| Art. 42 (old 40) + new para. | Network operators keep user information strictly confidential and, when handling PI, comply with the Civil Code and the PIPL |
| Art. 77 (old 75) | Foreign institutions, organisations or individuals endangering China's cybersecurity face legal liability; where consequences are serious, MPS and other departments may freeze assets or impose other necessary sanctions |

### DSL

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Art. 21 | Categorised and graded protection; important-data catalogues by regions/sectors; "core national data" (national security, economic lifelines, major public interests) under a stricter management system |
| Art. 24–25 | National security review of data activities affecting national security; export control on controlled data categories |
| Art. 27 | Whole-workflow data security management system, education and training, technical measures; network-based handling must build on MLPS |
| Art. 29 | Risk monitoring; immediate remediation of deficiencies; on a data security incident, act immediately, notify users and report to competent departments as provided |
| Art. 30 | Important-data handlers: periodic risk assessments with reports to competent departments (now annual under the 2026 Risk Assessment Measures) |
| Art. 31 | Export of important data: CSL rules for CIIOs; CAC-led measures for other handlers (now a mandatory CAC security assessment) |
| Art. 36 | No provision of data stored in China to foreign judicial or law-enforcement bodies without approval of competent PRC authorities — a blocking statute; route foreign discovery and subpoenas through counsel |

### PIPL

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Art. 13 | Seven legal bases: consent; contract necessity or HR management under lawful rules; legal duties; public-health emergencies and life/property protection; news reporting and public-interest supervision; lawfully disclosed PI within reasonable scope; other legal grounds |
| Arts. 23, 29, 39 | "Separate consent" for sharing PI with other handlers, handling sensitive PI, and cross-border provision (definition: a specific, explicit consent for a particular processing, Network Data Regs Art. 62(7)) |
| Art. 21 | Entrusted processing requires a contract covering purpose, duration, method, categories, protection measures and rights/duties, plus supervision of the processor |
| Art. 40 | CIIOs and handlers above CAC-set volumes must localise PI and pass a CAC security assessment to export |
| Art. 51 | Security measures scaled to risk: internal management systems, classified management, encryption/de-identification, access controls and training, incident response plans |
| Art. 52–54 | PI protection officer above CAC-set volume (1 million under the Audit Measures); local representative for foreign handlers; regular compliance audits |
| Art. 55–56 | Personal Information Protection Impact Assessment (PIPIA) before: sensitive PI, automated decision-making, entrusting/sharing/disclosing PI, cross-border provision, other high-impact handling. Content: lawfulness/necessity, impact and security risk, adequacy of measures. Reports and records retained at least 3 years |
| Art. 57 | On actual or possible leak, distortion or loss: immediate remediation and notification to regulators and individuals (categories, cause, harm; measures taken and available to individuals; contact). Individual notice may be skipped if measures effectively avoid harm, unless the regulator orders it |
| Art. 58 | Large platforms: compliance system with an independent, mainly external oversight body; platform rules; suspend seriously non-compliant in-platform providers; periodic social-responsibility reports |
| Art. 69–70 | Reversed burden of proof in civil claims (handler must prove no fault); public-interest litigation by procuratorates and designated organisations |

### Cross-border data transfer (CAC Provisions of 22 Mar 2024, Decree 16, in force on publication)

| Scenario (cumulative since 1 January of the current year, non-CIIO unless stated) | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Any export by a CIIO of PI or important data; any export of important data | CAC security assessment (Art. 7) |
| ≥ 1 million individuals' non-sensitive PI, or ≥ 10,000 individuals' sensitive PI | CAC security assessment (Art. 7); result valid 3 years, extension of 3 years on application within 60 working days before expiry (Art. 9) |
| 100,000 to < 1 million non-sensitive PI, or < 10,000 sensitive PI | Standard contract filed with provincial CAC within 10 working days of taking effect (SCC Measures, Decree 13, effective 1 June 2023, Art. 7) **or** PI protection certification (Certification Measures, effective 1 Jan 2026; certificate valid 3 years, renew 6 months before expiry) |
| < 100,000 non-sensitive PI | Exempt from all three mechanisms (Art. 5(4)) |
| Exempt regardless of volume | Data with no PI or important data generated in trade, transport, academic cooperation, manufacturing and marketing (Art. 3); foreign-sourced PI transited through China without adding domestic PI/important data (Art. 4); transfers necessary for a contract with the individual, cross-border HR management under lawful rules, or emergencies (Art. 5(1)–(3)) |
| Free trade zones | May adopt "negative lists"; data outside the list is exempt from the mechanisms (Art. 6) |
| Always | Notice, separate consent and PIPIA (Art. 10); report actual or potential data security incidents to provincial-or-higher CAC (Art. 11) |

### Incident and breach reporting (CAC National Cybersecurity Incident Reporting Measures, 11 Sept 2025, effective 1 Nov 2025)

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who / what | All network operators in China; incidents graded "relatively major" (较大) or above under the annexed grading guide (aligned with GB/T 20986-2023) must be reported (Arts. 2, 4) |
| Clocks | CII: to the protection work department and public security at once, no later than **1 hour**; central and state organs: to their department's cyber office within **2 hours**; all others: to the provincial CAC within **4 hours**. Major / particularly major incidents are escalated to national CAC within 30 minutes (CII chain) or 1 hour (Art. 4). Sector rules may add reports; suspected crimes go to public security |
| Content | Entity and system; time, type, level, impact and measures (ransomware: ransom amount, method and date); trend; preliminary cause; attribution leads; planned measures and support requested; scene preservation. Partial first report allowed with supplements (Art. 7). Summary report within **30 days** after handling ends (Art. 8) |
| Grading examples | Particularly major: CII wholly down ≥ 6 h or main functions ≥ 24 h; ≥ 100 million citizens' PI leaked; ≥ RMB 100 million direct loss. Major: CII wholly down ≥ 1 h or main functions ≥ 3 h; ≥ 10 million PI; ≥ RMB 20 million. Relatively major: CII wholly down ≥ 10 min or main functions ≥ 30 min; ≥ 1 million PI; ≥ RMB 5 million (Annex) |
| Consequences | Late, omitted, false or concealed reports causing serious harm attract heavier penalties (Art. 10); reasonable protective measures plus timely reporting can mitigate or waive liability (Art. 11). Third-party security/operations providers must be contractually bound to report to the operator (Art. 5) |
| Parallel duties | PIPL Art. 57 (immediate notice, personal-data breaches); DSL Art. 29; Network Data Regs Art. 10 (product/service flaws endangering national security or public interest: report to authorities within 24 hours) and Art. 11 (emergency plan, report per rules); CII Regs Art. 18 (major incidents/threats to protection department and public security) |

## Enforcement and penalties

| Regime | Corporate penalties | Individuals |
|---|---|---|
| PIPL Art. 66 | Order to correct, warning, confiscation, app suspension; refusal to correct: up to RMB 1 million. Grave cases (provincial-or-higher regulator): up to RMB 50 million or 5% of previous year's turnover, business suspension, licence revocation | RMB 10,000–100,000; grave: RMB 100,000–1 million plus bans from director, supervisor, senior manager or PI protection officer roles. Violations enter credit files and are publicised (Art. 67) |
| DSL Arts. 45–46 | Breach of Arts. 27/29/30: RMB 50,000–500,000; refusal or large-scale leak: RMB 500,000–2 million with suspension or licence revocation; core-data violations endangering sovereignty/security: RMB 2–10 million. Unlawful important-data export: RMB 100,000–1 million | RMB 10,000–100,000; RMB 50,000–200,000 (serious); RMB 100,000–1 million (important-data export) |
| CSL Art. 61 (amended) | Network operators breaching Arts. 23/27: warning and optional RMB 10,000–50,000; refusal or harm: RMB 50,000–500,000. CIIOs breaching Arts. 35/36/38/40: RMB 50,000–100,000, rising to RMB 100,000–1 million. Serious consequences (large data leak, partial CII loss of function): RMB 500,000–2 million; particularly serious (CII loses main functions): RMB 2–10 million | RMB 10,000–100,000; RMB 50,000–200,000; RMB 200,000–1 million by tier |
| CSL Arts. 63, 67, 69 (amended) | Selling uncertified critical network equipment: RMB 20,000–100,000 or 1–5x unlawful gains; CIIO using products that failed or skipped security review: 1–10x procurement value; prohibited-content failures: up to RMB 10 million in particularly serious cases; website or app closure and licence revocation available | Up to RMB 1 million by tier |
| CSL Art. 71 (amended) | PI infringements and CIIO localisation breaches are now punished under the PIPL, DSL and other laws rather than a stand-alone CSL fine | — |
| Network Data Regs Arts. 55–57 | General duties: up to RMB 1 million; Art. 13 (national security) breaches: RMB 100,000–1 million, rising to RMB 1–10 million; important-data duties: RMB 50,000–500,000, rising to RMB 500,000–2 million | RMB 10,000–1 million by tier |
| CII Regs Arts. 39–42 | RMB 100,000–1 million for governance, assessment or reporting failures; 1–10x procurement value for skipped security review; RMB 50,000–500,000 for refusing inspections | RMB 10,000–100,000 |

Enforcement is active and public. In July 2026 the Shanghai CAC office fined a major online travel company RMB 10 million under the PIPL for exporting PI without completing the required data-export security assessment — reported as the largest cross-border-transfer fine to date (secondary source). Regulators also use "interviews" (约谈), rectification orders and app takedowns; the 2025 CSL amendment adds an express mitigation article (Art. 73) referencing the Administrative Penalty Law.

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Classify your China footprint**: network operator vs CIIO (await formal notification but plan for it), PI volumes by tier (100k / 1m / 10m), important-data exposure by sector catalogue, and whether PIPL Art. 3(2) captures offshore entities. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Run MLPS grading and assessment** for in-scope systems against GB/T 22239-2019; map MLPS controls to your ISO 27001 / NIST CSF baseline to avoid a parallel control set. See [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md).
3. **Engineer cross-border transfers**: count exports cumulatively from 1 January, pick the mechanism per the threshold table, file standard contracts within 10 working days, calendar the 3-year expiry of assessments and certificates, and document each exemption relied on. See [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md) for the PIPIA.
4. **Embed PIPIAs** (sensitive PI, automated decisions, sharing, export) with 3-year retention; use [../../templates/dpia-template.md](../../templates/dpia-template.md) as the base and add the PIPL Art. 56 elements.
5. **Build the reporting playbook** around the 1 h / 2 h / 4 h clocks and the grading annex, the 30-day summary report, PIPL Art. 57 individual notice, and the 24-hour flaw report under the Network Data Regs; pre-map protection work departments and provincial CAC contacts (12387 hotline). See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md), [../../workflows/incident-regulatory-response.md](../../workflows/incident-regulatory-response.md) and [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).
6. **Governance appointments**: PI protection officer (> 1 million individuals), management-level data security officer and body for important-data processors and > 10 million PI handlers, dedicated CII security body with background checks, local representative for foreign handlers (filed with city-level CAC).
7. **Audit and assessment cadence**: PI compliance audit every 2 years above 10 million (every 5 years self-check below 100,000), annual important-data risk assessment with 20-working-day submission, annual CII testing. Feed results into [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md) evidence sets.
8. **Third parties**: entrustment contracts (PIPL Art. 21), 3-year sharing records, supplier security/confidentiality agreements and procurement security review for CIIOs, contractual incident-reporting duties for security and operations providers. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
9. **Foreign legal process**: DSL Art. 36 blocks direct responses to foreign courts or law enforcement; route through counsel and log as an exception. See [../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md](../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md).
10. **AI systems**: generative-AI training-data security (Network Data Regs Art. 19), CSL Art. 20 policy direction and CAC AI service rules; intake via [../../workflows/ai-system-intake.md](../../workflows/ai-system-intake.md) and [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR:** PIPL mirrors GDPR concepts (legal bases, DPIA/PIPIA, rights, extraterritoriality, turnover fines) but requires *separate* consent for transfers and sensitive data, has no "legitimate interests" basis, and layers state-security review on top. A group serving both markets needs distinct consent flows and two transfer toolkits (SCCs to leave the EU; CAC mechanisms to leave China). See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md).
- **NIS2 / DORA:** CSL MLPS and CII duties play the role NIS2 essential-entity and DORA ICT-risk obligations play in the EU, but China's incident clocks (1–4 hours for relatively major incidents) are tighter than NIS2's 24-hour early warning. See [nis2.md](nis2.md) and [dora.md](dora.md).
- **US regimes:** No direct analogue to the DSL blocking rule; US discovery demands routinely collide with DSL Art. 36. Sector rules ([hipaa.md](hipaa.md), [glba-ftc-safeguards.md](glba-ftc-safeguards.md), [sec-cyber-disclosure.md](sec-cyber-disclosure.md)) may also require disclosure of a China incident on their own clocks.
- **Frameworks:** MLPS baseline controls map reasonably to [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md), [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md) and [../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md](../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md); use the [framework crosswalk](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md) for coverage analysis, but MLPS grading and filing evidence are China-specific and cannot be substituted.
- **Other jurisdictions:** compare localisation and transfer regimes in [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md); China's regime is the strictest on important-data export and foreign legal process.
- **EU AI Act:** CSL Art. 20 and CAC AI service rules are policy and registration oriented; the EU's risk-tier obligations still apply to AI products exported from China. See [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md).
