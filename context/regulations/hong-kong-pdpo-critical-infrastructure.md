# Hong Kong: Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO, Cap. 486) and Protection of Critical Infrastructures (Computer Systems) Ordinance (PCICSO, Cap. 653)

Two separate regimes with two separate regulators. The PDPO is Hong Kong's general, principle-based privacy law (1996; amended 2012 and 2021) enforced by the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data (PCPD). The PCICSO is Hong Kong's first dedicated critical-infrastructure cybersecurity statute (in operation 1 January 2026), a designation-based critical-infrastructure regime run by the Commissioner of Critical Infrastructure (Computer-system Security) under the Security Bureau, with the Monetary Authority and the Communications Authority as sectoral designated authorities. Financial firms additionally carry HKMA and SFC technology-risk expectations, summarised at the end.

## What it is

The **PDPO** is one of Asia's oldest comprehensive data-protection laws, modelled on the 1980 OECD Guidelines following a 1994 Law Reform Commission report. It is technology-neutral and principle-based: compliance turns on six Data Protection Principles (DPPs) in Schedule 1, backed by specific offences (direct marketing, erasure, doxxing) and the PCPD's enforcement-notice mechanism. Its distinctive gaps relative to GDPR-era statutes — no mandatory breach notification, no direct obligations on processors, no administrative fines, and a cross-border transfer section (s.33) that has never been brought into operation — are exactly the items the Government and PCPD have been studying for amendment (see Timeline).

The **PCICSO** is a critical-infrastructure cybersecurity law rather than a privacy law: the Code of Practice states it targets the security of designated critical computer systems and "does not target personal data nor trade secrets". It draws on overseas CI legislation and international standards, applies only to designated operators in eight sectors (plus other infrastructure whose loss would substantially affect critical societal or economic activities), and layers a Code of Practice from OCCICS and sectoral codes from the Monetary Authority and Communications Authority over statutory obligations. Government departments are excluded; they follow the internal Government IT Security Policy and Guidelines.

## Who it covers / Scope

**PDPO.** *Personal data* is information relating to a living individual from which the individual can be identified, in a form in which access or processing is practicable. A *data user* controls the collection, holding, processing or use of personal data, alone or jointly. A *data processor* processes on behalf of a data user and is not directly regulated: DPP2 and DPP4(2) require the data user to use "contractual or other means" to bind processors on retention and security. The Ordinance applies to public and private sectors alike. Part 8 exemptions (e.g. s.52 domestic purposes, s.57 security/defence, s.58 crime, s.61 news, s.62 statistics and research, s.63B due diligence in business transactions) are defences to be established case by case, not standing carve-outs. Section 33 (transfer restriction) is not in operation; the PCPD's 2022 Recommended Model Contractual Clauses are best practice, not law.

**PCICSO.** Two categories of *critical infrastructure* (s.2): (a) infrastructure essential to the continuous provision in Hong Kong of an essential service in a Schedule 1 sector; (b) any other infrastructure whose damage, loss of functionality or data leakage may substantially affect critical societal or economic activities (OCCICS examples: major sports and performance venues, R&D parks).

| Schedule 1 sector | Regulating authority for organisational and preventive obligations |
|---|---|
| Energy | Commissioner |
| Information technology | Commissioner |
| Banking and financial services | Monetary Authority for authorized institutions, SVF licensees under Cap. 584, and settlement institutions / system operators of designated systems; Commissioner otherwise |
| Air transport; Land transport; Maritime transport | Commissioner |
| Healthcare services | Commissioner |
| Telecommunications and broadcasting services | Communications Authority for its licensees (e.g. unified carrier licence holders, domestic free TV licensees); Commissioner otherwise |

The Commissioner regulates incident reporting and response (Category 3) for every sector. Designation of an operator (s.12) and of its CCSs (s.13) is by written notice, revocable at any time; designation factors include dependence of the CI's core function on computer systems, sensitivity of digital data controlled, and the operator's degree of control over the infrastructure. A CCS may be a system outside the operator's control if it is accessible in or from Hong Kong and essential to the core function (s.22(2)(c)); whether a system is isolated from the Internet is irrelevant (CoP 3.1). Suppliers are not regulated directly — "CI operators can outsource their work, but not their responsibilities" (OCCICS FAQ 21), and the CoP supplies sample contract clauses (Annex H).

## Core obligations

### PDPO — the six Data Protection Principles and key provisions

| Provision | Requirement |
|---|---|
| DPP1 Collection | Lawful purpose directly related to the data user's function; adequate but not excessive; fair means; Personal Information Collection Statement (purpose, transferees, access/correction rights) when collecting from the data subject |
| DPP2 Accuracy and retention | All practicable steps to keep data accurate and not longer than necessary; processor retention bound contractually. s.26 erasure duty is a separate offence (HK$10,000) |
| DPP3 Use | No new purpose without express, voluntary consent (withdrawable in writing). Part 6A direct marketing needs informed consent plus opt-out; silence is not consent |
| DPP4 Security | All practicable steps against unauthorised or accidental access, processing, erasure, loss or use, having regard to data sensitivity, harm, storage, security measures and staff integrity and competence; DPP4(2) obliges contractual or other means over processors |
| DPP5 Openness | Publish personal data policies and practices, kinds of data held and main purposes |
| DPP6 Access and correction | Data access and correction requests under Part 5; reasons for refusal; log book of refusals |
| s.64 doxxing (2021) | Tier 1 (s.64(3A)): disclosure without consent with intent/recklessness as to specified harm — HK$100,000 + 2 years. Tier 2 (s.64(3C)): disclosure causing specified harm — HK$1,000,000 + 5 years. PCPD cessation notices with extraterritorial reach (appeal to Administrative Appeals Board within 14 days; notice remains operative pending appeal); PCPD criminal investigation and prosecution powers |

**Breach handling.** No statutory notification duty. The PCPD's *Guidance on Data Breach Handling and Data Breach Notifications* (June 2023) recommends a five-step response (gather essential information, contain the breach, assess the risk of harm, consider giving notification, document the breach), notification to affected data subjects and to the PCPD "as soon as practicable after becoming aware of the incident, regardless of the progress of any internal investigation", and use of the PCPD's Data Breach Notification Form, submitted online, by fax, in person or by post (oral notifications are not accepted). Contravention of DPP4 found in a breach investigation leads to advisory/warning letters or an enforcement notice. Notifications and compliance-check cases rose to 217 in 2024 from roughly 100 a year previously (Government reply to LegCo, 22 January 2025).

### PCICSO — statutory obligations of a designated CI operator

| Section | Obligation | Clock | Maximum fine (summary / indictment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| s.19 | Maintain an office in Hong Kong (not a P.O. box or company-secretary address) and notify its address | Within 1 month of designation; changes within 1 month | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + HK$30,000 / HK$50,000 per day |
| s.20 | Notify operator change (change of the organisation operating the CI) | As soon as practicable, within 1 month | HK$3,000,000 / HK$5,000,000 + HK$60,000 / HK$100,000 per day |
| s.21 | Set up and maintain a computer-system security management unit (may be outsourced or overseas) supervised by an appointed employee with adequate professional knowledge (CoP cites CISP, CISA, CISM, CISSP as examples); notify the appointment and any change | Unit and appointment notice within 1 month of designation (extendable where none existed); changes within 1 month | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + HK$30,000 / HK$50,000 per day (failure to notify) |
| s.22 | Notify material changes to a CCS (design, configuration, security, operation), removal of a CCS, or addition of an essential system | Within 1 month of the event | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + daily fines |
| s.23 | Submit and implement a computer-system security management plan covering Schedule 3 matters (OCCICS: 20+ areas including risk, asset, access and account management, change management, remote access, cloud, supply chain) | Within 3 months of designation; revisions within 1 month | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + daily fines |
| s.24 | Computer-system security risk assessment, including vulnerability assessment and penetration test (CoP 6.3), by reference to recognised methodologies (CoP lists ISO/IEC 27001/27005, IEC 62443-3-2, NIST SP 800-30, GB/T standards) | First within 12 months of designation, then at least every 12 months; report within 3 months of period end | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + daily fines |
| s.25 | Computer-system security audit by an independent auditor who did not build or operate the system (CoP 6.4) | First within 24 months of designation, then at least every 24 months; report within 3 months | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + daily fines |
| s.26 | Participate in Commissioner-run security drills (tabletop, functional or simulated attack; CoP: no more than once every two years per operator) | On reasonable written notice | HK$3,000,000 / HK$5,000,000 |
| s.27 | Submit to the Commissioner and implement an emergency response plan covering Part 2 of Schedule 3; CoP requires Board or senior-management endorsement and review on material change and at least every two years | Within 3 months of designation; revisions within 1 month | HK$300,000 / HK$500,000 + HK$30,000 / HK$50,000 per day |
| s.28 | Notify computer-system security incidents affecting a CCS to the Commissioner (Schedule 6 times below) | 12 h / 48 h / 14 days | HK$3,000,000 / HK$5,000,000 |

**Schedule 6 incident clocks.** (1) Initial notification: within 12 hours of becoming aware if the incident has disrupted, is disrupting or is likely to disrupt the CI's core function; within 48 hours in any other case. (2) If the initial notification was not in the specified form, a written record in the specified form within 48 hours of that notification. (3) A written report within 14 days of becoming aware (CoP Annex F template). OCCICS FAQ 16–18: reportable incidents involve unauthorised access or an actual adverse effect on availability, integrity, confidentiality or protective capability of the CCS (ransomware, DDoS outage, malicious tampering, insider data theft); incidents caused by human-error misconfiguration, natural disaster or accident are out of scope; "becoming aware" means the security unit has reasonably confirmed an incident, not the first anomaly alert.

**Code of Practice layer.** The generic CoP (v1.0, 1 January 2026, 51 pages) is not subsidiary legislation and non-compliance is not itself an offence, but it is admissible in proceedings (s.9) and regulating authorities may issue written directions by reference to it. Notable content: designation criteria for CCSs (ch. 3); plan content and controls including least-privilege administration, cryptography, annual access-rights review, supply-chain management with diversification of sources against geopolitical and export-control risk (6.2.25); operational-technology controls (6.5); drill and emergency-response requirements (ch. 7). Sectoral codes: Energy (28 January 2026), Authorized Institutions (2 June 2026), Payment System Infrastructure operators (10 June 2026), SVF licensees (12 June 2026), Land Transport (22 June 2026).

**Commissioner's powers.** Information requests during designation (ss.14–17), early-intervention inquiries and magistrate's warrants (ss.29–32), computer-system security investigations including warrants against third-party organisations and power of entry in emergencies (ss.33–40). Secrecy provisions bind officials; operators may disclose their own designation.

## Enforcement and penalties

| Regime | Mechanism | Sanction |
|---|---|---|
| PDPO | Complaint or own-motion investigation; published investigation reports; enforcement notice directing remedial steps | Contravening an enforcement notice: HK$50,000 + 2 years + HK$1,000/day; second or subsequent conviction HK$100,000 + 2 years + HK$2,000/day (s.50A). Repeating the same act after compliance: HK$50,000 + 2 years (s.50A(3)) |
| PDPO | Obstructing the Commissioner or false statements (s.50B) | HK$10,000 + 6 months |
| PDPO | Direct marketing offences (ss.35C–35L) | HK$500,000 + 3 years; HK$1,000,000 + 5 years where data provided for gain |
| PDPO | Doxxing (s.64(3A)/(3C)); breach of cessation notice | HK$100,000 + 2 years / HK$1,000,000 + 5 years; cessation-notice breach HK$50,000 + 2 years + HK$1,000/day (HK$100,000 + HK$2,000/day on repeat). PCPD issued 2,104 cessation notices to 57 platforms (33,743 messages) from 8 October 2021 to 31 December 2025 |
| PDPO | Civil claim | Data subjects may seek compensation by civil action for damage caused by a contravention; the Privacy Commissioner may provide legal assistance to aggrieved data subjects |
| PCICSO | Written directions by a regulating authority on defective compliance; prosecution of offences | Non-compliance with a direction: up to HK$5,000,000 + HK$100,000/day (OCCICS FAQ 20). Statutory offences carry the fines in the table above. Fines apply at the organisational level only and are restricted to fines (FAQ 24); a due-diligence defence is available (FAQ 22). Regulations made by the Secretary for Security may prescribe offences up to HK$3,000,000 (summary) / HK$5,000,000 (indictment) plus daily fines |

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Determine designation exposure.** Map services to the eight sectors and the Category 2 definition; identify which regulating authority would designate you (Commissioner, MA or CA); prepare the designation information set OCCICS lists (org chart, system functions, network diagrams, data volumes, hardware/software makes, third-party services, backup plans). See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Build the CI operator compliance calendar** from the designation date: 1-month office notice, 3-month security management plan, 12-month risk assessment cycle, 24-month independent audit cycle, 1-month notices for material CCS changes and operator changes. Track each as a control with evidence in [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
3. **Appoint and evidence the security unit head** — an employee with recognised qualifications and experience commensurate with CCS risk; notify appointment on CoP Annex C. Document any outsourced or overseas unit arrangement.
4. **Wire the 12 h / 48 h / 14-day clocks into incident response** alongside PCPD (as soon as practicable), HKMA (prompt notification of significant incidents under TM-E-1 8.2.5), SFC (Code of Conduct para 12.5(e)) and Police reporting; pre-fill CoP Annex E and Annex F templates; define "becoming aware" in the playbook. See [../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md).

7. **Flow obligations to suppliers**: contract clauses for information provision, incident cooperation and audit support (CoP Annex H); supply-chain component inventory and diversification analysis; remember overseas-provider refusal is not a defence unless due diligence is shown. ## Interplay

- **PDPO and PCICSO on the same incident.** A cyberattack on a CCS that exposes personal data triggers the statutory PCICSO clocks to OCCICS and the recommended PCPD notification; OCCICS FAQ 19 confirms the PCICSO notification does not discharge other legal or regulatory reporting. Keep one incident record feeding both.
- **HKMA-regulated institutions.** The Monetary Authority is both the PCICSO designated authority for AIs, SVF licensees and designated payment systems (with its own sectoral codes for authorized institutions, payment system infrastructure operators and SVF licensees, effective June 2026) and the issuer of technology-risk guidance: SPM TM-G-1 *General Principles for Technology Risk Management* (V.1, 24 June 2003, a non-statutory guidance note), SPM TM-E-1 *Risk Management of E-banking* (V.4, 25 October 2024, a statutory guideline under Banking Ordinance s.7(3); section 3.3 independent assessment and penetration tests; 8.2.5 prompt notification of significant incidents), and the Cybersecurity Fortification Initiative (launched 2016; CFI 2.0 announced 3 November 2020, effective 1 January 2021, phased) with three pillars — the Cyber Resilience Assessment Framework (C-RAF, which includes intelligence-led Cyber Attack Simulation Testing, iCAST), the Professional Development Programme and the Cyber Intelligence Sharing Platform.
- **SFC-licensed firms.** *Guidelines for Reducing and Mitigating Hacking Risks Associated with Internet Trading* (issued under SFO s.399, published 27 October 2017) set baseline controls — two-factor authentication for client login, infrastructure security, cybersecurity management — for Type 1, 2, 3 and (fund-distributing) Type 9 firms engaged in internet trading. SFC circular SFO/IS/020/2026 (2 June 2026) on AI-enabled cyberattacks expects MIC-IT accountability, current asset inventories enabling same-day prioritisation, expedited patching, regular backups (at least daily for electronic-trading LCs and VATPs) and prompt notification of material cybersecurity incidents under Code of Conduct para 12.5(e) and VATP Guidelines paras 16.7(b)–(c); it cites HKCERT incident counts of 15,877 in 2025 versus 12,536 in 2024.
- **Comparable regimes.** PCICSO's designation model, sector schedules and tiered clocks resemble the EU's [nis2.md](nis2.md) essential-entity regime (24 h early warning / 72 h notification / 1 month final report) and Australia's SOCI Act; financial-sector operators will recognise the DORA pattern of sectoral lex specialis in the MA's role — see [dora.md](dora.md). For the PDPO, the missing GDPR features (72-hour Art. 33 notification, Art. 28 processor duties, Art. 83 fines, Chapter V transfers) are the amendment agenda — compare [gdpr.md](gdpr.md). Other APAC privacy regimes are summarised in [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md).
- **Frameworks.** The CoP explicitly points to ISO/IEC 27001 and 27005, IEC 62443, NIST SP 800-30 and ISO/IEC 42001 as acceptable methodologies; an ISO 27001 ISMS scoped to the CCSs is the natural evidence base for the s.23 plan, s.24 assessment and s.25 audit — see [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md) and [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md).
