# APRA Prudential Standards CPS 234 (Information Security) and CPS 230 (Operational Risk Management)

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | **CPS 234 Information Security** — Banking, Insurance, Life Insurance, Health Insurance and Superannuation (prudential standard) determination No. 1 of 2018, F2018L01745 (registered 12 December 2018). **CPS 230 Operational Risk Management** — determination No. 1 of 2026, F2026L00475 (registered 29 April 2026), replacing the July 2023 determination. Both are legislative instruments made under Banking Act 1959 s 11AF, Insurance Act 1973 s 32, Life Insurance Act 1995 s 230A, Private Health Insurance (Prudential Supervision) Act 2015 s 92 and Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993 s 34C |
| Regulator | Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA). Both standards sit in the Risk Management pillar of APRA's prudential framework as "supporting" standards under CPS 220 / SPS 220 Risk Management |
| Status and key dates | CPS 234 in force since **1 July 2019** (third-party-managed assets: earlier of next contract renewal or 1 July 2020). CPS 230 commenced **1 July 2025**; pre-existing service-provider contracts had until the earlier of next renewal or **1 July 2026**; the amended CPS 230 and CPG 230 (non-traditional service provider exemptions, paragraph renumbering) commenced **1 July 2026** |
| Who is covered | All APRA-regulated entities: ADIs (including foreign ADIs and authorised banking NOHCs), general insurers (including Category C insurers, insurance NOHCs, Level 2 insurance group parents), life companies (including friendly societies, EFLICs, registered NOHCs), private health insurers, and RSE licensees (superannuation trustees). Group-wide application where the entity is Head of a group |
| Structure | CPS 234: 36 paragraphs — roles, capability, policy framework, asset classification, controls, incident management, control testing, internal audit, APRA notification. CPS 230: 61 paragraphs plus an Attachment — risk-management framework, roles, operational risk management, business continuity (critical operations and tolerance levels), service-provider management (material service providers, formal agreements, exemptions, notifications) |
| Guidance layer | Prudential Practice Guides **CPG 234** (June 2019, with Attachments A–H) and **CPG 230** (June 2024, updated July 2026). PPGs state APRA's view of sound practice; they do not themselves create enforceable requirements |
| APRA clocks | CPS 234: **72 hours** (material information security incident), **10 business days** (material control weakness not remediable in a timely manner). CPS 230: **72 hours** (material operational risk incident), **24 hours** (disruption to a critical operation outside tolerance), **20 business days** (new/materially changed agreement supporting a critical operation), **prior notice** (material offshoring). Annual submission of the material service provider (MSP) register |
| Enforcement | Supervisory (prudential and thematic reviews, tripartite assessments), plus formal powers: directions, licence conditions, additional capital, mandated independent reviews/remediation programs, enforceable undertakings, FAR disqualification. No monetary penalty is written into the standards; failure to report a significant breach of a prudential requirement is an offence under the industry Acts (200 penalty units for ADIs, life and general insurers; 50 for RSE licensees; 30 for private health insurers) |
| Certifiable? | No. Compliance is self-assessed, examined by APRA, and periodically subject to APRA-required independent (tripartite) assessments |
| Relationship to neighbours | CPS 230 para 24 requires entities to meet CPS 234; a CPS 234 incident notification need not be repeated under CPS 230; CPS 230 replaced CPS 231 Outsourcing, CPS 232 Business Continuity Management, SPS 231, SPS 232 and HPS 231. Privacy Act NDB, SOCI Act and FAR obligations run in parallel |

## What it is

CPS 234 is APRA's cross-industry information security standard. It is short and principles-based: the Board is "ultimately responsible" for the entity's information security, and the entity must maintain a capability "commensurate with the size and extent of threats to its information assets", classify assets, implement and test controls, and notify APRA of material incidents and control weaknesses. It binds third-party and related-party managed assets directly: paras 16, 22, 28 and 34 apply to *all* such assets, not only those under outsourcing agreements for "material business activities". CPG 234 supplies the control-level detail (identity and access, software security, cryptography, customer security, testing techniques, Board reporting).

CPS 230 is the operational-resilience layer that arrived six years later. Consulted from July 2022, finalised 17 July 2023 and commenced 1 July 2025, it consolidated five outsourcing and business-continuity standards into one, introduced **critical operations** with Board-approved **tolerance levels**, and replaced the "outsourcing" concept with a broader **material service provider** regime including an annual register submission to APRA. A targeted amendment (consulted December 2025, finalised 30 April 2026, in force 1 July 2026) added exemptions from specific contractual requirements for arrangements with non-traditional service providers such as central banks, exchanges and payment schemes, dropped the spent contract-transition paragraph (so every paragraph from 7 onward is renumbered one lower — e.g. incident notification moved from para 33 to 32, service-provider notifications from 59 to 60 after the two new exemption paragraphs), and now takes its definitions from a new Prudential Standard CPS 001 Defined terms. Cite the July 2026 numbering, used throughout this pack.

Together the two standards, CPS 220 (risk management), CPS 190 (recovery and exit planning) and the Financial Accountability Regime form APRA's resilience stack. APRA's 2026-27 Corporate Plan (19 August 2026) flags continued CPS 230 thematic reviews, formalised data collection on material service providers and common technology platforms, AI-enabled cyber threats, and post-quantum cryptography planning as supervisory priorities.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Test | CPS 234 | CPS 230 |
|---|---|---|
| Entity classes | Para 2: ADIs, foreign ADIs, authorised banking NOHCs; general insurers, Category C insurers, authorised insurance NOHCs, Level 2 insurance group parents; life companies, friendly societies, EFLICs, registered life NOHCs; private health insurers; RSE licensees "in respect of their business operations" | Para 2: same five classes |
| Foreign branches | Para 3: foreign ADIs, Category C insurers and EFLICs — Australian branch operations only; "Board" read as the senior officer outside Australia | Para 3: same |
| Groups | Para 4: a Head of a group must apply the standard throughout the group, "including in relation to entities that are not APRA-regulated", and on a group basis (paras 13–36 read as applying to the group) | Para 4: same; for RSE licensees the group includes connected entities and related bodies corporate |
| Third parties | Paras 16, 22, 28, 34–35 extend assessment, control-design evaluation, testing assessment, internal audit and notification duties to information assets "managed by related parties and third parties" | Applies to all service providers; heightened duties for *material* service providers and *material* arrangements (para 48); fourth parties (para 47(c)) |
| Adjustment | Para 11: APRA may adjust or exclude a specific requirement for an entity (industry Act subsections cited in the standard) | Para 10: same; paras 57–58: NTSP exemption from paras 53, 54, 55(d), 59(a), 59(c) where the provider is in an Attachment category **and** the arrangement uses standardised terms or has no formal agreement; APRA may exempt other arrangements by written notice |
| Proportionality | "Commensurate with" threats, criticality/sensitivity, lifecycle stage and consequences (paras 15, 18, 21, 27) | "Appropriate to its size, business mix and complexity"; CPG 230 expects significant financial institutions (SFIs) to show stronger practices |

Non-APRA financial firms (AFS licensees that are not ADIs/insurers/trustees, payment providers not yet licensed by APRA) are outside both standards unless they are inside a regulated group — check applicability with [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).

## Core obligations

### CPS 234 (July 2019 text)

| Para | Area | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 13–14 | Roles | Board ultimately responsible for information security; entity must clearly define security roles of the Board, senior management, governing bodies (committees, working groups, forums) and individuals |
| 15–17 | Capability | Maintain an information security capability (resources, skills, controls) commensurate with threats; assess the capability of related/third parties managing information assets; actively maintain capability as vulnerabilities, threats, assets and business environment change |
| 18–19 | Policy framework | Maintain a policy framework (policies, standards, guidelines, procedures) commensurate with exposures; it must direct responsibilities of all parties including staff, contractors, related parties, third parties and customers |
| 20 | Asset classification | Classify information assets (including those managed by related/third parties) by **criticality** (impact of loss of availability) and **sensitivity** (impact of loss of confidentiality or integrity), reflecting impact on the entity and on depositors, policyholders, beneficiaries or customers |
| 21–22 | Controls | Implement controls in a timely manner commensurate with vulnerabilities and threats, criticality and sensitivity, lifecycle stage (planning to disposal) and consequences; evaluate the *design* of third-party controls protecting the entity's assets |
| 23–26 | Incident management | Robust detection and response mechanisms; documented response plans for plausible incidents covering all stages from detection to post-incident review and escalation/reporting to the Board; plans reviewed **and tested annually** |
| 27–31 | Control testing | Systematic testing program with nature and frequency driven by rate of change in threats, criticality/sensitivity, consequences, exposure to "untrusted" environments and materiality of asset change; assess adequacy of third-party testing where relied on; escalate to Board/senior management deficiencies that cannot be remediated in a timely manner; testers must be **appropriately skilled and functionally independent**; review the program at least annually or on material change |
| 32–34 | Internal audit | Internal audit must review design and operating effectiveness of controls including those of related/third parties; assurance personnel must be appropriately skilled; internal audit must assess third-party assurance where an incident could materially affect the entity or its customers and audit intends to rely on it |
| 35 | Incident notification | Notify APRA as soon as possible and no later than **72 hours** after becoming aware of an incident that materially affected or had the potential to materially affect (financially or non-financially) the entity or its depositors/policyholders/beneficiaries/customers, **or** that has been notified to another regulator in Australia or elsewhere |
| 36 | Control-weakness notification | Notify APRA as soon as possible and no later than **10 business days** after becoming aware of a material control weakness the entity expects it cannot remediate in a timely manner |

CPG 234 expectations worth quoting in assessments: a sufficient set of controls tested **at least annually**, with controls facing untrusted environments (internet, service-provider and customer connections) tested throughout the year; testers without operational responsibility for the controls tested; notification even with incomplete information; material weaknesses identified through testing, assurance, incidents and vendor vulnerability notices; Attachment H lists Board-level reporting content. Notifications are made electronically on APRA's forms (incident; material control weakness). Where an incident is notifiable under both CPS 230 and CPS 234, one notification suffices and the **earliest applicable timeframe applies**.

### CPS 230 (July 2026 text)

| Para | Area | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 11–14 | Key principles | Manage operational risk; maintain critical operations within tolerance through severe disruptions; do not rely on a service provider unless prudential obligations can still be met in full |
| 15–17 | Framework | Within the CPS 220/SPS 220 framework: governance, operational risk profile with appetite, indicators, limits and tolerance levels, effective controls, monitoring/escalation, BCPs tested with severe-but-plausible scenarios, service-provider processes; reviewed with the CPS 220 framework review; BCP must not undermine CPS 190 recovery and exit planning |
| 18 | APRA powers | Where APRA finds material weaknesses: independent review, remediation program, additional capital (ORFR target for RSE licensees), licence conditions, other supervisory action |
| 19–22 | Board | Board ultimately accountable; must set senior-manager roles, receive regular operational-risk-profile updates, **approve the BCP and tolerance levels**, review testing results, **approve the service provider management policy** and review MSP risk/performance reporting; senior management must brief the Board on critical-operation impacts of decisions |
| 23–24 | Operational risk | Full range of operational risks (legal, regulatory, compliance, conduct, technology, data, change); maintain sound IT capability, monitor age and health of information assets and **meet CPS 234** |
| 25–27 | Profile | Assess impact of business/strategic decisions, new products, geographies and technologies; document processes and resources (people, technology, information, facilities, service providers) for each critical operation with interdependencies; scenario analysis; risk assessment before providing a material service to another party |
| 28–30 | Controls | Design, implement and embed controls; regularly test design and operating effectiveness at a frequency commensurate with risk materiality; report results to senior management; remediate root causes and carry unremediated gaps in the risk profile |
| 31–32 | Incidents | Identify, escalate, record and address incidents and near misses; notify APRA within **72 hours** of an operational risk incident likely to have a material financial impact or material impact on critical operations (CPS 234 notifications need not be repeated) |
| 33–38 | Critical operations | Register of critical operations; minimum list unless justified otherwise — ADIs: payments, deposit-taking and management, custody, settlements and clearing; insurers: claims processing; RSE licensees: investment management and fund administration; all: customer enquiries and supporting systems/infrastructure; APRA may add operations. Tolerance levels per critical operation for **maximum disruption time**, **maximum data loss** and **minimum service levels** (CPG 230 maps these to maximum allowable outage/RTO, RPO and recovery level objective); APRA may require changes or set tolerances |
| 39–45 | BCP | BCP content (register and tolerances, triggers, actions, execution-risk and dependency assessment, communications strategy); maintain execution capability; report tolerance failures with a remediation plan to the Board; notify APRA within **24 hours** of a disruption outside tolerance; systematic testing program covering all critical operations with an **annual business continuity exercise** including MSP-disruption scenarios (APRA may prescribe scenarios); annual BCP update; periodic internal audit assurance to the Board |
| 46–51 | Material service providers | Comprehensive service provider management policy covering entry, monitoring, substitution and exit, MSP risks and fourth-party risks; **register of MSPs** — those relied on for a critical operation or exposing the entity to material operational risk; minimum MSP categories unless justified otherwise — ADIs: credit assessment, funding and liquidity management, mortgage brokerage; insurers: underwriting, claims management, insurance brokerage, reinsurance; RSE licensees: fund administration, custody, investment management, promoters and financial planners; all: risk management, core technology services, internal audit; **register submitted to APRA annually**; APRA may designate providers as material |
| 52–56 | Agreements | Due diligence, selection and ongoing-capability assessment plus financial/non-financial risk assessment (including geographic location and concentration) before entering or materially modifying a material arrangement; formal legally binding agreement covering services and service levels, rights/responsibilities (asset ownership, data ownership and control, dispute resolution, audit access, liability and indemnity), legal/compliance enablement, sub-contractor notification and liability, force majeure continuity, termination (including RSE best-financial-interests termination); APRA access to documentation and data, on-site visit rights and non-impediment; manage provider-continuity, step-in and contagion risk, BCP executability and orderly exit |
| 57–58 | Exemptions | NTSP exemption (see Scope) from paras 53, 54, 55(d), 59(a) and 59(c) only; all other requirements continue; Attachment categories: government agencies, regulators, central banks, financial market exchanges, clearing and settlement facility operators (including SuperStream clearing houses), payment system/scheme operators, financial messaging infrastructures |
| 59–61 | Monitoring and notification | Senior-management reporting on performance, control effectiveness and mutual contract compliance; notify APRA within **20 business days** of entering into or materially changing an agreement supporting a critical operation, and **before** any material offshoring arrangement or significant change (data or personnel located offshore); internal audit review of any proposed outsourcing of a critical operation and regular reporting to the Board or Audit Committee |

APRA's MSP register template (first published 17 October 2024; first submissions due 1 October 2025; template updated 30 April 2026 to classify exempt arrangements) is the preferred submission format; APRA Connect returns are issued for each annual cycle. CPS 230 notification e-forms (operational risk incident, tolerance breach, new/changed material arrangement or offshoring) have been available since 27 June 2025. Breach reporting (as distinct from prudential-standard notifications) moved to APRA Connect on 21 May 2026.

## Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervision first.** APRA's stated approach is proactive supervision — prudential and thematic reviews, heightened engagement, required self-assessments — escalating to formal powers where cooperation or outcomes fall short. Its Enforcement Approach (16 April 2019, updated 3 September 2019) commits APRA to "act quickly and forcefully" and to set public examples where prudential obligations are not met.
- **Formal tools.** Directions, licence conditions, additional capital, enforceable undertakings (public register), infringement notices (SIS Act and FSCODA), and disqualification of accountable persons under the Financial Accountability Regime (banking since 15 March 2024; insurance and superannuation since 15 March 2025). CPS 230 para 18 lists the standard-specific responses: independent review, remediation program, additional capital, licence conditions.
- **Breach reporting.** A significant breach of a prudential standard must be reported under the industry Acts (Banking Act s 62A, Insurance Act s 38AA, Life Insurance Act s 132A, SIS Act s 29JA, PHIPS Act s 95): ADIs, life and general insurers within 10 business days of awareness (immediately if it concerns financial position), RSE licensees within 30 days, private health insurers as soon as practicable. Failure to report is an offence (200 / 50 / 30 penalty units respectively). CPS 234/230 incident and weakness notifications are not breach reports; the June 2025 letter to RSE licensees shows APRA expects a material-control-weakness notification to be followed by a breach assessment.
- **Independent tripartite assessments.** Under its 2020–2024 Cyber Security Strategy APRA required more than 300 banks, insurers and trustees (a mid-2021 pilot, then four tranches planned for completion by end-2023) to appoint an independent auditor to assess CPS 234 compliance. First-tranche findings (5 July 2023; ~24% of entities) named six recurring gaps: incomplete identification and classification of critical/sensitive assets; limited assessment of third-party security capability; inadequate control-testing programs (coverage, independence, evidence retention); untested incident response plans; limited internal audit review of security controls; inconsistent and late notification of material incidents and weaknesses. Gaps trigger breach reporting and intensified supervision.
- **Targeted directions to industry.** On 10 June 2025 APRA required every RSE licensee board to self-assess authentication controls, implement MFA or equivalent for high-risk member activities and all privileged/administrative access, lodge a material control weakness notification (or justify non-materiality), run a breach assessment, and name the FAR accountable person(s) for CPS 234 — all by 31 August 2025.

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Fix the perimeter**: confirm entity class, group scope (non-APRA subsidiaries included), foreign-branch limits and any APRA adjustments — [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Classify every information asset by criticality and sensitivity**, including third-party-managed assets, and reconcile the asset register with the CPS 230 critical-operations register and MSP register — the top tripartite finding was incomplete classification.
3. **Run a documented, independent control-testing program** with annual coverage of a sufficient control set, continuous testing of internet-facing controls, defined success criteria and retained evidence; escalate unremediable deficiencies — [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md), [../../templates/control-test-workpaper.md](../../templates/control-test-workpaper.md).
4. **Wire the five APRA clocks into incident response** (72h/10 business days under CPS 234; 72h/24h/20 business days and prior offshoring notice under CPS 230), define materiality criteria in policy, and log every decision — [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md), [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md), [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).
5. **Uplift MSP contracts** to CPS 230 paras 53–54 (audit and APRA access, sub-contracting notification, force majeure continuity, termination, data ownership) and CPS 234 (incident and weakness reporting to the entity), apply the NTSP exemption narrowly and record it in the register — [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md), [../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md](../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md).
6. **Set and test tolerance levels** (maximum outage, data loss, minimum service level) per critical operation, run the annual severe-but-plausible exercise including MSP loss scenarios, and report tolerance breaches with remediation plans to the Board.
7. **Evidence Board accountability**: approvals of the BCP, tolerance levels and service-provider policy; regular operational-risk-profile and security reporting (CPG 234 Attachment H); FAR accountable-person mapping for CPS 234/230 — [../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md), [../../templates/grc-board-report.md](../../templates/grc-board-report.md).
8. **Resource internal audit** to review security controls (including third-party controls) and BCP credibility with appropriately skilled staff — [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).
9. **Treat control gaps as formal exceptions** with owners, dates and Board visibility; unremediated gaps must remain in the operational risk profile — [../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md](../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **Privacy Act 1988 / NDB scheme:** a cyber incident involving personal information likely to cause serious harm triggers OAIC and individual notification on a separate clock; a CPS 234 notification is also required where the incident "has been notified to other regulators", so an NDB notification makes the 72-hour APRA clock mandatory. See [australia-privacy-act.md](australia-privacy-act.md).
- **SOCI Act:** APRA-regulated entities that hold critical infrastructure assets carry separate ACSC incident-reporting and risk-management-program duties; see the Australia section of [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md).
- **Financial Accountability Regime:** accountable persons must be mapped to CPS 234/230 responsibilities; APRA's 2025 RSE letter required this explicitly.
- **DORA and UK operational resilience:** CPS 230's critical operations, tolerance levels, annual scenario testing and MSP register mirror DORA's critical or important functions and register of information and the UK important business services / impact tolerances model, but with no threat-led penetration testing mandate and with APRA-specific notification clocks for new critical-operation contracts and offshoring. Groups spanning jurisdictions should maintain one dependency map serving all three — see [dora.md](dora.md) and [uk-financial-operational-resilience.md](uk-financial-operational-resilience.md).
- **Frameworks:** CPS 234's capability/classification/testing/assurance structure maps cleanly onto ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A organisational controls (asset management and classification, supplier relationships, 5.23 cloud services, 5.30 ICT readiness for business continuity) and NIST CSF 2.0 Govern/Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond/Recover; CPG 234 attachments resemble CIS Controls safeguards. Use [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md), [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md), [../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md](../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md) and [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md); SOC 2 reports from MSPs support but do not replace the entity's own para 16/22/28 assessments ([../frameworks/soc2-tsc.md](../frameworks/soc2-tsc.md)).
