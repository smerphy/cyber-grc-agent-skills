## What it is

The SOCI Act began in 2018 as an ownership-transparency register for a handful of sectors. The *Security Legislation Amendment (Critical Infrastructure) Act 2021* (commenced 3 December 2021) expanded it to 11 sectors, added mandatory cyber incident reporting and the government-assistance ("step-in") powers; the *Security Legislation Amendment (Critical Infrastructure Protection) Act 2022* (commenced 2 April 2022) added the risk management program and SoNS enhanced obligations. The *Security of Critical Infrastructure and Other Legislation Amendment (Enhanced Response and Prevention) Act 2024* (ERP Act, assented 29 November 2024; Schedules 1–4 and 6 commenced 20 December 2024, Schedule 5 on 4 April 2025) clarified that data storage systems holding business critical data form part of the asset, extended Part 3A to all-hazards consequence management, created a "protected information" definition, added the power to direct an entity to fix a seriously deficient CIRMP, and moved telecommunications security into new Part 2D.

The *Cyber Security Act 2024* is Australia's first standalone cyber statute, delivering four measures of the 2023–2030 Australian Cyber Security Strategy: mandatory security standards for consumer smart devices, mandatory ransomware payment reporting, a "limited use" regime for information shared with the National Cyber Security Coordinator, and a no-fault Cyber Incident Review Board (CIRB). The Act applies extraterritorially (s 5) and binds the Crown (s 6). Both regimes are administered by Home Affairs and are designed to interlock: the SOCI definition of *cyber security incident* (s 12M — unauthorised access, modification or impairment of computer data, programs, communications or availability) is imported into the Cyber Security Act (s 9).

## Who it covers / Scope

**SOCI sectors (s 8D):** communications; data storage or processing; financial services and markets; water and sewerage; energy; health care and medical; higher education and research; food and grocery; transport; space technology; defence industry.

**Asset classes (s 9(1)):** critical telecommunications, broadcasting, domain name system, data storage or processing, banking, superannuation, insurance, financial market infrastructure, water, electricity, gas, energy market operator, liquid fuel, hospital, education, food and grocery, port, freight infrastructure, freight services, public transport, aviation and defence industry assets (22 classes), plus assets declared under s 51 or prescribed by rules. Each class has its own quantitative definition in Part 1, Division 2 of the Act (verify the threshold for your class). Commonwealth-owned assets are excluded unless declared (s 9(2A)); no space technology assets are currently defined.

**Which obligations switch on** depends on the rules, not the Act alone: the Application Rules (LIN 22/026) list the classes subject to the Part 2 register and Part 2B incident reporting (Part 2B covers 20 classes plus carrier-owned telecommunications assets); the CIRMP Rules s 4(1) list 13 classes subject to Part 2A (broadcasting, DNS, data storage or processing, electricity, energy market operator, gas, designated hospitals in Schedule 1, food and grocery, freight infrastructure, freight services, liquid fuel, payment-system financial market infrastructure, water); telecommunications assets are covered by the separate TSRMP Rules 2025. Banking, superannuation and insurance assets have register and Part 2B duties but no CIRMP obligation (they sit under APRA prudential standards). Part 2C applies only to assets declared SoNS under s 52B.

**Data storage systems:** a data storage system that stores or processes *business critical data* is part of the critical infrastructure asset (s 9(7)). Business critical data includes personal information on at least 20,000 individuals, research and development information, and information about or needed to operate the asset (s 5). A responsible entity must, as soon as practicable, inform any commercial data storage/processing provider that it holds business critical data (s 12F(3), 50 penalty units) — this pulls cloud and managed-service providers into scope.

**Cyber Security Act:** Part 2 applies to *relevant connectable products* (internet- or network-connectable, s 13) manufactured or supplied new on or after commencement, with the standard applying to consumer-grade products (Rules s 8) excluding desktops, laptops, tablets, smartphones, therapeutic goods, and road vehicles and their components. Part 3 applies to a *reporting business entity*: a business carried on in Australia (not a Commonwealth or State body) whose previous-financial-year turnover exceeded A$3 million (pro-rated for part-year businesses, Rules s 6), or any responsible entity for a Part 2B SOCI asset regardless of turnover (s 26(2)).

### CIRMP Rules (LIN 23/006) — baseline and enhanced requirements

| Hazard domain | Baseline (all 13 CIRMP asset classes) | Enhanced (9 classes: broadcasting, DNS, electricity, energy market operator, gas, liquid fuel, water, freight infrastructure, freight services — s 4A(1)) |
|---|---|---|
| All hazards / material risk (ss 6, 6A, 7) | Identify operational context, interdependencies, responsible positions, risk methodology; material risk includes stoppage, loss of a critical component, OT/ICT interference, offshoring of sensitive operational information, remote access to control systems, impact on business-critical-data systems | Additional material risks: impairment prejudicing social/economic stability, national security or defence; foreign ownership, control or influence (FOCI); offshore or remote access to critical components or business critical data |
| Cyber and information security (ss 8, 8A–8C) | Within 12 months after the 6-month grace period (i.e. by 17 August 2024 for original assets), comply with one of: AS ISO/IEC 27001:2015; ASD Essential Eight **Maturity Level 1**; NIST CSF; DOE C2M2 MIL-1; AEMO AESCSF 2020-21 SP-1; or an equivalent framework | Uplift to AS ISO/IEC 27001:2023; Essential Eight **Maturity Level 2**; NIST CSF 2.0; C2M2 v2.1 MIL-2; AESCSF 2023 SP-2 (or equivalent to items 2, 4 or 5); manage patching, legacy and emerging-technology risks (s 8A(2)); **phishing-resistant MFA** with central logging and review of all MFA attempts (s 8B); **network segregation** of critical systems able to run independently for at least three months, logical access controls, logged communication paths, least privilege (s 8C) |
| Personnel (ss 9, 9A) | Identify critical workers; assess suitability before granting access to critical components (AusCheck background check optional); manage malicious/negligent insiders and off-boarding | Critical workers must hold an AusCheck background check or an NV1-or-higher clearance (or documented risk treatment); manage privileged access, credential misuse and non-critical-worker access |
| Supply chain (ss 10, 10A) | Identify major suppliers; minimise supply-chain exploitation, privileged-access misuse, disruption and over-reliance | Map major suppliers and critical components across the supply chain; define maximum acceptable outage; vendor assessment covering FOCI, sanctions and the supplier's access, influence and control |
| Physical and natural (ss 11, 11A) | Identify physical critical components; control and test access arrangements; respond to unauthorised access | Centrally manage physical security; document site, critical components, data-holding areas, surveillance and out-of-hours controls; consider physical consequences of cyber, supply-chain and personnel hazards |

Grace periods for enhanced requirements (s 4A(6)): 12 months for ss 6A, 8A(2) and 9A(2); 24 months for the remaining enhanced sections (8A otherwise, 8B, 8C, 9A otherwise, 10A, 11A); assets that become critical later get the same periods from that date. Measured from the enhanced rules' 10 June 2026 commencement that is 10 June 2027 and 10 June 2028 (verify: s 4A(6) as compiled refers to "the commencement of this instrument"). Assets declared under s 51 before 10 June 2026 are not automatically subject to the enhanced tier (s 12).

## Enforcement and penalties

- **Mechanism:** every civil penalty provision in both Acts is enforceable under the *Regulatory Powers (Standard Provisions) Act 2014* — pecuniary penalty orders, enforceable undertakings and injunctions (SOCI s 49; Cyber Security Act s 79); SOCI adds monitoring and investigation powers and infringement notices (Part 5, Divs 3–4). Courts may impose up to **5 times** the stated penalty on a body corporate (Regulatory Powers Act s 82(5)).
- **Penalty unit value:** A$364 from 1 July 2026, the result of the CPI indexation in Crimes Act 1914 s 4AA(3) and published under s 4AA(1A) by the *Crimes (Amount of a Penalty Unit) Instrument 2026* (the pre-indexation figure in s 4AA(1) is A$330). Indexation recurs every third 1 July, so the next is 1 July 2029 — check the published amount before quoting dollar figures. At A$364, a 200-unit CIRMP breach is A$72,800 (A$364,000 for a corporation); the 2,000-unit telecoms direction breach is A$728,000 (A$3.64 million).
- **Regulatory posture:** CISC treated the first 12 months of Part 2B reporting (from 8 July 2022) as a learning phase (secondary source). Home Affairs ran an "education-first" phase for ransomware reporting from 30 May to 31 December 2025, moving to a compliance and enforcement posture from 1 January 2026 (Home Affairs factsheet). ASD is expressly *not* the regulator for ransomware reports.
- **Board exposure:** the s 30AG annual report is a board-approved attestation; false or misleading statements engage the Criminal Code offences (ss 137.1–137.2) referenced in the Cyber Security Act, and s 30AI directions target "seriously deficient" programs.
- **Information protection cuts both ways:** SOCI protected information carries a criminal offence for misuse by officials (s 45), while the ransomware and limited-use provisions shield reporting entities from regulatory self-incrimination but not from criminal exposure (e.g. sanctions or terrorism-financing law when paying a ransom).

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Classify every asset against ss 8D–12P and the Application/CIRMP Rules** and record which Parts apply (register, 2A, 2B, 2C, 2D) — including data storage systems holding business critical data and cloud/MSP providers to be notified under s 12F(3). See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Run two incident clocks in the playbook:** 12 hours (availability significantly impacted) and 72 hours (any relevant impact, including imminent incidents), plus the 84/48-hour written follow-up, the ASD ReportCyber channel, and — if a ransom is paid — the 72-hour ransomware payment report. Log every notification in [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md) and see [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md).
3. **Build or uplift the CIRMP** as a documented, board-owned program across all five hazard domains; map the chosen framework (Essential Eight ML1/ML2, ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF 2.0, C2M2, AESCSF) to controls with [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md); see [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md) and [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md).
4. **Enhanced-tier assets:** plan phishing-resistant MFA, critical-system segregation with three-month independent operation, AusCheck/NV1 for critical workers, supply-chain mapping with maximum acceptable outage, and FOCI vendor assessment against the June 2027 / June 2028 deadlines. Use [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
5. **Annual attestation:** calendar the 90-day post-financial-year board approval of the s 30AG report; evidence that the program was reviewed, kept current, and that significant-impact hazards were evaluated. See [../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/grc-board-report.md](../../templates/grc-board-report.md).
6. **Ransomware decision governance:** define who may authorise a payment, how sanctions/criminal exposure is checked, and how the 72-hour report (including non-monetary benefits and negotiations) is compiled; the report cannot be used against you in regulatory action but is not a safe harbour for unlawful payments.
7. **Track pending change** — Part 3 directions reforms, CIRB review outputs, penalty-unit indexation, and future asset-class rules — via [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md).
8. **Product teams:** for consumer-connected products, embed the three smart-device requirements in design reviews, publish the support period and vulnerability-disclosure channel, and retain statements of compliance for five years.

## Interplay

- **Privacy Act 1988 (NDB scheme) and APRA CPS 234:** one incident can trigger SOCI 12/72-hour reporting to ASD, APRA CPS 234 material-incident notification, and OAIC notifiable-data-breach assessment; business critical data is defined partly by personal-information volume (20,000 individuals). See [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md) and [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md); dedicated packs on the Australian Privacy Act and APRA CPS 234/230 sit alongside this one.
- **Telecommunications Act 1997:** carriers and carriage service providers report incidents under licence conditions/service-provider rules mirroring Part 2B (secondary source); Part 2D and the TSRMP Rules now carry the security obligation.
- **Corporations Act continuous disclosure:** listed entities must reconcile ASX disclosure with SOCI and limited-use confidentiality; a power to defer disclosure during a high-risk incident has been floated in reform commentary but is not law (verify).
- **EU NIS2 / CER:** SOCI's all-hazards CIRMP resembles the CER Directive's resilience plans and NIS2's risk-management measures, but SOCI's 12-hour availability clock is shorter than NIS2's 24-hour early warning; see [nis2.md](nis2.md).
- **UK PSTI and EU Cyber Resilience Act:** the smart-device standard is derived from ETSI consumer-IoT baseline requirements (per legal commentary) and mirrors the UK PSTI regime's three requirements (passwords, vulnerability disclosure, update period), while the EU CRA goes further into secure-by-design and conformity assessment; multinational product programs should treat PSTI compliance as the baseline and check the Australian statement-of-compliance and five-year retention specifics.
- **Frameworks:** the CIRMP Rules are the only Australian law that names ISO/IEC 27001, NIST CSF 2.0 and the Essential Eight as compliance options — see [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md), [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md) and [../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md).
