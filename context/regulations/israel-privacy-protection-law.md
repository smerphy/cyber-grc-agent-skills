# Israel: Protection of Privacy Law 5741-1981, Amendment 13 and the Data Security Regulations (PPL / PPA regime)

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | Protection of Privacy Law, 5741-1981 (PPL), as amended by Amendment No. 13, 5784-2024 (Sefer HaHukim 5784 p. 1430); Protection of Privacy (Data Security) Regulations, 5777-2017; Protection of Privacy (Transfer of Data to Databases Abroad) Regulations, 5761-2001; Protection of Privacy (Provisions for Data Transferred to Israel from the EEA) Regulations, 5783-2023; Protection of Privacy (Administrative Warning) Regulations, 5786-2026 |
| Regulator | Privacy Protection Authority (PPA), a unit of the Ministry of Justice whose independence is anchored in Government Resolution 1890 of 2 October 2022 (now the PPL's First Schedule); headed by the "Head of the Authority" |
| Status and key dates | PPL passed 23 February 1981; Data Security Regulations signed 5 April 2017, in force one year after publication; EEA Regulations published 7 May 2023 (phased to 1 January 2025); Amendment 13 passed by the Knesset in August 2024 and in force since August 2025 (consolidated text current to 14 August 2025 — verify the exact commencement date against the Sefer HaHukim text); Administrative Warning Regulations signed 14 April 2026 |
| Who is covered | Any "controller" (בעל שליטה) or "holder"/processor (מחזיק) of a "database" — a collection of personal data processed by digital means — in the public and private sectors; only personal-use collections and name/address/contact-only lists of at most 100,000 people are excluded |
| Structure | Chapter A: privacy torts and offences; Chapter B: databases (registration/notification, transparency, access and correction, security, DPO, direct mail, the PPA); Chapter D: public bodies; Chapters D1–D3 (Amendment 13): inspection, administrative inquiry, administrative enforcement; Schedules 3–5: fine tables |
| Penalties | Administrative fines fixed per breach type — flat amounts (15,000 / 150,000 / 300,000 NIS) or per-data-subject amounts (2–8 NIS per person with floors of 20,000–200,000 NIS), doubled for databases of 1,000,000+ people or for repeat breaches within two years; criminal offences up to five years' imprisonment; statutory damages up to 50,000 NIS without proof of harm |
| Breach clock | Data Security Regulations reg. 11(d): a "serious security event" in a medium- or high-security database must be notified to the PPA **immediately**, with a follow-up report on remedial steps; the PPA may order notification of affected data subjects after consulting the head of the Israel National Cyber Directorate |
| Assessment model | No certification. Self-implementation evidenced by the database definitions document, security procedure, periodic internal/external audits (every 24 months, medium/high) and risk surveys and penetration tests (every 18 months, high); the PPA can deem compliance with an approved standard or sectoral regulator's directions as compliance (reg. 20(b)) |
| Relationship to neighbours | EU adequacy since Commission Decision 2011/61/EU, reconfirmed in the Commission's first review report of 15 January 2024; UK adequacy regulations also list Israel; Amendment 13 deliberately imports GDPR concepts (controller/processor, broad personal data, special categories, DPO) without copying the GDPR |

## What it is

The PPL is Israel's omnibus privacy statute. Chapter A creates a civil wrong and criminal offence of "infringement of privacy" (surveillance, publication of private matters, use of information for a purpose other than the one it was given for, and so on); Chapter B, added in the 1990s, regulates computerised databases. For three decades Chapter B rested on a universal database-registration model, weak supervisory powers and a Registrar of Databases. The Data Security Regulations of 2017 modernised the security layer with a tiered, prescriptive control set; the 2023 EEA Regulations added GDPR-style deletion, retention and transparency duties for data received from Europe to preserve EU adequacy.

Amendment 13 — the most extensive revision since enactment — rewrote the definitions around "personal data", "processing", "controller" and "holder", narrowed database registration to two categories and replaced it for most large sensitive databases with a notification duty, made a privacy protection officer (DPO) mandatory for defined bodies, and gave the PPA a full administrative-enforcement toolkit: inspectors, administrative inquiries, search and computer-access warrants, cease-and-desist orders, administrative fines, administrative warnings, undertakings with deposits, mandatory publication of sanctions, preliminary opinions on request and an annual report to the Knesset Constitution Committee. The Hebrew text is the only authoritative version; the PPA publishes English translations of the law and regulations but they are unofficial.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Concept (PPL s. 3, post-Amendment 13) | Definition and practical test |
|---|---|
| Personal data (מידע אישי) | Data relating to an identified person or a person identifiable "with reasonable effort", directly or indirectly, including via name, ID number, biometric identifier, location data, online identifier, or one or more factors of physical, health, economic, social or cultural status |
| Data of special sensitivity (מידע בעל רגישות מיוחדת) | Twelve categories: intimate/family life and sexual orientation; health and medical data; genetic data; biometric identifiers used for identification; ethnic origin; criminal record; political opinions, religious beliefs and worldview; professional personality assessments; location and traffic data from licensed telecom providers (and location data revealing other sensitive categories); salary and financial-activity data; data subject to a statutory duty of confidentiality; further categories designated by the Minister of Justice for data imported from abroad |
| Database (מאגר מידע) | A collection of personal data processed by digital means, excluding (1) collections for personal, non-business use and (2) name/address/contact-only lists of 100,000 people or fewer that reveal nothing further, provided the owner (or a controlled company) holds no other collection about the same people |
| Controller (בעל שליטה) | Whoever determines, alone or jointly, the purposes of processing of the data in the database, or a body authorised by statute to process it |
| Holder (מחזיק) | A party external to the controller that processes data on its behalf — the processor. Data Security Regulations reg. 19 extends the controller's security duties to the holder and to the "database manager" |
| Processing / use | Any operation on personal data, including receipt, collection, storage, copying, viewing, disclosure, exposure, transfer, delivery or granting access |
| Public body (s. 23) | Government ministries and other state institutions, local authorities and other bodies performing public functions by law (paragraph (1)); further bodies designated by order (paragraph (2)) — public bodies carry stricter registration, DPO and disclosure rules |
| Territorial reach | The PPL has no GDPR-style territorial-scope article; application to foreign controllers turns on the location of the database, the processing and the affected individuals and on general Israeli conflict-of-laws principles (verify case by case). The transfer regulations bite whenever data leaves a database in Israel |

Sectoral overlays run in parallel and are not displaced by the PPL: Bank of Israel Proper Conduct of Banking Business directives on cyber-defence management (Directive 361 — verify current version) for banks, Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority circulars for insurers and pension bodies, and Israel National Cyber Directorate (INCD) methodologies for critical infrastructure and the wider economy. Regulation 20(b) of the Data Security Regulations lets the PPA recognise compliance with such directions as compliance with the regulations.

## Core obligations

### Lawful processing, registration and notification (PPL Chapter B)

| Provision | Requirement |
|---|---|
| s. 8(b)–(d) | Process personal data in a database only for the database's lawfully determined purpose, only with the controller's authorisation, and not if the data was created, received or collected in breach of the PPL or any other law governing processing (with a good-faith exception for data received from a third party) |
| s. 8A(a) — registration | Registration with the PPA is required only for (a) databases whose primary purpose is collecting personal data for supply to others as a business or for consideration (including direct-mail services) and which hold data on more than 10,000 people, and (b) databases controlled by a public body under s. 23 paragraph (1), unless they hold only employee data. The Head of the PPA registers within 60 days; a database counts as registered if that period lapses without a refusal or suspension |
| s. 8A(b) — notification | A non-registrable database holding data of special sensitivity on more than 100,000 people must be notified to the PPA within 30 days of crossing the threshold: controller identity and contacts, the DPO (where required) and a copy of the database definitions document; changes and cessation notified within 30 days |
| s. 11 — notice at collection | Every request to a person for personal data for database processing must state whether provision is mandatory or voluntary and the consequence of refusal, the purpose, the controller's name and contacts, the recipients and purposes of onward disclosure, and the rights of access (s. 13) and correction (s. 14) |
| ss. 13–14 — rights | Right to access one's own personal data (in Hebrew, Arabic or English), subject to listed exemptions (security bodies, tax authorities, investigations, anti-money-laundering register, serious harm to health); right to request correction or deletion of data that is incorrect, incomplete, unclear or outdated; recipients of corrected data must be informed; refusals are appealable to court (s. 15) |
| s. 16 — confidentiality | Employees, managers and holders may disclose personal data obtained in their role only to perform their work, the PPL or a court order — breach is an offence punishable by five years' imprisonment |
| s. 17 / s. 17B — security and security officer | Controller and holder are each responsible for data security. A qualified **information security officer** is mandatory for a controller or holder of five registrable/notifiable databases, every public body, and banks, insurers and credit-rating/credit-scoring companies |
| ss. 17B1–17B3 — privacy protection officer (DPO) | Mandatory for: public-body controllers/holders (except security bodies); data brokers with more than 10,000 people; controllers/holders whose core activities require regular and systematic monitoring of people at significant scale (mobile operators and online search providers are named); and those whose core activity involves processing special-sensitivity data at significant scale (banks, insurers, general hospitals and health funds are named). Duties: professional authority and knowledge centre, training plan, ongoing compliance-monitoring plan with reports to management, verifying the security procedure and database definitions document, handling data-subject requests, PPA liaison. Reports to the CEO or a direct report; may be external; must have privacy-law, technology and business knowledge and be free of conflicts |
| Chapter B Part B — direct mail | Direct-mail databases must be registered with direct mail as a stated purpose; source-of-data records and opt-out/deletion rights apply |
| Chapter D — public bodies | Disclosure of personal data by public bodies restricted to statutory routes (ss. 23A–23H); regular data feeds from public bodies must be recorded and notified |

### Data Security Regulations 5777-2017 — tiered controls

Security level is fixed by the type of database (reg. 1 and the Schedules): **basic** (default); **medium** (First Schedule: data-broker/direct-mail databases, public-body databases, or databases holding listed sensitive data types — intimate life, medical/mental health, genetic, political/religious opinions, criminal record, telecom traffic data, biometric data, financial position, revealing consumption habits — unless the small-employer exception applies); **high** (Second Schedule: a medium-type database with data on 100,000 or more people or more than 100 authorised users). A "database managed by an individual" (owner plus at most two users, under 10,000 people, no professional-secrecy data, not a data broker) carries only a minimal subset (reg. 21).

| Reg. | Obligation | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Database definitions document: collection and use description, purposes, data types, transfers abroad (purpose, destination, method, recipient), processing by holders, main security risks and mitigations, names of the database manager, holder and security officer; review annually by 31 December and on significant change; annual check that no excess data is held | All (reg. 2 not extended to holders) |
| 3 | Security officer subordinate to the database manager or an active manager, prepares the security procedure and a continuous-control plan, no conflicting role, adequate resources | Where an officer is required or appointed |
| 4 | Written security procedure covering physical/environmental security, access authorisations, protective measures, user instructions, risk treatment including accepted encryption, incident handling and portable devices; medium/high add identification and authentication, access-control logging, periodic audits, backup and development controls; reviewed annually and on material change | All (reduced content for basic) |
| 5 | Current system-structure document and inventory (hardware, software, interfaces, network diagram); **risk survey at least every 18 months** and **penetration tests at least every 18 months**, both with findings discussed and remediated | Inventory: all; survey and tests: high only |
| 6–7 | Protected server locations; entry/exit and equipment logging (medium/high); vetting before granting access; training before access and **refresher training at least every two years** (medium/high) | As stated |
| 8–9 | Role-based least-privilege authorisations with a maintained authorisation register; identification and authentication, preferably by a physical token under the user's exclusive control (medium/high); password strength, lockout and rotation no longer than six months; automatic session disconnection; immediate revocation on role end | As stated |
| 10 | Automatic, tamper-resistant access logging (user, time, component, access type, outcome), routine log review with a problems-and-actions report, **logs retained at least 24 months** | Medium/high |
| 11 | Document all security events (automatically where possible); incident-response provisions in the security procedure; security-event review at least annually (medium) or **quarterly** (high); for a **serious security event** (high: any unauthorised use or integrity breach; medium: the same affecting a substantial part of the database) notify the PPA **immediately** and report remedial steps; PPA may order data-subject notification after consulting the INCD head | All for (a)–(b); (c)–(d) medium/high |
| 12–14 | Portable-device restrictions with accepted encryption; segregation of database systems from other systems as far as reasonably possible; patching and no unsupported systems without compensating controls; internet exposure only behind protective measures; encryption of data in transit over public networks; remote access with additional identity verification and a physical token for medium/high users | All (token requirement medium/high) |
| 15 | Outsourcing: pre-contract security risk assessment; contract fixing permitted data and purposes, systems and processing types, duration, return/destruction and reporting, security measures, staff confidentiality undertakings, flow-down to sub-processors, **annual compliance report and security-event notification** to the controller; controller supervision proportionate to risk | All except contracts with an individual |
| 16 | **Internal or external audit at least every 24 months** by a qualified person other than the security officer; findings discussed and reflected in the definitions document or procedure; high-level databases may fold this into the risk survey | Medium/high |
| 17–18 | Retain security data generated under regs 6(b), 8–11, 14, 15(a)(4) and 16 securely for 24 months; medium/high back it up restorably with documented backup and restore procedures; high keeps an integrity-protected copy | As stated |
| 19 | Duties apply to the database manager and (except regs 2 and 15(a)) to holders; every non-document action must be reasonably documented | All |
| 20 | PPA may exempt or extend duties for a specific database and may deem compliance with an approved standard or a competent authority's security directions as compliance | — |

### International transfers

| Instrument | Rule |
|---|---|
| Transfer Regulations 5761-2001, reg. 1 | No transfer from a database in Israel abroad unless the destination's law ensures a level of protection not lower than Israeli law, including lawful and fair collection, purpose limitation, accuracy, access and correction rights and adequate security |
| reg. 2 — derogations | Consent of the data subject; vital health interests where consent cannot be obtained; transfer to a corporation controlled by the transferring controller that has ensured privacy protection; a written contractual undertaking by the recipient to observe Israeli database conditions mutatis mutandis; data lawfully published; public safety or security; required by Israeli law; destination is a party to the Council of Europe Convention 108, receives data from EU member states on the same terms, or has a privacy authority with a published cooperation arrangement |
| reg. 3 | For every transfer the controller must obtain the recipient's written undertaking to take sufficient measures to protect privacy and not to onward-transfer the data (the PPA has read this as permitting onward transfers under conditions) |
| EEA Regulations 5783-2023 | For data received from the EEA (and, from 1 January 2025, any other data in the same database): deletion on written request where data was unlawfully obtained, its use has become unlawful or it is no longer necessary, subject to listed exceptions; an organisational/technological mechanism to purge data no longer necessary; an accuracy mechanism with correction or deletion; information to the data subject (identity of controller and manager, purpose, data types, rights) within one month of receipt and again before onward disclosure; ethnic origin and trade-union membership treated as sensitive data. Breaches attract fines under the PPL's Fourth Schedule |

## Enforcement and penalties

**Administrative enforcement (PPL Chapter D3, Amendment 13).** The Head of the PPA may order cessation of a breach (s. 23KE) — e.g., purpose deviation, processing of unlawfully obtained data, breaches of the EEA Regulations, or DPO failures — and, after a notice of intent and a 45-day right to be heard (ss. 23KZ–23KH), impose financial sanctions (s. 23KV):

| Tier | Amount | Illustrative breaches |
|---|---|---|
| Flat 150,000 NIS (doubled if the database holds 1,000,000+ people) | Processing in an unregistered registrable database; false registration details; failure to notify a notifiable database or changes to it; unregistered direct-mail database |
| Flat 15,000 NIS | Refusing access; changing data without informing recipients; not notifying a refusal to correct |
| 50 NIS per person approached (100 NIS for special-sensitivity data), floor 30,000 NIS | Solicitation/direct-mail notice breaches |
| 2 NIS per data subject (4 NIS for special-sensitivity data), floor 20,000/40,000 NIS | No information security officer; no DPO where required for public bodies or data brokers; missing direct-mail source records; ignoring DPO-related cease orders |
| 4 NIS per data subject (8 NIS for special-sensitivity data), floor 200,000 NIS | Ignoring cease orders; processing for an unlawful purpose; processing without or beyond the controller's authorisation; processing unlawfully obtained data |
| Third Schedule (Data Security Regulations) | Per-item amounts by tier — individual-managed / basic / medium / high — e.g., missing or stale definitions document 2,000 / 2,000 / 40,000 / 160,000 NIS; failure to notify a serious security event immediately 80,000 (medium) / 320,000 (high); missing risk survey or penetration test 320,000 (high); outsourcing contract or supervision failures 4,000 / 80,000 / 320,000; missing periodic audit 40,000 / 160,000; doubled for high-level databases of 1,000,000+ people |
| Flat 300,000 NIS | Failure to hand documents or computer material to an inspector |

Modifiers: a repeat breach of the same provision within two years adds a further equal amount (s. 23L); amounts are CPI-indexed each 1 January, first in 2026 (s. 23LB); reductions under the Fifth Schedule for clean history (10–20%), self-reporting (30%), remediation (20%), late DPO appointment (10%) and personal circumstances, capped at 70% cumulatively, with caps for micro businesses (turnover up to 4 million NIS) and small businesses (4–10 million NIS) and a ceiling of 5% of turnover on request. Alternatives to a fine: an **administrative warning** (s. 23LV) — the Administrative Warning Regulations of 14 April 2026 allow it for breaches in the first three months of a new provision, first breaches of listed data-security items, and periods of up to three months (extendable to six) after the PPA announces a stricter enforcement policy or interpretation — or a written **undertaking with a deposit** (ss. 23LT–23M). Every fine is published on the PPA website for four years (corporations) or two years (individuals) (s. 23MV); decisions are appealable to the Magistrates' Court within 45 days.

**Criminal.** Intentional infringement of privacy (s. 5) and breach of confidentiality (s. 16): five years' imprisonment; processing without the controller's authorisation and misleading a data subject at collection: three years; misleading the PPA: two years; obstructing an inspector: six months. Individuals may also file private criminal complaints.

**Civil.** Infringement of privacy is a tort (s. 4) and any breach of Chapters B or D or the regulations is a tort under s. 31B; courts may award up to 50,000 NIS without proof of damage, doubled where intent to harm is proven (s. 29A); Amendment 13 added exemplary damages up to 10,000 NIS for listed breaches (unregistered database, missing s. 11 notice, denied access, unactioned correction) after the data subject's demand goes unanswered for 30–90 days (s. 15A). Class actions under the Class Actions Law are the main private-litigation vehicle in practice.

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Inventory databases and classify each to a security level** (individual-managed / basic / medium / high) using the Schedules' data-type, headcount (100,000) and user-count (100) thresholds; the level drives every other obligation and the fine column. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Decide registration vs notification** under s. 8A: register data-broker and public-body databases; notify special-sensitivity databases above 100,000 people within 30 days, attaching the definitions document; track changes on a 30-day clock.
3. **Appoint and empower the DPO and the information security officer** where mandated (ss. 17B, 17B1–17B3): written mandate, direct reporting line to the CEO, conflict check, resources, published contact details.
4. **Produce and maintain the two core documents** — database definitions document and security procedure — with annual review by 31 December, and keep the system-structure document and inventory current. Use [../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md](../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/policy-template.md](../../templates/policy-template.md).
5. **Run the assurance cadence and keep the evidence**: risk survey and penetration test every 18 months (high), audit every 24 months (medium/high), refresher training every two years, quarterly (high) or annual (medium) security-event reviews, 24-month log and security-data retention. See [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
6. **Wire the "serious security event" definition into incident response** with an immediate PPA notification path and a follow-up remediation report; pre-agree who decides on data-subject notification and how INCD consultation is handled. See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md), [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md) and [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).
7. **Remediate vendor contracts against reg. 15** (scope, systems, duration, return/destruction, security measures, confidentiality undertakings, sub-processor flow-down, annual report, incident notice) and add supervision proportionate to risk. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
8. **Map and paper every cross-border flow**: 2001 regulation route plus written recipient undertaking; for EEA-origin data implement the deletion, purge, accuracy and one-month notice mechanisms. See [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
9. **Budget for fines by exposure**: per-data-subject amounts scale with headcount and sensitivity; document mitigation factors (self-reporting, remediation, clean history) that the Fifth Schedule rewards, and record known gaps as formal exceptions ([../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md](../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md)).
10. **Report to the board** on database inventory coverage, overdue surveys/tests/audits, open PPA correspondence and published sanctions. See [../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR:** Amendment 13 borrows the controller/processor split, the broad identifiability test and special categories, but keeps Israel's database-centric architecture (registration, definitions document, security levels) and fixed-amount fines instead of turnover-based ceilings. There is no general DPIA duty, no records-of-processing article and no 72-hour clock — the security-event notification is "immediate". An EU-headquartered group serving Israel must run both regimes; adequacy makes EU-to-Israel transfers frictionless but Israel-to-EU flows still need a reg. 2 route. See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md) and [eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md](eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md).
- **UK:** UK adequacy regulations list Israel, so UK-to-Israel transfers need no IDTA; see [uk-data-protection.md](uk-data-protection.md).
- **EU AI Act and AI governance:** the PPA has issued guidance on applying the PPL to AI systems (secondary sources, 2025 — verify); personality assessments and systematic monitoring are special-sensitivity/DPO triggers under the PPL. See [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) and [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md).
- **ISO/IEC 27001 and NIST CSF 2.0:** the Data Security Regulations read like a prescriptive subset of Annex A (access control, logging, cryptography, supplier relationships, incident management, backup) and reg. 20(b) lets the PPA recognise an approved standard; an ISO 27001 ISMS scoped to the database systems is the usual evidence backbone. See [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md), [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md) and [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **Sectoral cyber regimes:** Bank of Israel, Capital Market Authority and INCD directions apply cumulatively (reg. 25: the regulations apply in addition to other security provisions unless they conflict); a serious security event at a bank or insurer typically triggers parallel supervisory reporting with different clocks — verify the sectoral text.
- **Other regional regimes** for comparison: [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md).
