# European Health Data Space Regulation (EHDS — Regulation (EU) 2025/327)

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instrument | Regulation (EU) 2025/327 of 11 February 2025 on the European Health Data Space, amending Directive 2011/24/EU (cross-border healthcare) and Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act). | Entered into force | 26 March 2025 (per the Commission; Art. 105: twentieth day after OJ publication) |

## What it is

The EHDS is the first sector-specific common European data space. It does three things: gives natural persons enforceable rights of access to and control over their electronic health data for healthcare ("primary use"), including across borders through MyHealth@EU; creates a product-regulation regime for electronic health record (EHR) systems built around two mandatory harmonised software components (an interoperability component that reads and writes the European electronic health record exchange format, EEHRxF, and a logging component); and establishes a permit-based regime for the re-use of health data for research, innovation, policy and regulatory purposes ("secondary use") through national health data access bodies connected by HealthData@EU (Art. 1). Proposed by the Commission in 2022, it was adopted on 11 February 2025 and published on 5 March 2025.

For security and GRC teams the regulation matters because it converts several practices into hard legal duties with dates: access logging on every read of priority health data (Annex II, 3.2), a three-day serious-incident clock for EHR-system manufacturers (Art. 44(7)), EU-only storage and processing for secondary-use pseudonymisation and secure processing environments (Art. 87), a mandatory secure processing environment specification with one-year minimum log retention and regular third-party audits (Art. 73), and a re-identification prohibition backed by 4%-of-turnover fines (Arts. 61(3), 64(5)). Most of the operational detail sits in implementing acts due by 26 March 2027; the level-1 text fixes the architecture and the dates.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Actor | Test | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Healthcare providers / health professionals | Where data are processed electronically, must register priority-category data in an EHR system, keep it updated, and identify who registered/updated what and when; must be connected to the national contact point for digital health with two-way exchange | Arts. 11–13, 23(5) |
| EHR system manufacturers (and importers, distributors, authorised representatives) | "EHR system" = software, or hardware plus software, allowing priority-category personal electronic health data to be stored, intermediated, exported, imported, converted, edited or viewed, and intended by the manufacturer for use by healthcare providers in patient care or by patients accessing their data (Art. 2(2)(k)). General-purpose software used in healthcare is excluded (Art. 25(2)). In-house EHR systems built and used inside EU health institutions and EHR systems offered as a service (SaaS) to EU persons count as "put into service" (Art. 26(2)). Commission FAQ examples in scope: pharmacy dispensing systems, patient portals, EEHRxF conversion layers; out of scope: appointment schedulers, billing systems that do not process priority-category data, non-medical wellness apps |
| Medical devices, IVDs and high-risk AI systems claiming EHR interoperability | Must prove compliance with the Annex II Section 2 (interoperability) essential requirements and the Art. 36 common specifications | Art. 27 |
| Wellness applications claiming EHR interoperability | Manufacturer-issued label showing compliance with Annex II and the common specifications; registration in the EU database | Arts. 47–49 |
| Health data holders | Any natural or legal person, public authority or body in the healthcare or care sectors (including reimbursement), any person developing products or services for the health sector, wellness-app developers, health-related researchers, mortality registries and EU bodies, that either (i) as controller/joint controller has the right or obligation to process personal electronic health data for healthcare, public health, reimbursement, research, innovation, policymaking, statistics, patient safety or regulatory purposes, or (ii) can make non-personal electronic health data available through control of a product's technical design (Art. 2(2)(t)). **Processors are not holders** for the data they process on a controller's behalf (Commission FAQ Q32). **Exempt:** natural persons (including individual researchers) and microenterprises, unless national law extends the duties (Art. 50) |
| Health data users | Any natural or legal person, including EU bodies, granted access under a data permit, an approved health data request, or an access approval from a HealthData@EU authorised participant (Art. 2(2)(u)) |
| Third-country applicants | Eligible only if their country is an authorised HealthData@EU participant or is covered by a Commission reciprocity implementing act (Art. 91) |

Out of scope: processing outside Union law and processing by competent authorities for criminal-law purposes (Art. 1(9)). Contractual and administrative data-sharing arrangements between public or private entities are not affected (Art. 1(8)).

## Core obligations

### Primary use: patient rights and provider duties (Ch. II)

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Art. 3 | Right of access to at least the priority categories, immediately after registration in an EHR system, free of charge, through electronic health data access services; right to download a copy in EEHRxF. Member States may delay access on patient-safety grounds (GDPR Art. 23 basis) |
| Art. 4 | Member States must run free access services with proxy functionality (authorised persons and legal representatives), interoperable across borders, accessible to disabled and low-literacy users |
| Arts. 5–7 | Rights to insert information in one's own EHR, to rectification, and to data portability |
| Art. 8 | Right to restrict health-professional access to all or part of the data; the fact of a restriction must not be visible to providers; vital-interest override with logging (Art. 11(5)) |
| Art. 9 | Right to information on every access through the health professional access service, including automatic notifications; information available for at least **three years** from each access, covering who accessed, date/time, and which data |
| Art. 10 | Optional national opt-out from access via the access services; must be reversible |
| Art. 12 | Health-professional access services restricted to professionals holding eIDAS-recognised (Regulation 910/2014 Art. 6) or common-specification-compliant electronic identification |
| Art. 13 | Providers register priority-category data in an EHR system; every registration/update records the professional, provider and time; Commission data-quality implementing act due 26 March 2027 |
| Art. 14 / Annex I | Priority categories: (a) patient summaries, (b) electronic prescriptions, (c) electronic dispensations, (d) medical imaging studies and reports, (e) medical test results incl. laboratory results, (f) discharge reports |
| Art. 15 | EEHRxF technical specifications by implementing act due 26 March 2027; Member States must ensure priority data are issued in that format; receiving providers must accept and read it |
| Art. 16 | Patients may identify themselves with any eIDAS-recognised eID; Commission to specify a cross-border identification and authentication mechanism |
| Art. 23 | MyHealth@EU: Commission-run central platform; one national contact point for digital health per Member State (to be notified by 26 March 2027); national contact points are joint controllers, Commission is processor; implementing acts on security, confidentiality, compliance checks and cybersecurity/interoperability requirements due 26 March 2027 |
| Art. 86 | Member States must ensure a "particularly high level of protection and security" for primary-use processing and may require priority-category data to be stored within the Union |

### EHR systems and wellness applications (Ch. III)

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Art. 25 | Every EHR system must include the European interoperability software component and the European logging software component |
| Art. 30 | Manufacturer duties: conformity with Annex II and the common specifications; technical documentation (Art. 37, Annex III) kept up to date; information sheet (Art. 38); EU declaration of conformity (Art. 39, Annex IV); CE marking (Art. 41); registration (Art. 49); corrective action, recall or withdrawal with notification to national authorities; complaint channels and registers of complaints and non-conforming systems; design/development/deployment procedures that keep the components compliant; retention of technical documentation and declaration for **10 years** after placing on the market; source code or programming logic available to authorities on reasoned request |
| Art. 36 | Common specifications by implementing act due 26 March 2027, with an implementation time limit |
| Art. 37(4) | Technical documentation to a market surveillance authority within **30 days** of request (shorter if serious and immediate risk); authority may order independent testing at the manufacturer's expense |
| Art. 40 | Mandatory automated testing of the harmonised components in a European digital testing environment before placing on the market; results referenced in the technical documentation and entered in the EU database |
| Art. 44(7) | **Serious-incident reporting:** manufacturers report any serious incident (malfunction or deterioration that leads, might have led or might lead to death or serious harm to health, serious prejudice to a person's rights, or serious disruption of health-sector critical infrastructure — Art. 2(2)(r)) to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States where it occurred and where the system is on the market, with corrective action taken or envisaged, immediately after establishing a causal link or its reasonable likelihood and **no later than three days after becoming aware**. Without prejudice to NIS2 incident notification |
| Art. 44(10) | Where patient safety or information security is at risk, authorities may require immediate corrective action |
| Art. 45 | Non-compliance findings (Annex II, technical documentation, declaration, CE marking, registration) trigger corrective-action orders, then market prohibition, recall or withdrawal |
| Annex II §3 | Security and logging essential requirements: reliable identification and authentication of health professionals (3.1); the logging component records, for every access event or group of events, the accessing provider/individual, the natural person(s) whose data were accessed, the categories of data, date and time, and data origin(s) (3.2); tools or an interface for log review and analysis (3.3); support for differentiated retention periods and access rights by data origin and category (3.4) |
| Annex II §2 | Interoperability requirements: EEHRxF import/export interface; no features that prohibit, restrict or unduly burden authorised access, sharing or export for the purpose of switching systems (2.5–2.6) |
| Arts. 47–48 | Wellness apps claiming interoperability: manufacturer label, user-controlled export, EU database registration |

### Secondary use (Ch. IV)

| Article | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Art. 51 | Minimum data categories holders must make available: 17 categories including EHR data, administrative and claims data, genetic/genomic and other omics data, medical-device data, wellness-app data, registries, clinical-trial data, research cohorts and biobank data. Member States may add categories and may impose stricter safeguards for genetic, omics, wellness-app and biobank data (Art. 51(4)) |
| Art. 53 | Permitted purposes only: public health, policymaking/regulatory activities, official statistics (these three reserved to public bodies), education, scientific research including product development and algorithm training/testing/evaluation, and improving care delivery |
| Art. 54 | Prohibited uses: decisions detrimental to individuals, employment/insurance/credit discrimination, advertising or marketing, developing harmful products (tobacco, alcohol, weapons, addictive designs), activities against national ethics rules |
| Art. 60 | Holder duties: supply data to the HDAB within a reasonable time and **no later than three months** of the HDAB's request (extendable by three months); maintain and annually verify a dataset description in the national catalogue (Art. 77); document any data quality and utility label (Art. 78); non-personal data via trusted open databases |
| Art. 61 | User duties: process only under the permit; no onward access for third parties; **no re-identification or attempts**; publish results (anonymous only) within **18 months** of finishing processing; acknowledge the EHDS source; report significant health findings to the HDAB |
| Art. 66 | Data minimisation: anonymised data by default; pseudonymised only where the applicant shows the purpose cannot otherwise be met; re-identification keys held only by the HDAB or a trusted third party |
| Art. 67 | Application content includes named individuals who will access data, safeguards proportionate to risk (including anti-re-identification measures), needed SPE tools, processing period and, for pseudonymised data, a GDPR Art. 6(1) compliance description |
| Art. 68 | HDAB decides within **three months** of a complete application (extendable by three months; two months plus one for public-health bodies under the accelerated procedure); data made available to the user within **two months** of receipt from holders; permit duration up to **10 years**, extendable once for up to 10 years; SPE data deleted within **six months** of permit expiry |
| Art. 69 | Health data requests return only anonymised statistical answers; assessment within three months, response within a further three months |
| Art. 71 | **Opt-out from secondary use:** any natural person, at any time, without reasons, reversible; applies to permits and requests approved after the opt-out; Member States may legislate a narrow public-interest exception for public bodies (Art. 71(4)). Holders need not acquire extra identifiers solely to honour opt-outs (Art. 71(8); FAQ Q44) |
| Art. 72 | Member States may designate **trusted health data holders** that run their own compliant SPE and pre-assess applications (proposal within two months; HDAB decides within a further two months) |
| Art. 73 | **Secure processing environment (SPE):** access limited to persons named in the permit; state-of-the-art measures against unauthorised reading, copying, modification or removal; input/inspection/deletion limited to identifiable authorised individuals; unique user identities and confidential access modes; identifiable access and activity logs kept for at least **one year**; monitoring; HDAB review of download requests so only non-personal data leave; **regular audits including by third parties** with corrective action; Commission implementing act on technical, organisational, information-security and interoperability requirements due 26 March 2027 |
| Art. 74 | Controllership: holder is controller for making data available; HDAB is controller for its own tasks and processor for the user's processing inside the SPE; Commission may issue a template controller–processor agreement |
| Art. 75 | HealthData@EU: one national contact point for secondary use per Member State (notified by 26 March 2027); Commission runs the central platform as processor; implementing acts on architecture, security and confidentiality due 26 March 2027 |
| Art. 87 | HDABs, trusted holders, the Union health data access service and anyone acting on their behalf must store and process personal electronic health data **in the Union** for pseudonymisation, anonymisation and SPE operations; exception only for GDPR Art. 45 adequacy territories |
| Arts. 88–91 | Non-personal health data derived from personal data may be deemed "highly sensitive" under the Data Governance Act for third-country transfer (Art. 88); third-country court or administrative orders for non-personal health data held in the Union are enforceable only under an international agreement or strict conditions, with minimum disclosure and holder notification (Art. 89); personal-data transfers follow GDPR Ch. V plus any national restrictions (Art. 90) |

## Enforcement and penalties

| Regime | Who imposes | Ceiling / measure | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holder/user duty breaches (Art. 60; Art. 61(1), (5), (6)) | HDAB | EUR 10 000 000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher | Art. 64(4) |
| Prohibited uses (Art. 54), extracting personal data from an SPE, re-identification or attempts, ignoring HDAB enforcement measures | HDAB | EUR 20 000 000 or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher | Art. 64(5) |
| Non-compliance by users | HDAB | Revoke the permit, stop processing, exclusion from EHDS secondary-use access for up to **five years** | Art. 63(3) |
| Holder withholding data or missing the Art. 60(2) deadline | HDAB | Daily periodic penalty payments set under national law; repeated non-cooperation: exclusion from submitting applications for up to five years while remaining obliged to supply data | Art. 63(4) |
| Patient-rights articles (Arts. 3, 5–10) | GDPR supervisory authority | Fines up to the GDPR Art. 83(5) ceiling (the top GDPR tier — see [gdpr.md](gdpr.md)) | Art. 22 |
| Secondary-use opt-out (Art. 71) | GDPR supervisory authority | Fines up to the GDPR Art. 83 amounts | Art. 65 |
| EHR-system non-conformity | Market surveillance authority | Corrective-action orders, market prohibition, recall, withdrawal | Arts. 44–46 |
| All other infringements | Member States | National penalties, "effective, proportionate and dissuasive"; rules notified to the Commission by 26 March 2027; criteria include turnover in the Union | Art. 99 |
| Civil | Courts | Right to compensation for material or non-material damage (Art. 100); representation by not-for-profit bodies (Art. 101); complaints to digital health authorities (Art. 21) and HDABs (Art. 81) | Arts. 100–101 |

Fines under Art. 64 are cumulative-capped at the most serious infringement for linked permits (Art. 64(3)); Member States may exempt public bodies (Art. 64(6)); a Member State without administrative fines must provide equivalent remedies (Art. 64(8)). HDABs must refer suspected GDPR breaches to supervisory authorities (Art. 63(2)). Commission guidelines on HDAB enforcement measures are due by 26 March 2032 (Art. 63(8)).

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Classify your role(s):** healthcare provider, EHR-system manufacturer (including in-house and SaaS builds), interoperability-claiming device/AI/wellness-app vendor, health data holder, health data user — many organisations are several at once. Processors are not holders; controllers are. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Inventory systems that touch Art. 14 priority categories** and decide which are EHR systems under Art. 2(2)(k). Those need the two harmonised components, Annex II conformity, testing-environment results, technical documentation retained 10 years, CE marking and EU database registration before market placement (2029/2031 deadlines).
3. **Engineer the logging component now:** per-access records of who, whose data, which categories, when and from where (Annex II 3.2), reviewable (3.3), feeding the patient's three-year access-history right (Art. 9). Map it to existing logging controls with [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md).
4. **Add the Art. 44(7) three-day serious-incident clock** to the incident playbook alongside the NIS2 significant-incident and GDPR Art. 33 personal-data-breach clocks; the trigger definitions and recipients differ. See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md), [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md) and [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md).
5. **As a holder, build the dataset catalogue and a three-month fulfilment process** (Art. 60), an opt-out handling design consistent with data minimisation (Art. 71(8)), and pseudonymisation that keeps keys away from users (Art. 66(3)). A DPIA is the natural vehicle: [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
6. **As a user, treat the permit as a control baseline:** named individuals only, SPE-only processing, no downloads of personal data, anti-re-identification safeguards documented in the application (Art. 67(2)(g)), 18-month publication duty. Re-identification carries the 4% fine tier.
7. **If operating or supplying an SPE (HDAB, trusted holder, or their cloud/IT providers):** EU-only storage and processing (Art. 87), one-year minimum access logs, regular third-party audits (Art. 73(3)), and readiness for the Art. 73(5) implementing act. Flow these into vendor contracts via [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
8. **Align identity and access with eIDAS-recognised eID** for professionals and patients (Arts. 12, 16) and support the Art. 8 restriction and Art. 10 opt-out semantics in access-control design.
9. **Track the 2027 implementing acts and the Digital Omnibus** as horizon items; most technical obligations crystallise only when those acts land. See [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR:** the EHDS "specifies and complements" GDPR rights (Art. 1(2)(a)); GDPR definitions apply (Art. 2(1)(a)); Art. 6(1) lawful basis must still be shown for pseudonymised secondary use (Arts. 67(4), 68(1)(c)); controller/processor roles are allocated by Art. 74; international transfers follow GDPR Ch. V (Art. 90); supervisory authorities enforce Arts. 3, 5–10 and 71 at GDPR fine levels. A breach inside an SPE is simultaneously a GDPR Art. 33 event. See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md).
- **NIS2:** health is a NIS2 sector (see [nis2.md](nis2.md) for the entity list); EHDS Art. 44(7) reporting is expressly without prejudice to NIS2 incident notification, digital health authorities must cooperate with NIS2 authorities (Art. 19(2)(m)), and the Commission's EHDS page names NIS as one of the horizontal frameworks the EHDS builds on. A serious incident in an EHR system at an essential or important entity can therefore trigger EHDS, NIS2 and GDPR reporting to three different recipients.
- **Cyber Resilience Act:** Art. 104 amends the CRA so that manufacturers of products with digital elements classified as EHR systems demonstrate CRA Annex I conformity through the EHDS Ch. III procedure, with a single set of technical documentation and a single risk assessment where other Union acts apply.
- **AI Act / MDR / IVDR:** high-risk AI systems, medical devices and IVDs claiming EHR interoperability must meet the Annex II interoperability requirements and common specifications (Art. 27) and are registered in both the sectoral databases and the EHDS database (Art. 49(3)); the EHDS is without prejudice to those acts on security (Art. 1(5)). Secondary use explicitly covers training, testing and evaluating algorithms (Art. 53(1)(e)(ii)) — pair with [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) and [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md).
- **Data Governance Act and Data Act:** the EHDS is without prejudice to both (Art. 1(3)); DGA definitions of "secure processing environment" and "public sector body" apply (Art. 2(1)(c)); data-altruism organisations processing health data must meet Art. 73 SPE measures (Art. 73(4)); fee disputes go to Data Act Art. 10 dispute-settlement bodies (Art. 62(4)); HealthData@EU must be interoperable with other common European data spaces under both acts (Art. 75(11)). The Digital Omnibus proposal to repeal the DGA would require these cross-references to be re-read against the amended Data Act.
- **Cross-border healthcare Directive 2011/24/EU:** amended by Art. 103; e-prescription dispensing across borders via MyHealth@EU follows Art. 11 of the Directive (Art. 23(6)).
- **HIPAA (US):** no equivalence or mutual recognition. US organisations are reached as manufacturers or importers placing EHR systems on the EU market (Ch. III), as EU-established holders or users, or as third-country applicants under the Art. 91 reciprocity test. Compare the entity-based coverage model in [hipaa.md](hipaa.md).
- **Frameworks:** Annex II 3.x and Art. 73 map naturally to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 logging, access-control and supplier controls and to the NIST CSF 2.0 Protect and Detect functions; see [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md) and [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md).
