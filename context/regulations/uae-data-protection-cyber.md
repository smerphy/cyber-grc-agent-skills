# United Arab Emirates: Federal PDPL, DIFC and ADGM data protection, and UAE cybersecurity requirements

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | **Mainland privacy:** Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data (PDPL), 31 articles. **Free zones:** DIFC Data Protection Law, DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020 (as amended by DIFC Laws Amendment Law No. 1 of 2025); ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021 (amended 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025). **Cyber/sectoral:** Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 on Countering Rumours and Cybercrimes; Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on ICT in Health Fields; CBUAE Operational Risk Management Regulation C 1/2026; UAE Information Assurance (IA) Standard; Dubai Information Security Regulation (ISR) |
| Regulators | PDPL: UAE Data Office (Federal Decree-Law No. 44 of 2021), being absorbed into a Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data announced June 2026; DIFC Commissioner of Data Protection; ADGM Office/Commissioner of Data Protection; Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE); UAE Cyber Security Council (successor to NESA, the National Electronic Security Authority created by Federal Decree-Law No. 3 of 2012); Dubai Electronic Security Center (DESC); TDRA / aeCERT for telecoms and national incident coordination |
| Status | PDPL in force 2 January 2022 but its Executive Regulations were **still not issued as of June 2026**, so its detailed breach clock, penalties and DPO thresholds remain undefined and enforcement has not started; DIFC and ADGM regimes fully operative and actively enforced; CBUAE C 1/2026 effective 14 September 2026 |
| Who is covered | PDPL: controllers/processors in the UAE, and foreign ones processing data of UAE data subjects — but **not** free-zone entities with their own DP law, government entities, or health and banking/credit data governed by their own laws. DIFC/ADGM: entities incorporated or established in the respective zone (DIFC also reaches non-DIFC entities targeting or monitoring DIFC data subjects since 2025) |
| Breach clocks | PDPL: "immediately" on awareness, within a period the Executive Regulations will set (unset). DIFC Art. 41: "as soon as practicable". ADGM s.32: without undue delay and where feasible within **72 hours**. CBUAE C 1/2026 Art. 15: **4 hours** for events hitting Critical Operations, 24-hour summary report, 72 hours for high-risk incidents |
| Penalties | PDPL: administrative penalties to be set by Cabinet decision (Art. 26) — none issued. DIFC: Schedule 2 fixed maxima (typically USD 25,000–100,000 per contravention) plus an uncapped "general fine" (Art. 62(3)) and, since July 2025, a private right of action (Art. 64A). ADGM: fines up to **USD 28 million** (s.55). Cybercrime law: criminal fines up to AED 3,000,000 plus imprisonment |
| Data localisation | Health data generated in the UAE may not be stored/processed/transferred abroad without an authorised exception (Federal Law 2/2019 Art. 13); CBUAE-licensed institutions must keep their Master System of Record in the UAE (C 1/2026 Art. 8.9) |
| Assessment model | No certification. DIFC and ADGM run registration, annual assessment/fee and Commissioner supervision; CBUAE supervises via reporting, independent penetration testing and non-objection for critical outsourcing; UAE IA Standard and DESC ISR compliance is assessed against sector regulator/CSC and DESC expectations |
| Neighbours | GDPR-style architecture in DIFC/ADGM; PDPL modelled loosely on GDPR concepts but with consent-centric lawful basis and no fines yet; overlaps with DORA-type operational resilience through CBUAE C 1/2026 |

## What it is

The UAE has no single data-protection or cybersecurity code. Personal data is governed by three parallel regimes: the federal PDPL for the mainland and ordinary free zones, and two separate common-law regimes in the financial free zones — the DIFC (Dubai) and ADGM (Abu Dhabi) — each with its own law, commissioner, registration and fines. The PDPL was the first federal law drafted with private-sector technology companies and came into force on 2 January 2022, but Art. 28 required Executive Regulations within six months of promulgation and they have not appeared; as of June 2026 practitioners still describe the federal supervisory function as not fully operational. The 14 June 2026 announcement of a Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data — absorbing the AI Office, TDRA's digital-government sector and the UAE Data Office — is widely expected to unblock the regulations and enforcement, but nothing had issued as of September 2026.

Cybersecurity obligations are sectoral and layered: the cybercrime decree-law criminalises hacking, data infringement and attacks on critical facilities; the Cyber Security Council (which inherited NESA's mandate) issues the national IA Standard and national policies for critical information infrastructure; DESC imposes the ISR on Dubai government entities; CBUAE imposes operational-resilience, incident-reporting and third-party rules on licensed financial institutions; and the health ICT law localises health data. A security or GRC team operating in the UAE therefore maps obligations by (a) where the entity is incorporated, (b) which sector regulator licenses it, and (c) which data categories it processes.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Regime | Applies to | Key exclusions / limits |
|---|---|---|
| PDPL (Art. 2) | Processing, wholly or partly by automated or other means, by any controller/processor **in the UAE** processing data of data subjects inside or outside the UAE, and any controller/processor **outside the UAE** processing data of subjects inside the UAE | Government data and government authorities; data held by security and judicial authorities; purely personal processing; health data subject to its own legislation; banking and credit data subject to its own legislation; free-zone companies subject to special DP legislation (DIFC, ADGM). Art. 3 lets the Office exempt establishments that do not process "a large amount" of data — thresholds to be set by the Executive Regulations |
| DIFC DP Law (Art. 6) | Controllers/processors **incorporated in the DIFC** wherever processing occurs; any entity processing personal data **in the DIFC as part of stable arrangements**; and — since the 2025 amendment — any entity, wherever incorporated, **offering goods or services to, or monitoring the behaviour of, data subjects in the DIFC** | Purely personal or household activity; limited carve-outs for DIFC Bodies (Art. 65) |
| ADGM DPR 2021 (s.3) | Processing in the context of activities of an **establishment of a controller or processor in ADGM**, wherever processing occurs; processors for non-ADGM controllers comply "to the extent possible" | Applies to natural persons of any nationality or residence |
| Cybercrime Decree-Law 34/2021 | Any person; aggravated penalties for attacks on government entities and **critical facilities**, and for personal data relating to health, medical records, bank accounts or e-payments | Criminal statute — enforced by Public Prosecution, not a data regulator |
| Health ICT Law 2/2019 | All ICT use in health fields, onshore and in free zones | Exceptions to localisation only via decisions of a health authority in coordination with the Ministry (e.g., Ministerial Resolution 51/2021) |
| CBUAE C 1/2026 | All Licensed Financial Institutions (LFIs) that are juridical persons — banks, (re)insurers and other CBUAE licensees, not banks only | Foreign branches may keep a UAE copy of the Master System of Record with CBUAE approval |
| UAE IA Standard / national policies | Federal government entities and non-government operators designated as critical information infrastructure (CII); CBUAE requires payment service providers to meet the IA Standards as a minimum (Retail Payment Services Regulation C 15/2021 Art. 13) | CII designation is communicated by the sector regulator or CSC — confirm status rather than assume |
| DESC ISR | All Dubai Government Entities, their contractors and consultants; DESC IoT Security Standard is mandatory for Dubai government and semi-government entities | Private-sector suppliers typically inherit ISR requirements through contract |

## Core obligations

### Privacy regimes compared

| Obligation | PDPL (federal) | DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020 | ADGM DPR 2021 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lawful basis | Consent is the default (Art. 4); Art. 4 lists the cases where processing is lawful without consent (including processing necessary to protect the public interest — verify the full list against the official text) | GDPR-style lawful bases; DPO, DPIA and accountability programme under Part 2 | GDPR-style principles (s.4) and lawful bases (s.5) |
| Security of processing | Art. 20: technical and organisational measures per "best international standards", including encryption/pseudonymisation, CIA and resilience of systems, timely restoration, regular testing | Art. 14(2): security appropriate to risk, "prevailing information security good industry practice", data protection by design and by default (Art. 14(3)); Schedule 2 maximum USD 50,000 | s.30: mirrors GDPR Art. 32 — pseudonymisation/encryption, CIA and resilience, restoration, regular testing |
| Breach to regulator | Art. 9: "immediately upon becoming aware" report breach **and investigation results** to the Office within a period the Executive Regulations will set; content: nature/causes/approximate numbers, DPO details, expected effects, measures taken, documentation | Art. 41(1): "as soon as practicable in the circumstances" where the breach compromises confidentiality, security or privacy; content mirrors GDPR Art. 33(3); phased reporting allowed (41(5)); written breach log (41(6)); Schedule 2 maximum USD 50,000 | s.32: without undue delay and where feasible **within 72 hours** unless unlikely to result in risk; reasons required for late notification; phased reporting; breach documentation (s.32(5)); filed via the Online Registry Solution |
| Processor to controller | "Immediately" (Art. 9(3)) | Without undue delay (Art. 41(2)) | Without undue delay (s.32(2)) |
| Breach to data subjects | Required whenever the breach would prejudice privacy, confidentiality or security — timing per Executive Regulations | Art. 42: as soon as practicable where high risk; "promptly" if immediate risk of damage; public communication where disproportionate effort | s.33: without undue delay where high risk; encryption/mitigation/disproportionate-effort exceptions |
| DPO | Art. 10: mandatory where processing creates high risk via new technologies or data volume, involves systematic evaluation of sensitive data (profiling/automated processing), or large amounts of sensitive data; may sit outside the UAE; contact details notified to the Office | Art. 16: mandatory for DIFC Bodies and for **High Risk Processing Activities on a systematic or regular basis**; Commissioner may require one; DPO must reside in the UAE unless a group DPO; failure to appoint: USD 50,000 | s.35: mandatory for public authorities, large-scale regular and systematic monitoring, or large-scale special-category processing |
| DPIA | Art. 21: before using modern technologies posing high risk; mandatory for automated evaluation with legal/serious effects and large-scale sensitive data; minimum content prescribed | Art. 20: before High Risk Processing Activities; group DPIAs may be relied on; maximum fine raised from USD 20,000 to USD 50,000 in 2025 | s.34: before processing likely to result in high risk; DPO advice required |
| Cross-border transfer | Art. 22: to countries with adequate DP legislation or treaty partners, as approved by the Office; Art. 23: otherwise by contract binding the recipient to PDPL-equivalent controls, explicit consent, litigation, contract necessity, judicial cooperation or public interest — controls to be set by Executive Regulations | Art. 26: Commissioner's adequacy determinations (published list; the EU/EEA and UK are on it — verify current entries); Art. 27: appropriate safeguards otherwise. Note the relationship is one-way: no EU adequacy decision covers the DIFC (verify) | Part V mirrors GDPR Chapter V (adequacy, safeguards, derogations) |
| Records / registration | Art. 7: controller must keep a special record of personal data (controller and DPO details, processing operations) and produce it to the Office on request | Registration with the Commissioner (Art. 14(7)), records (Art. 15), annual assessment (Art. 19; USD 25,000 fine) | Records (s.28), Data Protection Fee and renewal (s.24, s.56) |
| Public-authority requests | — | Art. 28 (2025 rewrite): assess validity and proportionality, minimise data, seek written assurances; fine raised to USD 50,000 | — |

### Cybersecurity and sectoral obligations

| Instrument | Core requirements |
|---|---|
| Cybercrime Decree-Law 34/2021 (in force 2 January 2022; repeals 5/2012) | Art. 2 hacking: fine AED 100,000–300,000, rising to AED 150,000–500,000 where data is deleted, disclosed or altered; Art. 3 hacking government systems: temporary imprisonment and AED 200,000–500,000, up to AED 1,500,000 and at least 5–7 years where data is damaged or acquired; Art. 4 wilful harm to information systems: AED 500,000–3,000,000, aggravated for banking, media, health or scientific targets; Art. 5 harm to government or **critical facility** systems: temporary imprisonment and AED 500,000–3,000,000, cyberattack an aggravating circumstance; Art. 6 unauthorised acquisition, leak or disclosure of personal electronic data: at least 6 months and/or AED 20,000–100,000, aggravated for health, medical-record, bank-account or e-payment data. Insider misuse and negligent exposure of data are therefore criminal risks for the organisation's staff and vendors, not only regulatory ones |
| Health ICT Law 2/2019 + implementing Cabinet Decision 32/2020 (verify) + Ministerial Resolution 51/2021 | Art. 13: health information relating to services provided in the UAE may not be stored, processed, generated or transferred outside the UAE except in cases defined by a health-authority decision in coordination with the Ministry; Ministerial Resolution 51/2021 defines permitted exception cases. Reported fine for Art. 13 violation: AED 500,000–700,000 plus licence suspension (secondary source — verify). Cloud, SaaS and analytics hosting for any health-sector customer must be UAE-resident by default |
| CBUAE Operational Risk Management Regulation C 1/2026 (effective 14 September 2026; repeals Circular 163/2018) | Board-approved operational risk appetite and tolerance for disruption; mapping of Critical Operations and their people/technology/data/third-party dependencies (Art. 3.4); Art. 7.13–7.14 periodic stress and penetration testing, with independent third-party penetration tests for Critical Functions reported to the Board; Art. 8 ICT and cybersecurity risk framework including patching, obsolescence and change management; Art. 8.9 Master System of Record maintained in the UAE; Art. 9 incident response and recovery plans; Art. 11 BCP/DR tested against severe-but-plausible scenarios; Art. 13 third-party risk — due diligence before contracting, CBUAE non-objection before outsourcing anything that could significantly impact Critical Operations (13.5), mandatory contract terms including CBUAE access and subcontractor flow-down (13.6–13.8), exit plans (13.11), and CBUAE power to order discontinuation (13.12); Art. 15 notifications (below); Art. 17 supervisory and financial sanctions |
| CBUAE Retail Payment Services and Card Schemes Regulation C 15/2021, Art. 13 | Payment service providers must "apply and meet at a minimum the UAE Information Assurance Standards", run a technology and cyber risk framework with Board ownership, independent technology audit, secure SDLC and API safeguards, and privileged-access controls |
| UAE IA Standard (Cyber Security Council) | National control baseline for government and CII entities, structured in management and technical control families with P1–P4 priorities; version 2 (2025) is reported to restructure the original 188 controls into 15 families, 134 controls and 449 sub-controls and to map to national policies on cloud, IoT, AI, third-party and remote-work security (secondary sources only — the CSC publication was not accessible at time of writing; verify counts and mandatory-compliance scope with CSC) |
| DESC Information Security Regulation (ISR) | Minimum information security controls for all Dubai Government Entities across 13 domains grouped into Governance, Operation and Assurance classes; entities must run an applicability review and risk-based implementation; issued under Resolution No. 13 of 2012 and maintained by DESC under Dubai Law No. 11 of 2014; companion DESC IoT Security Standard is mandatory for Dubai government and semi-government entities; ISR version 3 is the current edition (verify) |
| TDRA / aeCERT | National CERT receives cyber-incident reports and provides incident-response investigation and IoC analysis to entities; TDRA also runs the Internet Access Management policy for takedown of phishing, impersonation and privacy-invading content |

### Incident and breach clock summary

| Trigger | Recipient | Deadline | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operational risk event significantly impacting Critical Operations (LFI) | CBUAE | **4 hours** initial notice naming affected Critical Operations; **24 hours** summary report; notice on return to normal | C 1/2026 Art. 15.2 |
| High-risk incident per Board-approved criteria (LFI) | CBUAE | **72 hours** | C 1/2026 Art. 15.3 |
| Personal data breach (ADGM controller) | ADGM Commissioner of Data Protection | Without undue delay, **≤ 72 hours** where feasible; reasons if later | DPR 2021 s.32 |
| Personal data breach (DIFC controller) | DIFC Commissioner of Data Protection | "As soon as practicable in the circumstances" | DP Law Art. 41 |
| Personal data breach (mainland controller) | UAE Data Office / successor authority | "Immediately" on awareness; period and procedure await Executive Regulations | PDPL Art. 9 |
| Data subjects (all three regimes) | Affected individuals | DIFC/ADGM: high-risk breaches, as soon as practicable / without undue delay; PDPL: where privacy, confidentiality or security prejudiced | Art. 42 / s.33 / Art. 9 |

## Enforcement and penalties

| Regime | Mechanism | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| PDPL | Art. 26: Cabinet decision, on the Data Office's proposal, will list violations and administrative penalties; Art. 24–25 complaints and grievances to the Office | **No penalty schedule issued as of September 2026**; no public enforcement |
| DIFC | Art. 62(2): administrative fines per Schedule 2 (e.g., security measures USD 50,000; DPO USD 50,000; DPIA USD 50,000; breach reporting Arts. 41/42 USD 50,000 each; annual assessment USD 25,000; Arts. 33–38 individual rights USD 100,000 each); Art. 62(3): additional **general fine not limited to Schedule 2 amounts**, proportionate to seriousness and harm; Art. 62(8) Commissioner may seek compensation for data subjects; Art. 63 appeal to DIFC Courts within 30 days; Art. 64 compensation; **Art. 64A private right of action** (in force 15 July 2025) for financial and non-financial damage including distress, with processor liability limited to processor-specific breaches or acting outside instructions | Uncapped general fine; Schedule 2 maxima USD 25,000–100,000 |
| ADGM | s.55: Penalty Notice preceded by a Notice of Intent with at least 21 days for representations; factors mirror GDPR Art. 83(2); s.57 complaints; s.58 court applications; s.59 rights against controllers/processors | **USD 28 million** per contravention and in aggregate for linked processing |
| Cybercrime law | Criminal prosecution by Public Prosecution | Up to AED 3,000,000 and temporary imprisonment for attacks on critical facilities or information systems |
| CBUAE | Art. 17: supervisory action and administrative and financial sanctions; press reporting of the 2026 regulation notes powers extending to removal or restriction of senior management and board members (secondary — verify against the Central Bank Law) | Not capped in the regulation |
| Health ICT law | Fines and suspension of access to the central health information exchange (secondary — verify) | AED 500,000–700,000 for Art. 13 (verify) |

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Map entity-by-entity applicability**: incorporation (mainland vs DIFC vs ADGM), licensing regulator (CBUAE, health authority, TDRA) and data categories decide which regime's breach clock and fines apply — a group can be under all three privacy regimes at once. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Build a multi-clock incident playbook**: 4h/24h/72h to CBUAE, 72h to ADGM, "as soon as practicable" to DIFC, "immediately" to the federal authority, plus aeCERT and Public Prosecution where a crime is suspected; pre-map filing routes (ADGM Online Registry Solution; DIFC Commissioner contact and form). See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md).
3. **Register, appoint and assess in the free zones**: DIFC registration and annual assessment, ADGM data protection fee, DPO appointment where triggered (UAE-resident in DIFC), and DPIAs before high-risk processing — each carries a fixed fine. See [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/dpia-template.md](../../templates/dpia-template.md).
4. **Enforce data residency**: health data stays in the UAE absent an approved exception; CBUAE Master System of Record in the UAE; PDPL Art. 22–23 transfer mechanisms and DIFC/ADGM adequacy lists for everything else. Reflect this in cloud region selection and vendor contracts.
5. **Run CBUAE-grade third-party risk**: due diligence before contracting, non-objection for critical outsourcing, contract clauses for regulator access and subcontractor flow-down, exit plans, dependency mapping. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md](../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md).
6. **Test, not just document**: independent penetration tests for Critical Functions with Board-reported results (CBUAE), BCP/DR exercises under severe-but-plausible scenarios, and security-measure testing required by PDPL Art. 20, DIFC Art. 14 and ADGM s.30. See [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
7. **Baseline against the UAE IA Standard or DESC ISR** where designated CII, a payment provider, or a Dubai government supplier; map to ISO/IEC 27001 and NIST CSF to avoid duplicate control sets. See [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md), [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md) and [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md).
8. **Track the PDPL Executive Regulations** and the new federal authority as a horizon item with a six-month regularisation plan ready. See [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md).
9. **Treat insider and vendor data misuse as criminal exposure** under Decree-Law 34/2021 Art. 6 — reflect it in acceptable-use policy, sanctions and contractor terms. See [../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md](../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR**: DIFC and ADGM texts track GDPR closely (lawful bases, DPIA, DPO, 72-hour ADGM clock, adequacy/safeguards transfer model), so GDPR programmes port with light adaptation; the PDPL diverges (consent-first, no fine schedule, regulator-approved transfers). EU-based groups must still apply GDPR Chapter V to exports to the UAE, since neither the mainland nor the free zones hold an EU adequacy decision (verify). See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md) and [eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md](eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md).
- **DORA / operational resilience**: CBUAE C 1/2026 is the UAE counterpart to DORA and the UK operational-resilience regime — critical-operations mapping, tolerance for disruption, third-party non-objection and exit plans, independent penetration testing, 4-hour reporting. Groups regulated in both can share the impact-tolerance and register artefacts. See [dora.md](dora.md) and [uk-financial-operational-resilience.md](uk-financial-operational-resilience.md).
- **NIS2 / critical infrastructure**: the UAE IA Standard and cybercrime Art. 5 "critical facility" concept play the role NIS2 plays for essential entities, but designation is regulator-driven rather than self-assessed against published thresholds. See [nis2.md](nis2.md).
- **HIPAA**: the UAE health ICT law is a localisation statute rather than a safeguards rule; a HIPAA-style control set satisfies little of Art. 13 unless hosting is UAE-resident. See [hipaa.md](hipaa.md).
- **Sector overlaps**: a CBUAE-licensed bank in the DIFC faces DIFC breach reporting **and** CBUAE Art. 15 notification for the same event; PDPL Art. 2 carve-outs for banking/credit and health data do not remove sectoral security duties. Related regional packs: [singapore-pdpa-cybersecurity.md](singapore-pdpa-cybersecurity.md), [india-dpdp-cert-in.md](india-dpdp-cert-in.md), [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md).
- **Frameworks**: ISO/IEC 27001 and the IA Standard are the usual evidence base for PDPL Art. 20 "best international standards" and DIFC Art. 14 "good industry practice"; see [../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md).
