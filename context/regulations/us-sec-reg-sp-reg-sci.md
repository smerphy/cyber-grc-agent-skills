# US SEC cybersecurity rules for regulated entities: Regulation S-P, Regulation S-ID, Regulation SCI (17 CFR 248 and 242.1000–1007)

## At a glance

| Instruments | **Regulation S-P** — 17 CFR Part 248 Subpart A (§§ 248.1–248.30; the safeguards, incident-response, disposal rules sit in § 248.30). **Regulation S-ID** — 17 CFR Part 248 Subpart C (§§ 248.201–248.202 plus Appendix A guidelines). **Regulation SCI** — 17 CFR §§ 242.1000–242.1007 (Form SCI at § 249.1900). **FINRA Rule 4370** — business continuity plans for FINRA member broker-dealers |

## What it is

The SEC regulates the security of customer data and market infrastructure through three sectoral rules rather than one cybersecurity regulation. **Regulation S-P** (2000) implements the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act for SEC-registered firms: privacy notices, opt-out rights, and a "safeguards rule" requiring written policies to protect customer records. The **2024 amendments** converted the safeguards rule into an operational incident-response regime with a federal minimum standard for breach notification to individuals, oversight of service providers, expanded data scope (information received from other financial institutions), extension to transfer agents, and recordkeeping. **Regulation S-ID** (2013, jointly with the CFTC under Dodd-Frank amendments to the Fair Credit Reporting Act) requires an identity theft "red flags" program. **Regulation SCI** (2014) imposes systems-resilience, testing, change-management and event-reporting duties on the technology backbone of the securities markets — exchanges, clearing agencies, large ATSs, plan processors.

A fourth strand — direct cybersecurity risk-management rules for advisers, funds, broker-dealers and other registrants (proposed 2022–2023) — did not survive: the Commission withdrew those proposals in June 2025 and stated it would issue new proposals if it decides to act. Until then, SEC cyber expectations for advisers and broker-dealers rest on Reg S-P, Reg S-ID, the Advisers Act/Exchange Act compliance-program and books-and-records rules, FINRA rules, and examination priorities.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Rule | Applicability test | Notes and exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| Reg S-P privacy provisions (§ 248.1(b)) | Brokers, dealers, investment companies, and SEC-registered investment advisers, including SEC-registered foreign (non-resident) firms | Applies only to nonpublic personal information about individuals obtaining products primarily for personal, family or household purposes; unregistered foreign firms excluded. Does not override HIPAA for health information |
| Reg S-P § 248.30 (safeguards, incident response, disposal) | "Covered institution" (§ 248.30(d)(3)): any broker or dealer, any investment company, any SEC-registered investment adviser, and any transfer agent registered with the SEC or another appropriate regulatory agency | Funding portals comply "as they apply to brokers" (Regulation Crowdfunding). Notice-registered broker-dealers exempt from the disposal rule. "Customer information" now covers records about customers of *other* financial institutions provided to the firm; for transfer agents, natural-person securityholders of issuers served |
| Reg S-ID (§ 248.201(a)) | Financial institutions or creditors (FCRA sense) that are brokers, dealers or other persons registered or required to be registered under the Exchange Act; investment companies registered or required to be registered (incl. BDCs and employees' securities companies); or investment advisers registered or required to be registered | Program required only if the firm offers or maintains "covered accounts" (consumer accounts permitting multiple payments/transactions, e.g. brokerage or mutual fund accounts with wire capability, or any account with reasonably foreseeable identity-theft risk). § 248.202 applies to card issuers only |
| Reg SCI (§ 242.1000) | SCI SROs; SCI ATSs meeting NMS-stock thresholds (5% in any single stock and 0.25% overall, or 1% overall, of average daily dollar volume in 4 of the preceding 6 months; 5% for non-NMS equities); plan processors; exempt clearing agencies subject to ARP; SCI competing consolidators (5% of consolidated market-data revenue) | New SCI ATSs/competing consolidators get six months after first meeting a threshold. Notice-registered exchanges and limited-purpose national securities associations excluded. Systems scope: "SCI systems" (trading, clearance and settlement, order routing, market data, market regulation, market surveillance) and "indirect SCI systems" (security-standard duties only) |
| FINRA Rule 4370 | All FINRA member firms | Elements may be tailored to size; non-applicable categories must be documented with rationale |

## Core obligations

### Regulation S-P § 248.30 — safeguards rule, incident response and customer notification

| Provision | Requirement |
|---|---|
| § 248.30(a)(1)–(2) Safeguards | Written policies and procedures covering administrative, technical and physical safeguards, reasonably designed to ensure security and confidentiality, protect against anticipated threats, and protect against unauthorized access or use that could cause substantial harm or inconvenience |
| § 248.30(a)(3) Response program | Program reasonably designed to **detect, respond to, and recover from** unauthorized access to or use of customer information: assess nature and scope and identify affected systems/data types; contain and control; notify affected individuals (below) |
| § 248.30(a)(4) Customer notice — trigger | Notice required for each individual whose **sensitive customer information** was, or is reasonably likely to have been, accessed or used without authorization — at the firm *or at a service provider that is not itself a covered institution* — unless a reasonable investigation determines the information has not been and is not reasonably likely to be used in a manner causing substantial harm or inconvenience. If specific individuals cannot be identified, notify everyone whose sensitive information resides in the affected system |
| § 248.30(a)(4)(iii) Timing | "As soon as practicable, but not later than **30 days**, after becoming aware" that unauthorized access or use has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred. Delay only on written US Attorney General determination of substantial risk to national security or public safety: up to 30 days, extendable a further 30 days, and in extraordinary circumstances a final 60 days; beyond that only by SEC exemptive order |
| § 248.30(a)(4)(iv) Notice content | General description of incident and data types; date/estimated date/range if determinable; contact details (phone, email, postal address, named office); advice to review statements and report suspicious activity; fraud-alert explanation; credit-report recommendations incl. free reports; FTC and usa.gov identity-theft guidance and reporting pointers. Must be clear and conspicuous, delivered by means reasonably expected to give actual written notice |
| § 248.30(a)(5) Service providers | Written policies for oversight — due diligence and monitoring — of service providers (any person with access to customer information via services to the firm), reasonably designed to ensure providers protect customer information and **notify the firm as soon as possible but no later than 72 hours** after becoming aware of a breach resulting in unauthorized access to a customer information system they maintain. Receipt of that notice must trigger the firm's response program. A provider may be contracted to send customer notices, but the notification obligation stays with the covered institution |
| § 248.30(b) Disposal | Reasonable measures against unauthorized access in disposal of consumer information and customer information; written disposal policies (not applicable to notice-registered broker-dealers) |
| § 248.30(c) and books-and-records rules | Records of policies, detected incidents and responses, investigations and notification determinations (including AG delay documentation and copies of notices), service-provider policies and contracts, disposal policies. Retention set by the entity's existing rules (amendments to Rules 17a-4, 17ad-7, 31a-1 and 204-2); unregistered investment companies keep records six years, first two easily accessible. Funding portals are outside the enumerated recordkeeping amendments but keep records under Regulation Crowdfunding Rule 404 |
| § 248.30(d)(9) Sensitive customer information | Any component of customer information whose compromise could create a reasonably likely risk of substantial harm or inconvenience — e.g. SSN, driver's licence/passport/taxpayer numbers, biometric records, unique electronic identifiers; or account identifiers combined with authenticating data (access codes, security questions, DOB, mother's maiden name, partial SSN, card expiry) |

The pre-existing privacy provisions (initial and annual privacy notices, opt-out rights, limits on redisclosure and account-number sharing, §§ 248.4–248.15) remain in place; the 2024 amendments also conformed the annual-notice provisions to the FAST Act exception (no annual notice if sharing practices are unchanged and only exception-based sharing occurs).

### Regulation S-ID §§ 248.201–248.202 — identity theft red flags

| Provision | Requirement |
|---|---|
| § 248.201(c) | Periodically determine whether the firm offers or maintains covered accounts, via a risk assessment considering account-opening methods, access methods and prior identity-theft experience |
| § 248.201(d) | Written **Identity Theft Prevention Program** appropriate to size and complexity, with policies to identify relevant Red Flags, detect them, respond appropriately, and update the Program periodically |
| § 248.201(e) | Initial Program approved by the board (or a board committee); board, committee or senior-management designee involved in oversight; staff training; "appropriate and effective oversight of service provider arrangements" |
| § 248.201(f) and Appendix A | Consider the Interagency Guidelines: five Red Flag categories (alerts from consumer reporting agencies/fraud services; suspicious documents; suspicious identifying information; unusual account activity; notices from customers, victims or law enforcement); detection via customer identification program controls and authentication; graduated responses (monitoring, contacting the customer, changing credentials, closing/reopening accounts, notifying law enforcement); staff report to the board, committee or senior-management designee **at least annually** on Program effectiveness, service-provider arrangements, significant incidents and recommended changes |
| § 248.202 | Card issuers must validate address changes before issuing an additional or replacement card requested within at least 30 days of an address-change notice |

Appendix A notes that a data security incident causing unauthorized access to customer account records is an aggravating factor in choosing responses — the practical bridge between a Reg S-P incident and Reg S-ID red-flag handling.

### Regulation SCI §§ 242.1000–242.1007 — market infrastructure resilience

| Provision | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Rule 1001(a) Capacity, integrity, resiliency, availability, security | Written policies covering capacity planning; periodic stress tests; systems development and testing methodology; regular vulnerability reviews and testing incl. backups; **BC/DR plans geographically diverse and designed for next-business-day resumption of trading and two-hour resumption of critical SCI systems** after a wide-scale disruption; market-data standards; monitoring for SCI events. Deemed reasonably designed if consistent with "current SCI industry standards" (widely available financial-sector IT practices from a US governmental body or widely recognized organization) — but that is not the exclusive route |
| Rule 1001(b) Systems compliance | Policies so systems operate in compliance with the Exchange Act and the entity's own rules: pre-implementation testing of all changes, change-control internal controls, functionality assessments, regulatory/technology coordination; individual safe harbor for personnel who reasonably discharge assigned duties |
| Rule 1001(c) Responsible SCI personnel | Criteria for designating senior managers responsible for each system and escalation procedures to inform them quickly of potential SCI events |
| Rule 1002(a)–(b) SCI events (systems disruption, systems compliance issue, systems intrusion) | Begin corrective action immediately; **notify the SEC immediately** when responsible personnel have a reasonable basis to conclude an event occurred; **written notification within 24 hours** (systems affected, participant impact, market impact, steps taken, resolution timing); regular updates; **final written report within five business days** of resolution and closed investigation, or an **interim report at 30 calendar days** if unresolved, then a final report; includes loss analysis. De minimis events are exempt from individual notification but must be recorded and summarized in a **quarterly report** (within 30 calendar days of quarter-end) covering disruptions and intrusions |
| Rule 1002(c) Dissemination | Promptly inform affected members/participants of disruptions and compliance issues (all members for major SCI events); intrusions disseminated in summary unless doing so would compromise security or an investigation (documented). Market regulation/surveillance systems and de minimis events exempt |
| Rule 1003(a) Systems changes | Quarterly report (within 30 calendar days of quarter-end) of completed, ongoing and planned material changes to SCI systems and security of indirect SCI systems, under written materiality criteria |
| Rule 1003(b) SCI review | Annual review by objective, experienced personnel: risk assessment plus assessment of internal control design and effectiveness (logical/physical security, development processes, IT governance). **Penetration testing of network, firewalls and production systems at least every three years**; market regulation/surveillance systems assessed on a risk basis, at least every three years. Report to senior management within 30 days of completion, and to the SEC and the board within 60 days thereafter |
| Rule 1004 BC/DR testing | Designate members/participants necessary for fair and orderly markets and require their participation in functional and performance testing **at least every 12 months**; coordinate industry-wide testing with other SCI entities |
| Rules 1005–1007 | Records kept per Rule 17a-1 (SROs) or for **at least five years**, first two readily accessible (other SCI entities); filings on Form SCI via electronic signature; written undertakings from service bureaus holding records |

### FINRA Rule 4370 — business continuity (member broker-dealers)

Written BCP reasonably designed to meet obligations to customers and counterparties; updated on material change and reviewed annually; ten minimum elements (data backup and recovery, mission-critical systems, financial/operational assessments, alternate customer and employee communications, alternate location, critical constituent/bank/counterparty impact, regulatory reporting, regulator communications, customer access to funds and securities); approval and annual review by a senior-management registered principal; customer disclosure at account opening and on the website; two emergency contact persons reported to FINRA and updated promptly.

## Enforcement and penalties

- **SEC**: examination findings (Division of Examinations) escalate to Enforcement actions under the Exchange Act, Advisers Act and Investment Company Act. Remedies include cease-and-desist orders, censures, civil money penalties, disgorgement and compliance undertakings; penalty ceilings are statutory tiers (Exchange Act § 21B, Advisers Act § 203(i), Investment Company Act § 9(d)) adjusted annually for inflation under 17 CFR 201.1001(b), with the adjusted amounts published in the Federal Register on or before 15 January each year and posted on the SEC website — cite the current table, not a remembered figure. Examination scrutiny of Reg S-P safeguards predates the amendments (the adopting release lists a 16 April 2019 staff risk alert on adviser and broker-dealer Reg S-P privacy-notice and safeguards deficiencies), so the amended rule lands on an established examination practice.
- **FINRA**: Rule 4370 requires the BCP to be made available promptly to FINRA staff on request; deficiencies are handled through FINRA's examination and disciplinary process.
- **Reg SCI** events are reported directly to SEC staff via Form SCI; a pattern of unreported or misclassified events is an examination and enforcement exposure in itself.
- **Reg S-ID**: the rule's own evidence trail — board approval of the initial Program, oversight records, staff training, service-provider oversight and the at-least-annual compliance report to the board (Appendix A, section VI) — is what examiners test.

Nothing on the SEC's August 2026 agenda signals a re-proposal of adviser/broker-dealer cyber rules or of the Reg SCI expansion; keep both on horizon-scanning watchlists, since a withdrawn proposal can return only as a new proposal.

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Confirm which rules apply and the size tier** — covered institution under § 248.30 (including transfer agents and funding portals), covered accounts under Reg S-ID, SCI-entity status or thresholds under § 242.1000; document the analysis. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Build the § 248.30(a)(3) response program into the IR plan**: assessment, containment, the "sensitive customer information" trigger, the substantial-harm determination, the 30-day customer clock, AG-delay path, and the record of every determination. See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md).
3. **Pre-draft the customer notice** with all § 248.30(a)(4)(iv) content elements, and reconcile it with state breach-notification templates so one incident produces one consistent notice set ([../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md)).
4. **Remediate service-provider oversight**: inventory every provider with access to customer information, run due diligence and monitoring, and secure the 72-hour breach-notice commitment (contractually or otherwise) plus, where used, a written agreement for the provider to notify customers on your behalf. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
5. **Refresh the Reg S-ID Program**: re-run the covered-account risk assessment, update Red Flags for current fraud patterns (account takeover, SIM swap, synthetic identity), obtain board approval evidence, and report Program effectiveness to the board.
6. **Maintain the evidence set**: safeguards and disposal policies, incident logs, investigation/determination memos, provider contracts and diligence files, retained per Rules 17a-4 / 17ad-7 / 31a-1 / 204-2. See [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/audit-evidence-request-list.md](../../templates/audit-evidence-request-list.md).
7. **SCI entities**: operate the Rule 1002 event pipeline (immediate call, 24-hour Form SCI, updates, five-day final/30-day interim, quarterly de minimis summaries), the quarterly material-change report, the annual SCI review with three-year penetration-test cycle, and annual member BC/DR testing; keep board and senior-management reporting dated. See [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
8. **Broker-dealers**: keep the FINRA 4370 BCP current, annually reviewed and principal-approved, with the customer disclosure and emergency contacts on file.
9. **Map the whole set to the control framework** (Reg S-P safeguards, S-ID, SCI Rule 1001 and 4370 all decompose cleanly into NIST CSF 2.0 or ISO 27001 controls) so one control-testing program produces evidence for every rule. See [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md) and [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md).
10. **Track rulemaking**: withdrawn proposals can return in narrower form; log SEC agenda items and FINRA notices in horizon scanning. See [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **SEC public-company disclosure (Form 8-K Item 1.05, Reg S-K Item 106)**: an incident at a listed broker-dealer or adviser parent can run a Reg S-P 30-day customer clock and a four-business-day 8-K clock from the materiality determination in parallel — different triggers, different audiences. See [sec-cyber-disclosure.md](sec-cyber-disclosure.md).
- **GLBA and the FTC Safeguards Rule**: Reg S-P is the SEC's GLBA rule; the FTC rule governs non-bank, non-SEC financial institutions and carries its own FTC breach-notification duty. Groups with both SEC-registered and FTC-regulated affiliates need both programs. See [glba-ftc-safeguards.md](glba-ftc-safeguards.md).
- **State breach-notification laws**: Reg S-P sets a federal *minimum*; state laws (and NYDFS Part 500 for New York-licensed entities) still apply with their own thresholds, AG notices and timelines. See [us-state-privacy.md](us-state-privacy.md) and [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).
- **HIPAA**: § 248.1(b) expressly leaves HHS rules on individually identifiable health information untouched ([hipaa.md](hipaa.md)).
- **SOX ITGC**: SCI Rule 1001(b) change control and Reg S-P recordkeeping overlap with ITGC change-management and access evidence for listed entities ([sox-itgc.md](sox-itgc.md)).
- **EU DORA / NIS2**: US broker-dealers and asset managers with EU entities face DORA's ICT risk, incident-reporting and third-party regime in parallel; the register-of-information and contract clauses can be built to satisfy § 248.30(a)(5) provider oversight at the same time ([dora.md](dora.md), [nis2.md](nis2.md)).
- **Frameworks**: SCI's "current SCI industry standards" deeming provision points to IT practices issued by a US governmental body or widely recognized organization — NIST publications fit that definition ([../frameworks/nist-800-53.md](../frameworks/nist-800-53.md), [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md)); Reg S-P and S-ID controls map naturally to ISO 27001 Annex A and CIS Controls ([../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md), [../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md](../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md)).
