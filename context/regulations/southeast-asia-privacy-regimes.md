## What it is

Five separate national regimes with no common enforcement layer. Indonesia, Vietnam and (post-amendment) Malaysia are the newest generation: statute-level laws with GDPR vocabulary, revenue-linked fines and hard breach clocks, but with regulators still being built (Indonesia) or with a security-service regulator (Vietnam's Ministry of Public Security). Thailand's PDPA (2019) and the Philippines' DPA (2012) are older but now have active enforcement: Thailand issued its first administrative fine in 2024 and the Philippines runs mandatory registration, breach-notification and annual-report systems through the NPC.

The practical differences matter more than the shared vocabulary. Malaysia's and the Philippines' penalties are criminal, imposed on conviction and reaching responsible officers. Vietnam requires regulator-filed dossiers (processing impact and cross-border transfer) rather than internal DPIAs. Indonesia's administrative-fine regime exists on paper but cannot be exercised until the PDP institution is established. Thailand fines by data category, with sensitive-data violations carrying the highest tier.

### Indonesia — PDP Law and GR 33/2026

The two-year adjustment period in Art. 74 ran from promulgation on 17 October 2022, so the full Law has applied since 17 October 2024. Administrative sanctions (written warning, suspension of processing, erasure, fines up to 2% of annual revenue) are imposed "by the institution" (Art. 57(4)) — an authority the President must establish by Presidential Regulation (Art. 58). As of the latest available public analyses (March 2026), that regulation was still in harmonisation at the Ministry of Law and no PDP-authority-led administrative penalty had been issued; breach notifications are in practice submitted to Komdigi's Directorate General of Digital Space Supervision. GR 33/2026 (12 chapters, 225 articles) adds mechanics for consent, children's data, DPIAs, transfers (adequacy list to be compiled by the PDP institution, standard contractual clauses, exceptional transfers) and sanctions; it takes effect 16 January 2027. A separate 24-hour cyber-incident report to the national CSIRT applies to electronic system operators under BSSN Regulation 1/2024 (secondary).

### Malaysia — Act 709 as amended

Act A1727 received Royal Assent on 9 October 2024 and commenced in three phases fixed by P.U. (B) 522 (19 December 2024): 1 January 2025 (ss. 7, 11, 13, 14 — electronic service and administrative changes), 1 April 2025 (ss. 2–5, 8, 10, 12 — "data controller" terminology, biometric data as sensitive, processors bound by the Security Principle, RM 1 m / 3-year penalties, revised s. 129 transfer regime) and 1 June 2025 (ss. 6, 9 — DPO appointment, breach notification, data portability). The Commissioner's 2025 circulars on DPO appointment and data breach notification supply thresholds and the 72-hour/7-day clocks; the DBN circular also requires contractual breach-assistance duties on processors and, per secondary sources, a breach register kept for at least two years. The Cross Border Personal Data Transfer Guidelines (v1.0, 29 April 2025) introduce a transfer impact assessment whose findings remain valid for up to three years (secondary). A public consultation on amending the Personal Data Protection Regulations 2013 was issued in 2025; the amended regulations had not been located as of September 2026.

### Thailand — PDPA B.E. 2562

Published in the Government Gazette on 27 May 2019; the operative chapters took effect one year later under s. 2 but were deferred by Royal Decree and have applied fully since 1 June 2022 (secondary). Sensitive data (s. 26) attracts the highest fine tier. The PDPC's first administrative penalty (order of 31 July 2024, announced August 2024) totalled THB 7 million: THB 1 m for no DPO (ss. 41(2), 82), THB 3 m for inadequate security (ss. 37(1), 83) and THB 3 m for failure to notify the breach (ss. 37(4), 83), with follow-up directives enforceable under s. 89 (secondary).

### Vietnam — PDPL and Cybersecurity Law 2025

Law 91/2025/QH15 (5 chapters, 39 articles) was passed on 26 June 2025 and took effect on 1 January 2026, elevating Decree 13/2023 to statute; Decree 356/2025/ND-CP (31 December 2025, 42 articles) replaces Decree 13 and supplies the dossier forms, DPO conditions, cross-border procedures and sector guidance (secondary). Consents and dossiers filed under Decree 13 remain valid (Art. 39). Sector-specific rules cover children, employment, health and insurance, finance and credit, advertising, social networks, big data and AI, location and biometric data, and recording (Arts. 24–32). Law 116/2025/QH15 (adopted 10 December 2025, in force 1 July 2026) merges the 2015 Cyberinformation Security Law and the 2018 Cybersecurity Law; service providers must supply user information to the Ministry of Public Security within 24 hours (3 hours in emergencies), remove violating content within 24 hours (6 hours), retain user data and store designated data in Vietnam, and foreign providers must establish a local branch or representative office.

### Philippines — RA 10173 and NPC circulars

The NPC operationalises the Act through circulars: 16-03 (15 December 2016) on breach management (security incident management policy, breach response team, annual review, 72-hour notification, 5-day full report, annual incident summary), 2022-04 (5 December 2022) on registration (superseding 17-01; 180-day transitory period; automated decision-making and profiling systems always registrable) and 2023-06 on security of personal data (12-month transition to 30 March 2025, per secondary source). Government contractors accessing sensitive personal information of 1,000 or more individuals must register (s. 24).

## Enforcement and penalties

| Regime | Administrative | Criminal / civil |
|---|---|---|
| Indonesia | Warning, suspension, erasure, fines up to 2% of annual revenue (Art. 57) — dormant until the PDP institution exists; Komdigi and sector regulators (OJK, BSSN) enforce under electronic-systems rules | Unlawful acquisition/use: up to 5 years and/or IDR 5 bn; unlawful disclosure: 4 years / IDR 4 bn (Art. 67); falsification: 6 years / IDR 6 bn (Art. 68); corporations: fines up to 10× plus seizure, suspension or closure (Art. 70). Law No. 1 of 2026 re-based criminal fine maxima on the Criminal Code categories (secondary) |
| Malaysia | Commissioner investigation (s. 105), compounding of offences, registration revocation | Principle breaches: RM 1 m / 3 years (s. 5(2)); failure to notify breach: RM 250,000 / 2 years (s. 12B(3)) |
| Thailand | Expert committee fines: THB 1 m (s. 82, e.g. no DPO), THB 3 m (s. 83, security, breach notification, transfers), THB 5 m (s. 84, sensitive data); processors THB 1 m–5 m (ss. 85–87); THB 500,000 for ignoring committee orders (s. 89) | Sensitive-data disclosure or unlawful transfer: up to 6 months / THB 500,000, or 1 year / THB 1 m if for unlawful benefit (s. 79); compensation regardless of intent unless force majeure or official order (s. 77) |
| Vietnam | Fines up to 10× revenue from data trading, 5% of prior-year revenue for cross-border violations, VND 3 bn otherwise; individuals half (Art. 8); suspension of cross-border transfers (Art. 20(5)); inspections at most annually plus surprise inspections (Art. 20(4)) | Criminal prosecution and compensation (Art. 8(1)) |
| Philippines | NPC compliance orders, cease-and-desist, processing bans and administrative fines (secondary); registration revocation (Circular 2022-04 ss. 35–38) | Unauthorised processing, negligent access, improper disposal, unauthorised purposes, intentional breach, concealment of breach (s. 30: 1.5–5 years, PHP 500,000–1 m), malicious/unauthorised disclosure; combinations 3–6 years and PHP 1–5 m (s. 33); maximum penalty where ≥100 persons affected (s. 35); responsible officers liable (s. 34) |

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Map applicability per country** using each statute's establishment/equipment/targeting tests and the exemptions (Malaysia's commercial-transaction and government carve-outs, Vietnam's SME reliefs); record the outcome as an applicability decision. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Build a five-clock breach playbook**: Indonesia 3×24 h dual notice, Malaysia 72 h from occurrence plus 7 days to individuals, Thailand 72 h from reasonable belief, Vietnam 72 h from discovery, Philippines 72 h plus 5-day full report and annual summary. Log each in [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md); see [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md) and [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).
3. **Register where registration is mandatory**: Malaysian data controller classes and DPO notification (21 days), Philippine DPS/DPO registration (20 days for new systems, annual renewal), Vietnamese processing and transfer dossiers (60 days, six-monthly updates).
4. **Appoint and evidence DPOs** against the local thresholds (Malaysia 20,000 / 10,000; Thailand and Indonesia large-scale monitoring or sensitive data; Vietnam PDP department or contracted provider) and keep appointment, notification and training records.
5. **Run DPIAs to the strictest local trigger** — Vietnam's regulator-filed dossier and Malaysia's quantitative thresholds set the bar; reuse [../../templates/dpia-template.md](../../templates/dpia-template.md) and [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
6. **Re-paper processor contracts**: Malaysian processors now carry direct security and DPO duties and must contractually support breach notification; Thai and Vietnamese processors must notify controllers promptly; Philippine PICs must flow down s. 20 security measures. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
7. **Document transfer mechanisms per corridor**: Malaysian transfer impact assessments (three-year validity), Thai adequacy or exceptions, Indonesian adequacy-then-safeguards-then-consent hierarchy, Vietnamese transfer dossiers and localisation duties under the Cybersecurity Law.
8. **Map security controls once** to the Malaysian Personal Data Protection Standard, Thai Committee minimum standard, Philippine NPC Circular 2023-06 and Indonesian Art. 35 using [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md) and [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md).
9. **Watch the pending items** — Indonesia's PDP institution and GR 33/2026 commencement, Malaysia's amended regulations, Vietnam's sanction decrees — through [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and the [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md) workflow.

## Interplay

- **GDPR**: all five regimes borrow GDPR structure, so a GDPR programme is the natural baseline, but clocks and triggers differ (Indonesia and the Philippines notify individuals on the same clock as the regulator; Malaysia's 72 hours runs from the breach, not awareness; Vietnam's trigger is a "violation" that may harm security or the data subject, not a risk-to-rights test). See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md).
- **Cyber and sector regimes**: Vietnam's Cybersecurity Law adds localisation, 24-hour information duties and log retention on top of the PDPL; Indonesia's BSSN 24-hour cyber-incident report and OJK financial-sector rules run in parallel with the PDP Law; Philippine government agencies carry additional s. 22–24 duties. Multi-regulator incidents need a single timeline across privacy and cyber authorities — see [../../workflows/incident-regulatory-response.md](../../workflows/incident-regulatory-response.md).
- **Regional neighbours**: Singapore's PDPA and Cybersecurity Act, China's PIPL/DSL/CSL, Japan's APPI, South Korea's PIPA and India's DPDP Act each have their own packs; for smaller jurisdictions see [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md).
- **Frameworks**: ISO/IEC 27701-style privacy management maps cleanly onto the DPO, records, DPIA and breach duties above; use [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md) for the incident-response backbone and [../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md) for board reporting of multi-country notification performance.
