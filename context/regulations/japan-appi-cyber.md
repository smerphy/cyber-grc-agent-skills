# Japan: Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) and cybersecurity legislation

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | **APPI** — Act on the Protection of Personal Information (Act No. 57 of 2003), as amended by Act No. 44 of 2020 (fully in force 1 April 2022) and Act No. 37 of 2021 (public/private unification, fully in force 1 April 2023); further amended by the Act passed 10 July 2026 and promulgated 17 July 2026 (main provisions not yet in force). **Basic Act on Cybersecurity** (Act No. 104 of 2014). **Act on the Prevention of Damage from Unauthorized Acts against Important Computers** (Act No. 42 of 2025, promulgated 23 May 2025 — the "Active Cyber Defense" law; the government's own English name is "Cyber Response Capability Strengthening Act") |
| Regulators | **Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC)** — independent commission, sole APPI supervisor (with delegation to sector ministers under Art. 150). **National Cybersecurity Office (NCO, 国家サイバー統括室)** in the Cabinet Secretariat, established July 2025 as the successor to NISC; Cabinet Cyber Officer. Sector ministers for critical-infrastructure reporting; FSA for financial institutions; METI/IPA for management guidelines |
| Who is covered (APPI) | Any "business handling personal information" — a person using a personal-information database for business, with no size or revenue threshold (Art. 16(2)); extraterritorial for foreign operators supplying goods or services to persons in Japan (Art. 171); public-sector chapters cover administrative organs and local governments |
| Breach clock (APPI) | Preliminary report to the PPC "promptly" (PPC guideline yardstick: roughly 3–5 days from awareness) and final report within **30 days** (**60 days** where a malicious act is suspected) — Enforcement Rules Art. 8; individuals notified promptly (Rules Art. 10) |
| Breach triggers (APPI) | Rules Art. 7: (i) sensitive personal information; (ii) data that could cause financial harm if misused; (iii) leak caused by an act with a likely wrongful purpose (cyberattack, ransomware, insider theft); (iv) more than 1,000 data subjects — actual leak, loss or damage *or a likelihood* of it |
| Penalties (APPI, current) | Violation of a PPC order: imprisonment up to 1 year or fine up to JPY 1 million (Art. 178); corporate fine up to **JPY 100 million** for Arts. 178–179 offences (Art. 184); false reports/obstructing inspection: fine up to JPY 500,000 (Art. 182). The 2026 amendment adds an administrative **surcharge (課徴金)** regime and raises criminal penalties (see Timeline) |
| Critical-infrastructure duties (2025 Act) | "Special critical-infrastructure operators" must notify designated important computers to their competent minister and report specified infringement events promptly, with a full report within 30 days; obligations commence **1 October 2026** |
| Certifiable / assessment model | None of the instruments is certifiable. APPI compliance is self-managed under PPC guidelines; accredited personal-information protection organisations (Arts. 47–56) are voluntary. METI's Cybersecurity Management Guidelines v3.0 and the FSA's 2024 financial-sector guidelines are supervisory expectations, not certifications |
| Relationship to neighbours | Mutual EU–Japan adequacy (with PPC Supplementary Rules for EU/UK-origin data); APPI cross-border rules (Art. 28) mirror GDPR Chapter V in function but not in form; the 2025 Act plays the NIS2/CER role for critical infrastructure but is narrower and security-service-led |

## What it is

The APPI is Japan's omnibus data-protection statute. Since the 2015 reform it has been enforced by a single independent authority, the PPC, and has been rewritten on a statutory "review every three years" cycle (Supplementary Provisions Art. 10 of Act No. 44 of 2020). The 2020 amendment introduced mandatory breach reporting (Art. 26), pseudonymously processed information (Arts. 41–42), stricter cross-border transfer disclosures (Art. 28(2)–(3)), the prohibition of inappropriate use (Art. 19) and the JPY 100 million corporate fine; the 2021 amendment folded the separate public-sector and incorporated-agency statutes into one act (Chapter V) and brought local governments under PPC oversight from 1 April 2023. The third triennial review produced the amendment act passed on 10 July 2026 — the most significant change since 2020, adding an administrative surcharge, a statistics/AI-development consent exception, children's and biometric-data rules and a new offence for fraudulent acquisition.

The cybersecurity layer is separate. The **Basic Act on Cybersecurity** (2014) defines "cybersecurity" (Art. 2), requires a national Cybersecurity Strategy (Art. 12; the current Strategy was adopted by Cabinet decision on 23 December 2025 with a five-year outlook) and establishes the Cybersecurity Strategic Headquarters in the Cabinet (Art. 25), which sets standards for government bodies and for critical-infrastructure operators (Art. 26). It imposes almost no direct obligations on private companies. The **2025 Act** (Act No. 42 of 2025) changes that for a defined set of critical-infrastructure operators: it creates the first statutory cyber-incident reporting duty in Japanese law, a public–private council, government acquisition and analysis of communications information (subject to a new Cyber Communication Information Oversight Commission, Art. 46), and police/SDF "neutralisation" powers via companion legislation. Below the statutes sit two influential soft-law instruments: METI/IPA **Cybersecurity Management Guidelines v3.0** (March 2023) for boards, and the FSA **Guidelines for Cybersecurity in the Financial Sector** (4 October 2024).

## Who it covers / Scope

| Instrument | Applicability test | Notes and exemptions |
|---|---|---|
| APPI Chapter IV (private sector) | Uses a "personal information database or the equivalent" — a searchable, systematically organised collection of personal information (Art. 16(1)) — for business (Art. 16(2)) | No headcount, record-count or revenue threshold. Excludes national and local government organs and incorporated administrative agencies (covered by Chapter V). Art. 57 carves out journalistic, literary, religious and political uses for those purposes; Art. 59 requires academic research organisations to endeavour to comply and to self-regulate handling for research purposes |
| Extraterritorial reach | Art. 171: the Act applies to a foreign operator that handles personal information of a person in Japan in connection with supplying goods or services to that person | Foreign operators in scope are subject to the same PPC powers as domestic ones; the 2026 amendment also revises the service-by-publication rules (Art. 163(2)–(3), in force six months after promulgation) |
| Data categories | Personal information (Art. 2(1)); individual identification codes incl. biometric codes (Art. 2(2)); sensitive ("special care-required") information: race, creed, social status, medical history, criminal record, crime-victim status plus Cabinet Order items (Art. 2(3)); pseudonymized (Art. 2(5)); anonymized (Art. 2(6)); personal-related information such as cookies/device data that is not personal information for the holder (Art. 2(7)) | "Personal data" = personal information held in a database (Art. 16(3)); most handling duties attach to personal data, not to loose personal information |
| 2025 Act, Chapter II | "Special critical-infrastructure operators" (特別社会基盤事業者): operators already designated as specified critical-infrastructure operators under Art. 50 of the Economic Security Promotion Act (Act No. 43 of 2022) *and* using "specified important computers" (Art. 2(3)) | Cabinet Order No. 47 of 2026 (23 March 2026) and the joint ministerial ordinance No. 4 of 2026 (28 May 2026) define the computers — those connected to specified critical equipment, their routers, firewalls, credential stores and access-control servers (Ordinance Art. 1). Government organs and designated corporations are covered by other parts of the Act |
| Basic Act on Cybersecurity | Government bodies, incorporated agencies, designated corporations; "critical social infrastructure operators" through HQ-set standards (Art. 26(1)(iii)) | Private companies have a duty to endeavour (努力義務) only |
| FSA Guidelines (Oct 2024) | Major banks, regional/cooperative institutions, insurers, securities firms, crypto-asset exchanges, funds-transfer and payment operators, credit-rating agencies, clearing/settlement institutions, financial-instrument exchanges and others listed in §1.4 | Supervisory expectations, applied via the supervisory guidelines (監督指針) amended the same day; "basic items" expected of all, "desirable items" for large or systemically important firms |
| METI Guidelines v3.0 | Management of large companies and SMEs (excluding small-scale enterprises) that supply IT systems/services or depend on IT | Voluntary; widely used as the reference for board accountability and in procurement questionnaires |

## Core obligations

### APPI — handling duties (Chapter IV, Section 2)

| Article | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Arts. 17–18 | Specify the purpose of use as concretely as possible; no use beyond it without consent; purpose changes only within a reasonably related scope |
| Art. 19 | No use of personal information in a manner that may foster unlawful or unjust acts (introduced 2020) |
| Art. 20 | Proper acquisition; **prior consent to acquire sensitive personal information** save statutory exceptions |
| Art. 21 | Notify or publish the purpose of use at acquisition |
| Art. 22 | Keep data accurate and up to date; delete when no longer needed (effort duty) |
| Art. 23 | "Necessary and appropriate" security-control measures against leak, loss or damage. PPC General Guidelines (Nov 2016, last revised June 2026) expand this into basic policy, handling rules, organisational, personnel, physical and technical measures, and **understanding the external environment** — i.e. the legal regime of any foreign country where personal data is stored or processed |
| Arts. 24–25 | Supervise employees and entrusted processors; entrustment (委託) is the Japanese analogue of processor engagement — no consent needed (Art. 27(5)(i)) but supervision is mandatory |
| Art. 26 | **Breach reporting** to the PPC and notification to individuals (see clocks below); an entrusted processor discharges its own duty by notifying the entrusting operator (Rules Art. 9) |
| Art. 27 | No provision of personal data to third parties without prior consent, except statutory exceptions; **opt-out** provision requires prior PPC notification and is barred for sensitive data and for data itself received by opt-out or acquired improperly (Art. 27(2) proviso) |
| Art. 28 | **Cross-border transfers**: consent to transfer to a foreign third party, unless the destination is a PPC-designated equivalent jurisdiction (the EU and the UK) or the recipient maintains a system of "equivalent measures" — Rules Art. 16: contractual or other appropriate arrangements securing Chapter IV Section 2-level handling, or certification under an international personal-information framework. Before consent, inform the individual of the destination country's regime and the recipient's safeguards (Art. 28(2)); after transfer on the equivalent-measures basis, ensure continued compliance and answer individual requests (Art. 28(3)) |
| Arts. 29–30 | Records of third-party provision and of receipt (traceability); verify the provider's identity and acquisition history on receipt |
| Art. 31 | Personal-related information (cookies, IDs) may be provided only after confirming that the recipient has the individual's consent to acquire it as personal data |
| Arts. 32–35 | Individual rights: publication of handling details, disclosure (including of third-party provision records), correction, cessation of use/deletion, cessation of third-party provision — since 2022 also where rights may be harmed, not only for violations |
| Arts. 41–42 | Pseudonymized information: internal analytics without purpose-limitation constraints; no third-party provision; breach-reporting and rights provisions disapplied |
| Arts. 43–46 | Anonymized information: processing standards, publication of item categories, re-identification prohibited, security measures |

### APPI — breach reporting mechanics (Art. 26; Enforcement Rules Arts. 7–10; PPC General Guidelines 3-5-3)

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Any of the four Rules Art. 7 categories, including a mere *likelihood* (おそれ) of leak, loss or damage. Category (iii) covers unauthorised access, malware C2 traffic, third-party warnings of a suspected leak, ransomware encryption without exfiltration, theft of media, insider misappropriation and web-skimming. Data protected by strong encryption or equivalent measures is excluded from categories (i) and (ii) |
| Clock start | When the operator "knew" — for a corporation, when *any department* became aware (Guidelines) |
| Preliminary report (速報) | "Promptly" — PPC yardstick roughly **3–5 days** — with the nine Rules Art. 8(1) items to the extent known: overview, data items, number of individuals, cause, secondary-harm risk, response to individuals, publication status, recurrence prevention, other matters. Filed through the PPC online reporting form |
| Final report (確報) | Within **30 days** of awareness; **60 days** for category (iii) malicious cases (Rules Art. 8(2)) |
| Individuals | Notify promptly "according to the situation", with the overview, data items, cause, secondary-harm risk and other reference items (Rules Art. 10); alternative measures (public notice, helpline) allowed where notification is difficult (Art. 26(2)) |
| Ransomware and cyberattacks | From 1 October 2025 ransomware-related leaks may be reported on the government-wide **common form** (共通様式) as part of unified cyberattack-damage reporting; the PPC states it will coordinate a single window with the NCO when 2025 Act reporting commences |
| Sector delegation | Where the PPC has delegated receipt to a sector minister (Art. 150), report to that minister on the same clock (Guidelines) |

Log each step in the incident notification log ([../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md)) and cross-check parallel GDPR/other clocks in [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).

### 2025 Act — critical-infrastructure duties (Chapter II, in force 1 October 2026)

| Article / rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Art. 3 | Cabinet adopts a Basic Policy on preventing damage from specified unauthorised acts against important computers |
| Art. 4; Ordinance Arts. 2–3 | Notify each specified important computer (product name, manufacturer, equipment category, operator details) to the competent minister **within 4 months of introduction**; changes within 4 months; hardware of appliances and OS/middleware/applications of other systems are in scope; widely used products designated by the minister and PM are exempt; filings under the Economic Security Promotion Act can substitute where product/supplier names match |
| Art. 5; Ordinance Art. 4 | Report specified infringement events and precursor events — malware execution, use of another person's credentials, access-control bypass, credential theft for unauthorised access, and recorded *traces* of any of these — to the competent minister **and the Prime Minister**: an initial report **promptly** after recognition (items known at the time) and a **full report within 30 days**. Seven content items: report type, operator overview, event overview, affected computer, technical details, response, other matters. Events in the first six months after designation are excluded |
| Arts. 6–7 | Competent minister may order notification/reporting or correction; the PM may express an opinion that an order be issued |
| Art. 8 | Government must protect reported information; officials face criminal penalties for disclosure (Art. 82: up to 2 years or JPY 1 million) |
| Art. 45 | Public–private council (協議会) for information sharing, with confidentiality duties |
| Penalties | Violating an Art. 6 order: fine up to JPY 2 million (Art. 83); failing or falsifying reports/materials requested under Art. 9: fine up to JPY 300,000 (Art. 84); corporate dual liability (Art. 86) |

### Governance and sector guidance

| Instrument | Content |
|---|---|
| METI/IPA Cybersecurity Management Guidelines v3.0 (24 March 2023; English 31 May 2023) | Three principles for management — cyber risk is a management issue requiring executive leadership and investment; responsibility extends across the whole supply chain including cloud and overseas sites; proactive communication with stakeholders in normal times and incidents. Ten directions to the CISO: 1 policy, 2 management system, 3 resources, 4 risk identification and plans, 5 effective controls, 6 PDCA, 7 incident-response team and procedures, 8 business continuity and recovery, 9 supply-chain measures, 10 information gathering, sharing and disclosure. Appendix C (incident preparedness) and Appendix F v2.0 (organisation and talent, June 2022); IPA practice collection 4th ed. (October 2023) |
| FSA Guidelines for Cybersecurity in the Financial Sector (4 October 2024, applied same day after consultation 28 June–29 July 2024) | Sections on governance (board policy, rules, resources, second-line challenge, internal audit), identification (asset management, risk process, vulnerability management, vulnerability assessment and penetration testing, exercises), protection (authentication/access, training, data protection, system hardening), detection (monitoring), response and recovery (incident and contingency plans, prompt reporting to regulators, customer communication, sharing TTPs with Financials ISAC/JPCERT/CC, containment), and third-party risk management. Cites NIST CSF and the CRI Profile among its reference materials; distinguishes basic from desirable items |
| Basic Act on Cybersecurity | Art. 12 national Strategy; Art. 25–26 Strategic Headquarters and its standards for government and critical infrastructure; Art. 38 criminal penalty (up to 1 year or JPY 500,000) for confidentiality breaches by council/HQ personnel; amended by the 2025 companion act to reference the Cabinet Cyber Officer |

## Enforcement and penalties

| Mechanism | Detail |
|---|---|
| PPC investigative powers | Reports and on-site inspections (Art. 146); guidance and advice (Art. 147); recommendations → orders where a recommendation is ignored without legitimate grounds and serious infringement is impending → **urgent orders** without prior recommendation where serious harm exists (Art. 148(1)–(3)); publication of non-compliance with orders (Art. 148(4)) |
| Criminal penalties (current) | Order violation: up to 1 year imprisonment with work or JPY 1 million (Art. 178). Providing or misappropriating a personal-information database for illicit gain: up to 1 year or JPY 500,000 (Art. 179). False reports or obstructing inspection: up to JPY 500,000 (Art. 182). Corporate liability: up to **JPY 100 million** for Arts. 178–179 (Art. 184). Offences under Arts. 176–177 and 179–181 apply extraterritorially (Art. 183). Civil fine up to JPY 100,000 for record-keeping and naming offences (Art. 185) |
| 2026 amendment — criminal | Statutory penalties raised: Art. 176 (public-sector employees and entrusted staff) 2→3 years / JPY 1→1.5 million; Arts. 179–180 1→2 years / JPY 0.5→1 million; provision "with intent to cause harm" added to the database offence; **new offence** for acquiring personal information by fraud or unauthorised access; corporate cap remains JPY 100 million. Penalty articles are renumbered. These provisions take effect six months after promulgation (January 2027) — verify the exact date in the commencement Cabinet Order |
| 2026 amendment — surcharge | New Arts. 148-3 to 148-17: the PPC **must** order payment of a surcharge equal to the economic benefit obtained from serious violations (inappropriate use, improper acquisition, unlawful third-party provision, misuse of the statistics/AI exception) where the operator failed to exercise due care; not imposed where 1,000 or fewer individuals are affected or harm is otherwise limited; 1.5× for repeat offenders within 10 years (Art. 148-5); reductions for self-reporting (Art. 148-6). Commences with the main amendment (within two years of 17 July 2026) |
| 2026 amendment — orders | Art. 148 order thresholds relaxed so the PPC can act sooner; PPC may order notification or publication of the violation to affected individuals; new Art. 148-2 lets the PPC request third parties who assist a violation to stop |
| No private right of action | APPI creates no statutory damages claim; individuals sue in tort. Collective injunction/redress mechanisms were deferred for further study (House of Representatives supplementary resolution, 21 May 2026) |
| 2025 Act | Administrative orders and fines as above; no surcharge; the Cyber Communication Information Oversight Commission polices government handling of communications data, not operators |

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Confirm scope early**: any organisation with a searchable database of personal information is covered regardless of size, and foreign operators serving people in Japan are in scope under Art. 171. Determine separately whether any Japanese entity is a designated critical-infrastructure operator under the Economic Security Promotion Act — that drives 2025 Act duties. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Wire the four Rules Art. 7 triggers into incident triage** with a 3–5-day preliminary-report target and 30/60-day final-report tracking; treat "likelihood of leak" — including ransomware encryption without proven exfiltration — as reportable. See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/incident-regulatory-response.md](../../workflows/incident-regulatory-response.md).
3. **Critical-infrastructure operators**: inventory specified important computers (including routers, firewalls, credential and access-control servers) for the Art. 4 notification, and build the Art. 5 prompt-plus-30-day reporting path to both the competent minister and the Prime Minister's office before 1 October 2026.
4. **Document Art. 23 security measures** against the PPC guideline categories, including the "external environment" analysis for every foreign country where personal data is stored, processed or accessed (cloud regions, offshore support). Map to ISO 27001 Annex A or NIST CSF 2.0 — see [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md), [../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md](../frameworks/iso-27001-2022.md), [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md).
5. **Cross-border transfer register**: for every transfer out of Japan record the legal basis (consent with Art. 28(2) disclosures, EU/UK equivalence, or equivalent-measures contract) and the Art. 28(3) ongoing-assurance evidence; the same register serves GDPR Chapter V for inbound EU data under the PPC Supplementary Rules. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
6. **Entrustment (Art. 25) supervision**: contracts, due diligence and breach-notification-to-client clauses; from the 2026 amendment entrusted processors acquire direct duties (new Art. 30-3) — prepare both sides. See [../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md](../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md).
7. **Board reporting**: use METI v3.0's three principles and ten directions (and, for financial institutions, the FSA guideline's governance section) as the structure for cyber-risk reporting and CISO mandates. See [../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/grc-board-report.md](../../templates/grc-board-report.md).
8. **Horizon-scan the 2026 amendment**: track the PPC's Cabinet Order/Rules drafting, the surcharge guidelines, the statistics/AI-development exception conditions, under-16 consent rules, biometric ("specified biometric personal information") notice duties and the revised Art. 26(2) individual-notification exceptions. See [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md) and [../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md](../../workflows/new-regulation-impact-assessment.md).
9. **AI and analytics**: pseudonymized information (Arts. 41–42) and, once in force, the statistics-purpose exception are the lawful routes for model training on Japanese data; document the safeguards the Diet resolutions demand. See [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md) and [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **GDPR**: mutual adequacy means EU-origin data flows to Japan without SCCs, but the PPC Supplementary Rules impose GDPR-like extras on that data (broader sensitive-data definition, purpose limitation on onward transfer, pseudonymized/anonymized standards, retention). Japanese breach clocks (3–5 days preliminary, 30/60 days final, likelihood-based trigger) differ from GDPR's 72-hour, risk-based Art. 33 — run both. See [gdpr.md](gdpr.md) (and the EU international-transfers pack, eu-gdpr-international-transfers, where available).
- **UK**: the UK adequacy bridge with Japan is reflected in the PPC's designation of the UK as an equivalent jurisdiction; see the UK data-protection pack (uk-data-protection) where available.
- **NIS2 / CER / DORA**: the 2025 Act's reporting duty (prompt + 30 days) is looser than NIS2's 24h/72h/1-month cascade and applies only to designated critical-infrastructure operators; DORA-regulated groups with Japanese financial subsidiaries face the FSA guidelines and, if designated, the 2025 Act instead. See [nis2.md](nis2.md) and [dora.md](dora.md) (and the CER Directive pack, eu-cer-directive, where available).
- **Other APAC regimes**: compare Australia's NDB scheme (likely serious harm test, "as soon as practicable") and Singapore's PDPA (3 calendar days) in [other-jurisdictions.md](other-jurisdictions.md) (and the Australian Privacy Act pack, australia-privacy-act, where available).
- **Frameworks**: the FSA guideline is organised by governance, identify, protect, detect, respond/recover and third-party risk and cites NIST CSF as a reference; METI v3.0 references ISO/IEC 38500 for the "direction" concept. Use [../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md) when mapping.
- **Economic Security Promotion Act (2022)**: the 2025 Act piggybacks on its critical-infrastructure designations and equipment filings — one inventory should serve both.
