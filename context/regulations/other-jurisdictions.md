# Global jurisdiction index — which pack covers which country

This is the entry point for jurisdiction questions, not a summary of any regime. Each row below names the core instruments, the regulator and the headline breach clock for one jurisdiction, then routes to the pack that holds the detail. Facts here are the short form of what the linked pack's own "At a glance" table says; when the two ever differ, the pack wins. Countries covered by a regional pack (Africa, Latin America, Southeast Asia) get their own row and share that pack. Deadline comparisons across regimes side by side: [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md).

## Where one jurisdiction spans several packs

The table gives each jurisdiction its main routing row. These jurisdictions carry more than that:

- **European Union** — beyond [gdpr.md](gdpr.md) and [nis2.md](nis2.md): financial operational resilience ([dora.md](dora.md), [eu-dora-technical-standards.md](eu-dora-technical-standards.md)), AI ([eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md)), product security ([eu-cyber-resilience-act.md](eu-cyber-resilience-act.md), [eu-product-security-red-machinery.md](eu-product-security-red-machinery.md)), transfers ([eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md](eu-gdpr-international-transfers.md)), NIS2 national law ([eu-nis2-implementing-and-transposition.md](eu-nis2-implementing-and-transposition.md)), plus the CER, Cybersecurity Act, Data Act, DSA, eIDAS 2.0, EHDS and ePrivacy packs listed in [../README.md](../README.md).
- **United Kingdom** — the two rows above plus financial-sector operational resilience ([uk-financial-operational-resilience.md](uk-financial-operational-resilience.md)) and consumer product security ([uk-psti-product-security.md](uk-psti-product-security.md)); the national technical baseline sits in [../frameworks/uk-cyber-essentials-ncsc-caf.md](../frameworks/uk-cyber-essentials-ncsc-caf.md).
- **United States** — the two rows above plus roughly twenty sectoral packs, among them [glba-ftc-safeguards.md](glba-ftc-safeguards.md), [us-nydfs-part-500.md](us-nydfs-part-500.md), [us-banking-incident-notification-third-party.md](us-banking-incident-notification-third-party.md), [nerc-cip.md](nerc-cip.md), [us-tsa-transportation-cyber.md](us-tsa-transportation-cyber.md), [us-fisma-federal-cyber.md](us-fisma-federal-cyber.md) and [us-doj-bulk-data-rule.md](us-doj-bulk-data-rule.md).
- **Australia** — three rows above, one per regime: privacy, critical infrastructure and prudential. The national technical baseline is in [../frameworks/australia-essential-eight-ism.md](../frameworks/australia-essential-eight-ism.md).
- **Germany, in the EU context** — the national security baseline and cloud attestation scheme sit in [../frameworks/germany-bsi-it-grundschutz-c5.md](../frameworks/germany-bsi-it-grundschutz-c5.md).

Some obligations follow a product, a network or a contract rather than a country, so no row in the table will find them: automotive type approval ([automotive-un-r155-iso-21434.md](automotive-un-r155-iso-21434.md)), payment card rules ([../frameworks/pci-dss-4.md](../frameworks/pci-dss-4.md)), the Swift network ([../frameworks/swift-customer-security-programme.md](../frameworks/swift-customer-security-programme.md)) and the automotive supply-chain assessment scheme ([../frameworks/tisax-vda-isa.md](../frameworks/tisax-vda-isa.md)).

## How to use this index

1. **Pick the jurisdiction, then load that one pack.** The row is a routing aid, not a compliance answer — it carries only enough to confirm you have the right regime. Do not quote a clock from here without opening the pack behind it.
2. **Applicability usually follows the individual, not the head office.** Most privacy statutes in the table bite when you process the data of people resident in the jurisdiction, whatever your place of establishment. Cyber and critical-infrastructure regimes work differently: they follow the asset, the sector designation or the licence. Run both tests — see [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
3. **Expect one incident to trigger several regimes at once.** A single event can start a privacy clock, a sectoral clock and a critical-infrastructure clock in parallel, and the binding constraint is usually the shortest sectoral one (MAS 1 hour, CERT-In 6 hours, SOCI 12 hours) rather than the privacy law. Build the combined matrix from [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md), then work it with [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md).
4. **Read the clock's trigger, not just its length.** The packs differ on what starts the count — awareness, discovery, a materiality determination, or the completion of an assessment — and two "72 hours" can mean very different calendars.
5. **Verify against the official text before you file or advise.** Several regimes above are mid-change: new or merging regulators, rules commenced but not yet operative, bills still before a legislature. Each pack names its primary sources and its own review date; check both, and track movement with [../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-horizon-scanning/SKILL.md).

## Where this library stops

No pack covers the following in depth. Treat them as unknown: say so, and route the question to local counsel rather than reasoning by analogy from a neighbouring regime.

- **Europe outside the EU/EEA, the UK and Switzerland** — Türkiye, Ukraine, Russia, Serbia and the other Western Balkans states.
- **Asia beyond the packs above** — Taiwan, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Mongolia and Central Asia. Within Southeast Asia the regional pack stops at five countries: Brunei, Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar are not covered.
- **The Middle East beyond Saudi Arabia and the UAE** — Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman and Jordan, including their financial free-zone regimes.
- **Africa beyond the eight states in the regional pack** — the AU Malabo Convention is summarised there, but most national implementations of it are not.
- **The Americas beyond the packs above** — Latin American states outside the eight in the regional pack (Venezuela, Bolivia, Paraguay and Panama among them), and the Caribbean.
- **Oceania beyond Australia and New Zealand** — the Pacific Island states.
- **Sub-national law generally**, other than the US states, the Canadian provinces and the UAE free zones named above.
- **Export controls, sanctions and government lawful-access regimes** — outside the library's scope except where a pack touches them, as [us-doj-bulk-data-rule.md](us-doj-bulk-data-rule.md) does for bulk data flows to countries of concern.

Where a jurisdiction is absent, the honest answer is that the library does not cover it — an inference drawn from the nearest pack is a fabricated compliance fact, not a shortcut.
