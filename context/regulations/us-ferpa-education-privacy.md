# US education privacy: FERPA, PPRA and state student-privacy laws (20 U.S.C. 1232g/1232h; 34 CFR Parts 99 and 98)

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | **FERPA** — Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, 20 U.S.C. 1232g (GEPA §444), regulations at 34 CFR Part 99. **PPRA** — Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment, 20 U.S.C. 1232h, regulations at 34 CFR Part 98. State layer: California SOPIPA (Bus. & Prof. Code §§22584–22585) and Ed. Code §49073.1; Illinois SOPPA (105 ILCS 85); New York Education Law §2-d and 8 NYCRR Part 121 |
| Regulator | US Department of Education — Student Privacy Policy Office (SPPO; the regulations call it the Office of the Chief Privacy Officer, 34 CFR 99.60) with the Privacy Technical Assistance Center (PTAC). States: Illinois Attorney General (SOPPA); New York State Education Department Chief Privacy Officer (2-d) |
| Status and key dates | FERPA enacted 1974; Part 99 last substantively amended by the final rule of 2 Dec 2011 (76 FR 75603, effective 3 Jan 2012); enforcement office renamed by the rule of 19 Jan 2017 (82 FR 6252, effective 21 Feb 2017). No FERPA proposed or final rule published in the Federal Register since 2017 (Federal Register index for 34 CFR Part 99 checked to Sept 2026). SPPO "revitalized" enforcement announced 28 Mar 2025; annual FERPA/PPRA notification to SEAs and LEAs reissued 26 Aug 2026 |
| Who is covered | Any public or private educational agency or institution receiving funds under a program administered by the Secretary of Education (K-12 districts, SEAs, postsecondary institutions incl. via Title IV student aid) — the whole entity, every component (99.1). PPRA: SEAs/LEAs and other ED-funded recipients, K-12 only. State laws: K-12 edtech "operators" and schools/LEAs |
| Structure | FERPA = access/amendment rights + consent-before-disclosure with 16 exceptions (99.31) + recordkeeping (99.32) + annual notice (99.7) + complaint/enforcement (Subpart E). PPRA = consent/opt-out for surveys, marketing data collection and invasive exams |
| Security and breach | FERPA prescribes **no specific security controls** and contains **no breach-notification clock** (ED guidance); state laws add both — Illinois 30-day and New York 7/10/60-day clocks, NIST CSF alignment in New York |
| Penalties / enforcement | Federal: complaint-driven investigations, findings, voluntary-compliance period, then withholding of funds, cease-and-desist or termination of program eligibility (99.67); third parties that redisclose improperly are barred from PII for ≥5 years. **No private right of action** (Gonzaga Univ. v. Doe, 2002). States: New York civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation and per-student breach penalties; Illinois AG action under the Consumer Fraud Act |
| Certifiable? | No. Only voluntary edtech signals exist (1EdTech TrustEd Apps rubrics and certified-product directory; sector pledges and model data-protection agreements); the FPF/SIIA Student Privacy Pledge was retired 25 Apr 2025 |
| Neighbours | COPPA (under-13 online services, school-consent path), GLBA Safeguards (Title IV institutions), HIPAA (treatment records carve-out), state breach-notification laws, state comprehensive privacy laws (most carve out FERPA-regulated data — verify per statute) |

## What it is

FERPA is a 1974 spending-clause statute: schools that receive Department of Education funds may not have a "policy or practice" of denying parents access to their children's education records or of releasing personally identifiable information (PII) from those records without written consent, except as listed in the statute. Rights belong to the parent until the student turns 18 or enrols in postsecondary education, when they transfer to the "eligible student" (99.3, 99.5). The Department enforces through an office and review board (1232g(f)–(g)); funds may be terminated only where compliance "cannot be secured by voluntary means". The Supreme Court held in *Gonzaga University v. Doe* (20 June 2002) that FERPA's nondisclosure provisions "create no personal rights to enforce under §1983" — there is no individual damages remedy under FERPA itself.

PPRA (originating in 1974, Pub. L. 93-380 §514; amended 1978 and 1994; subsection (c) added by the No Child Left Behind Act, Pub. L. 107-110, effective 8 Jan 2002) sits alongside FERPA and governs what K-12 schools may ask students: surveys, analyses or evaluations touching eight protected categories (political affiliations, mental or psychological problems, sex behaviour or attitudes, illegal or self-incriminating behaviour, appraisals of family members, privileged relationships, religious practices, income) require consent or opt-out, and LEAs must adopt parent-consulted policies on surveys, instructional-material inspection, physical examinations and collection of student data for marketing.

Because FERPA is silent on security engineering and breach notification, and its "school official" exception lets districts hand PII to contractors without parental consent, most of the operational security obligations for education data come from **state student-privacy statutes** (the Future of Privacy Forum counts more than 40 states with laws applying to edtech providers), from **state breach-notification laws**, and — for higher education — from the **GLBA Safeguards Rule** via the Title IV Program Participation Agreement.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Test | FERPA / PPRA position |
|---|---|
| Entity | An "educational agency or institution" to which funds are made available under any ED-administered program — by grant, contract, subgrant, or because students bring Pell Grants or federal loans (99.1(c)). Applies to the recipient as a whole, including each department (99.1(d)). SEAs are subject to Subpart B access rights where they maintain education records (99.10(a)(2)) |
| Record | "Education records" = records directly related to a student and maintained by the agency/institution **or by a party acting for it** (1232g(a)(4); 99.3). Excluded: sole-possession notes, law-enforcement-unit records, employee records, and treatment records of students 18+ or in postsecondary education |
| PII | Name, family names, address, SSN, student number, biometric record, indirect identifiers (date/place of birth, mother's maiden name), and any information "linked or linkable" that would let a reasonable person in the school community identify the student with reasonable certainty (99.3) |
| Directory information | Name, address, telephone, email, photograph, date and place of birth, major, grade level, enrolment status, dates of attendance, activities/sports, height and weight of athletes, degrees and awards, last school attended. Never SSN; student ID only if it cannot open records without a PIN/password (99.3). Disclosable after public notice and an opt-out period (99.37) |
| Vendors | Not directly regulated by FERPA; reached via the school-official exception (99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)), the redisclosure limits (99.33) and the 5-year debarment for third parties that violate them (99.67) |
| PPRA | LEAs (elementary/secondary schools, districts, local boards receiving ED funds) — **not** postsecondary institutions (1232h(c)(6)(C)); rights transfer at 18 or emancipation |
| State edtech laws | California and Illinois: an "operator" of a site, service, application or mobile app "with actual knowledge that the site, service, or application is used primarily for K–12 school purposes and was designed and marketed for K–12 school purposes"; cloud computing services included; general-audience services excluded. Illinois "school" includes private/nonpublic schools. New York: "educational agency" (district, BOCES, school, NYSED) and any "third-party contractor" receiving student or teacher/principal data under contract |

## Core obligations

### FERPA (34 CFR Part 99)

| Obligation | Section | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| Annual notification | 99.7 | Notify parents/eligible students yearly of rights to inspect, seek amendment, consent to disclosures and file a complaint; state the procedures; if the school-official exception is used, publish the **criteria for who is a school official and what is a legitimate educational interest** |
| Inspect and review | 99.10 | Access within a reasonable period, **not more than 45 days** after the request; applies to all education records regardless of filing location (SPPO 2025/2026 letters: "gender plans" or separately filed records are still education records) |
| Amendment and hearing | 99.20–99.22 | Right to request amendment of inaccurate/misleading records and to a hearing |
| Written consent | 99.30 | Signed and dated; must specify records, purpose and recipient party/class |
| School-official (outsourcing) exception | 99.31(a)(1) | A contractor, consultant or volunteer may be treated as a school official if it (1) performs an institutional service or function the school would otherwise use employees for, (2) is under the school's **direct control** over use and maintenance of the records, and (3) is bound by the 99.33(a) use/redisclosure limits. The school must use **reasonable methods** (physical, technological or effective administrative controls) so officials access only records in which they have a legitimate educational interest |
| Other consent exceptions | 99.31(a)(2)–(16) | Transfer schools; audit/evaluation by federal and SEA/LEA authorities and their **authorized representatives** (written agreement, destruction when no longer needed — 99.35); financial aid; studies for the school under a written agreement (99.31(a)(6)); accreditors; parents of dependent students; subpoena/court order; **health or safety emergency** (99.36, record the articulable threat — 99.32(a)(5)); directory information; postsecondary disciplinary and alcohol/drug disclosures; statutory additions for USDA program monitoring (2010) and child-welfare caseworkers (2013) |
| De-identified data | 99.31(b) | Release without consent after removing all PII; coded student-level research data allowed if the code cannot be reverse-engineered |
| Identity assurance | 99.31(c) | Reasonable methods to identify and authenticate parents, students, officials and other recipients before disclosing PII |
| Record of disclosures | 99.32 | Log each request for and disclosure of PII (parties, legitimate interests), kept with the record for as long as it is maintained and reviewable by the parent — the only FERPA mechanism through which a parent learns of an unauthorized disclosure |
| Redisclosure | 99.33 | Recipients may not redisclose without consent; officers/employees/agents may use PII only for the purpose of the disclosure |
| Conflict with state law | 99.61 | Notify the Office within 45 days, citing the conflicting law |

**Security and breach posture.** ED states that FERPA "does not require educational institutions to adopt specific security controls" and, quoting the 2008 final-rule preamble (73 FR 74843–74844), that the Department "does not have the authority under FERPA to require that agencies or institutions issue a direct notice to a parent or student upon an unauthorized disclosure"; FERPA only requires the disclosure to be recorded under 99.32(a)(1), although direct notification "may be advisable" where SSNs are involved. PTAC's *Data Breach Response Checklist* (2012), *Protecting Student Privacy While Using Online Educational Services* (2014, with Model Terms of Service) and the *Data Breach Scenario Trainings* (now including AI-generated phishing/deepfake-call, AI tutoring-platform leak and AI grading-system compromise scenarios) are best-practice guidance, not rules. The 2014 guidance stresses that a written agreement is not strictly required for the school-official exception but is in practice how "direct control" is established, and that provider metadata stripped of all identifiers is not PII.

### PPRA (20 U.S.C. 1232h; 34 CFR Part 98)

| Obligation | Cite | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| Prior consent | 1232h(b) | No student may be required, in an ED-funded program, to submit to a survey, analysis or evaluation revealing any of the eight protected categories without prior written parental consent (student's own consent if 18 or emancipated). The 1984 regulation at 98.4 still carries the pre-2002 version of the list — seven categories, without religious practices — and covers psychiatric or psychological examination, testing or treatment |
| Instructional-material inspection | 1232h(a), (c)(1)(C); 98.3 | Parents may inspect instructional materials used in ED-funded surveys/evaluations and, under local policy, any curriculum material (not academic tests) |
| Local policies | 1232h(c)(1) | LEAs adopt, in consultation with parents, policies on: inspecting third-party surveys before administration; privacy arrangements for protected-category surveys; instructional-material access; physical examinations; **collection, disclosure or use of personal information for marketing** and inspection of the instruments used |
| Notice and opt-out | 1232h(c)(2) | Notice of policies at least annually at the start of the school year and after substantive changes; annual notice of the specific or approximate dates of covered activities; opt-out for marketing data collection, protected-category surveys and non-emergency invasive physical examinations |
| Marketing exception | 1232h(c)(4)(A) | Collection exclusively to develop or provide educational products/services (college or military recruitment, book clubs, curriculum, tests, fundraisers, recognition programs) is outside the marketing policy requirement |
| Regulations | Part 98 (1984) | §§98.1–98.10 codify only the pre-2002 provisions (instructional-material access, psychiatric/psychological examination consent, complaints); the subsection (c) duties added in 2002 are applied from the statute and ED guidance (SPPO-21-01, Nov 2020) |

| Breach clocks | No SOPIPA clock; §49073.1 requires contractual notification procedures; general Civil Code breach law applies | Operator → school **≤30 calendar days** after determining a breach; school → parents **≤30 calendar days** after notice/determination, law-enforcement delay allowed; school posts a breach list with thresholds (Sec. 15(5), 27(a)(5), 27(d)) | Contractor → agency **≤7 calendar days**; agency → Chief Privacy Officer **≤10 calendar days**; agency → parents/eligible students/teachers/principals **≤60 calendar days** after discovery (121.10) |
| Transparency | Operators may use de-identified data to improve products and demonstrate effectiveness; aggregated de-identified sharing allowed | School website: plain-language data-use explanation, list of operators with agreements and subcontractors, complaint/breach procedures, breach list — updated within 30 days of fiscal- and calendar-year start (Sec. 27(a)) | Parents' Bill of Rights on the agency website; annual employee training (121.7); designated Data Protection Officer (121.8); complaint process (121.4) |
| Deletion / parent rights | Delete on LEA request; from 2025, delete a former pupil's **CCPA-excluded covered information** on parent or adult-former-pupil request once the pupil has been un-enrolled for at least 60 days, subject to documentation of un-enrolment (§22584(d)(2)–(3)) | Delete on school request; parents may inspect, obtain a paper or electronic copy and demand correction of factual inaccuracies (correction within 90 calendar days; school confirmation to the parent within 10 business days) (Sec. 33) | Parents may inspect and review education records (121.12); PII not included in public reports |
| Enforcement | Chapter 22.2 (§§22584–22585) contains no penalty clause and no private right of action; the LEA-contract route is §49073.1(c), which voids a non-compliant contract after notice and failure to cure | Attorney General under the Consumer Fraud and Deceptive Business Practices Act (Sec. 35) | Breach-notification failure: greater of $5,000 or up to $10 per affected student/teacher/principal (capped per GBL §899-aa); other violations $1,000 / $5,000 / $10,000 tiered; preclusion from student data for up to 5 years; no private right of action against the department or an educational agency (2-d(6)–(7); 121.11) |

## Enforcement and penalties

- **Federal process (99.62–99.67):** SPPO investigates timely complaints (filed within **180 days** of the violation or of when the complainant should have known) or on its own initiative; may require reports, policies, annual notices and training materials; issues written findings with required steps and a voluntary-compliance period. Non-compliance may lead the Secretary to withhold payments, issue a cease-and-desist complaint or terminate program eligibility under GEPA Part D. Findings against a **third party** (improper redisclosure, failure to destroy study data) oblige the originating school to cut that party off from PII for at least **five years** (99.67(c)–(e); 1232g(b)(4)(B)).
- **No damages remedy:** *Gonzaga* forecloses §1983 suits; PPRA's enforcement clause mirrors FERPA's — Secretary enforcement, termination of assistance only where compliance cannot be secured voluntarily (1232h(e)). Plaintiffs instead plead state privacy, contract, consumer-protection or negligence theories, and New York, Illinois and California statutes supply state enforcers.
- **2025–2026 federal posture:** the Secretary's 28 Mar 2025 letter announced a "revitalized effort" to enforce FERPA and PPRA and to clear the FERPA complaint backlog; SPPO's priority concerns are parental inspect-and-review rights (records kept in separate files remain education records), safety-related disclosures, PPRA survey consent and annual notifications. The SPPO letter of 26 Aug 2026 to chief state school officers and superintendents reports recent investigations of SEAs and LEAs over inspect-and-review practices, makes effective annual notification "a point of emphasis during our enforcement work this year", and restates the three outsourcing conditions (institutional function, direct control, redisclosure limits) for disclosures to contractors and online educational services.
- **Higher education overlay:** Title IV institutions are subject to GLBA data-security requirements and, under the Program Participation Agreement, must report data breaches to Federal Student Aid — see [glba-ftc-safeguards.md](glba-ftc-safeguards.md).

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Confirm which layer applies** — FERPA (any ED-funded institution), PPRA (K-12 LEAs), GLBA/FSA (Title IV institutions), and the state student-privacy and breach statutes for every state where students are enrolled. See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Inventory education records and their custodians**, including records held by parties "acting for" the institution; classify PII versus properly designated directory information and track opt-outs. See [../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
3. **Operationalize the school-official exception**: written criteria in the annual notice, a signed agreement (or binding terms of service) establishing direct control, 99.33 use limits, deletion at contract end, and — in Illinois/New York — the statutory contract content, public posting and Parents' Bill of Rights. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
4. **Enforce least-privilege access** to student-information systems with role-based controls and authentication of requesters (99.31(a)(1)(ii), 99.31(c)); map to NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA (Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control). See [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md) and [../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md).
5. **Keep the 99.32 disclosure log** for every non-exempt disclosure and every health-or-safety-emergency disclosure; it is the artefact SPPO asks for first and the parent's only window into unauthorized disclosures.
6. **Build a breach playbook with state clocks**: Illinois 30/30 days, New York 7/10/60 days, plus the applicable state breach-notification statute and FSA reporting for Title IV institutions; FERPA itself imposes no clock. See [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md), [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md) and [../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md](../../templates/incident-regulatory-notification-log.md).
7. **Run the annual cycle**: FERPA/PPRA rights notice, PPRA activity-date notice and opt-out forms, New York staff training and policy publication, Illinois website lists (operators, subcontractors, breaches) refreshed within 30 days of the fiscal and calendar year. See [../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md](../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md).
8. **Gate AI and analytics tools** the same way as any operator: no profiling beyond K-12 purposes, no targeted advertising, de-identification standards for model improvement, and PPRA review where a tool surveys students on protected categories. See [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md) and [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
9. **Evidence for audits and complaints**: annual notices, school-official criteria, contracts, access-control configuration, disclosure logs, training records and the New York DPO designation. See [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **COPPA:** the FTC rule (amended 22 Apr 2025, effective 23 Jun 2025, full compliance from 22 Apr 2026) governs operators collecting personal information from children under 13 online; see [us-coppa.md](us-coppa.md) for the school-authorization path, which does not displace FERPA's direct-control and redisclosure conditions.
- **State breach-notification laws:** FERPA's silence means the state statute where the student resides, plus Illinois SOPPA and New York 2-d clocks, set the timelines; see [us-state-breach-notification-laws.md](us-state-breach-notification-laws.md) and [us-state-privacy.md](us-state-privacy.md) — most state comprehensive privacy laws carve out FERPA-regulated data (verify per statute), so do not assume a consumer-privacy program covers education records.
- **HIPAA:** FERPA excludes postsecondary treatment records from "education records" only while they are used solely for treatment; records at school-based clinics are usually FERPA records rather than PHI, because HIPAA's definition of protected health information expressly excludes education records covered by FERPA and the treatment records described at 20 U.S.C. 1232g(a)(4)(B)(iv) (45 CFR 160.103). See [hipaa.md](hipaa.md).
- **GLBA Safeguards:** Title IV institutions must run a GLBA-compliant information-security program and report breaches to FSA; FERPA adds the disclosure-consent layer on top. See [glba-ftc-safeguards.md](glba-ftc-safeguards.md).
- **Frameworks:** New York mandates NIST CSF v1.1 alignment; CIS Controls IG1 is a practical baseline for districts, and SOC 2 reports are the common evidence request for edtech vendors. See [../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md](../frameworks/nist-csf-2.md), [../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md](../frameworks/cis-controls-v8.md), [../frameworks/soc2-tsc.md](../frameworks/soc2-tsc.md) and [../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md).
- **Edtech ecosystem signals:** 1EdTech publishes TrustEd Apps rubrics (including data privacy) and a directory of certified products. When FPF retired the Student Privacy Pledge on 25 Apr 2025 it pointed schools and vendors instead to CISA's K-12 Education Technology Secure by Design Pledge and the Student Data Privacy Consortium's National Data Protection Agreement. None of these is a legal safe harbour or a substitute for the school's own due diligence; signatories remain bound by Pledge commitments for data collected while they were signatories.
