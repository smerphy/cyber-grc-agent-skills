# ISO/IEC 27017 and ISO/IEC 27018 — cloud security controls and PII protection in public clouds

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | **ISO/IEC 27017:2026**, *Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Information security controls based on ISO/IEC 27002 for cloud services* (edition 2.0, published 2026-07-27, 39 pages). **ISO/IEC 27018:2025**, *… Guidelines for protection of personally identifiable information (PII) in public clouds acting as PII processors* (edition 3.0, published 2025-08-26, 35 pages) |
| Publisher | ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27 (Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection); 27017 is published in parallel by ITU-T as Recommendation X.1631 (12/25), which supersedes X.1631 (07/15) |
| Status | Both are current. 27017:2026 cancels and replaces 27017:2015; 27018:2025 cancels and replaces 27018:2019. Both editions are realigned to ISO/IEC 27002:2022 |
| What they are | Control **guidance**, not management system standards. 27017 adds cloud-specific guidance on ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls plus a small extended cloud control set; 27018 adds PII-processor guidance plus an extended PII control set built on the ISO/IEC 29100 privacy principles |
| Who they cover | 27017: cloud service customers (CSCs) **and** cloud service providers (CSPs), across all deployment models including private cloud. 27018: organizations of any type or size providing processing services as **PII processors** in the **public** cloud under contract; also relevant to PII controllers |
| Certifiable? | Not on their own. 27017's revision draft scope states that it "excludes any and all aspects of conformity assessment" (verify the wording in the published edition). In practice both are certified as an **extension of an ISO/IEC 27001 scope** — declared in the Statement of Applicability and audited alongside the ISMS (verify the exact wording of your certification body's scheme) |
| Structure (both, current editions) | ISO/IEC 27002:2022 four-theme taxonomy: clauses 5 organizational (5.1–5.37), 6 people (6.1–6.8), 7 physical (7.1–7.14), 8 technological (8.1–8.34), plus annexes |
| Cost | Paid standards (IEC Webstore list prices CHF 196 for 27017:2026, CHF 179 for 27018:2025); only front matter (foreword, introduction, scope, first terms) is publicly previewable |
| Nearest neighbours | ISO/IEC 27001/27002 (the base), ISO/IEC 27701 privacy management, ISO/IEC 22123-1:2023 cloud vocabulary, the ISO/IEC 27036 supplier-relationship series, the ISO/IEC 19086 cloud SLA series, CSA CCM/STAR, SOC 2 |

## What it is

ISO/IEC 27017 and 27018 are the two cloud extensions of the ISO/IEC 27000 family. Neither adds a management system: they are sector/technology **codes of guidance** layered on ISO/IEC 27002. 27017 answers "what do the generic controls mean when the service is consumed from, or supplied as, a cloud service, and who does what"; 27018 answers "what extra duties fall on a public cloud provider that processes someone else's PII under contract".

Both were rewritten in this cycle for the same reason: the 2015 and 2019 editions were keyed to the ISO/IEC 27002:2013 clause numbering (5–18), which stopped matching ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A. The revisions realign them to the 93-control, four-theme ISO/IEC 27002:2022 taxonomy, so a Statement of Applicability no longer needs a hand-built bridge between two numbering systems. See [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md).

The 27017 revision went further than a renumbering. Its foreword records three main changes: the title was modified (the phrase "Code of practice" was dropped), the document structure was changed to present controls "using a simple taxonomy and associated attributes", and "some controls have been merged, some have been removed and several new controls have been introduced", with the full correspondence to the 2015 edition given in a dedicated annex. The 27018 revision was lighter: text aligned to ISO/IEC 27002:2022, and a new correspondence annex added.

## Who it covers / Scope

| Question | ISO/IEC 27017:2026 | ISO/IEC 27018:2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | CSCs and CSPs; guidance is split so that some items are for the CSC implementing a control and some for the CSP supporting it | Public cloud providers acting as **PII processors** under contract; guidance "can also be relevant to organizations acting as PII controllers" |
| Deployment models | All, explicitly including private cloud (adjustments may be needed for internal-department relationships) | Public cloud only |
| Role model | CSC, CSP, cloud service partner (CSN), cloud service user (CSU); IaaS/PaaS/SaaS | PII principal, PII controller, PII processor; the cloud service customer may itself be a controller or a principal |
| Explicit carve-out | Conformity assessment is out of scope | Where the provider processes **cloud service customer account data** it can be acting as a PII controller — that activity is out of scope |
| Trigger | Voluntary. Pulled in by customer contract, tender requirement, ISMS scope decision or group policy | Voluntary, but frequently contracted for by controllers discharging their own processor-diligence duties |
| Normative references | ISO/IEC 27002:2022 and ISO/IEC 22123-1:2023 (cloud vocabulary) | ISO/IEC 27002:2022; privacy principles of ISO/IEC 29100 |

Neither standard is a legal instrument and neither is an approved certification mechanism under GDPR Art. 42 (verify against the current EDPB register before claiming otherwise). They are assurance and contracting evidence, not a presumption of conformity. See [gdpr.md](../regulations/gdpr.md).

## Structure and requirements

### ISO/IEC 27017:2026

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Clause 4 | Cloud computing specific concepts: structure of the document; supplier relationships in cloud services; relationships between CSCs and CSPs; managing information security risks in cloud services |
| Clause 5 | Cloud service specific guidance on the 37 organizational controls (5.1–5.37), including 5.19–5.22 supplier relationships, 5.23 information security for use of cloud services, 5.24–5.28 incident management, 5.31 legal/regulatory requirements, 5.34 privacy and protection of PII |
| Clause 6 | Cloud guidance on the 8 people controls (6.1–6.8) |
| Clause 7 | Cloud guidance on the 14 physical controls (7.1–7.14) |
| Clause 8 | Cloud guidance on the 34 technological controls (8.1–8.34) |
| Annexes | In the DIS text: Annex A (normative) cloud service extended control set; Annex B (informative) correspondence with ISO/IEC 27017:2015 for backwards compatibility; Annex C (informative) monitoring of cloud services. *(Annex lettering and normative status are taken from the draft — verify against the purchased 2026 edition.)* |

The identifiers and titles of the individual extended controls in Annex A are behind the paywall and **could not be confirmed from any publisher-controlled source** — do not populate a control register from a third-party list of them. What *is* confirmed, from the foreword, is the direction of travel: relative to the 2015 edition "some controls have been merged, some have been removed and several new controls have been introduced", with the full mapping in the correspondence annex. Any register still citing `CLD.*`-style identifiers is on the superseded edition.

The superseded **ISO/IEC 27017:2015** (first edition, *Code of practice for information security controls based on ISO/IEC 27002 for cloud services*) followed ISO/IEC 27002:2013 clauses 5–18 with "Annex A — Cloud service extended control set" and "Annex B — References on information security risk related to cloud computing". Its `CLD.*` control identifiers are widely quoted but are not reproducible from the public preview — treat any list of them as **(verify)**, and in any case as legacy.

### ISO/IEC 27018:2025

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Clause 4 | Overview: structure of the document; control layout |
| Clauses 5–8 | PII-processor guidance mapped onto the same ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls (5.1–5.37, 6.1–6.8, 7.1–7.14, 8.1–8.34), e.g. 5.14 information transfer, 5.20 supplier agreements, 5.28 collection of evidence, 5.34 privacy and protection of PII, 8.10 information deletion, 8.34 protection of information systems during audit testing |
| Annex A (informative) | Public cloud PII processor extended control set for PII protection — the PII-specific additions organized against the ISO/IEC 29100 privacy principles |
| Annex B (informative) | Correspondence between this edition and ISO/IEC 27018:2019 |

The standard's own stated objectives (Introduction 0.1) are the three things a controller actually buys from it: provider **transparency** so customers can select well-governed processing services; support for **entering into a contractual agreement**; and a **mechanism for exercising audit and compliance rights** where a multi-tenant virtualized estate is technically impractical for the customer to audit directly. The processor/controller boundary is maintained by the provider having "no data processing objectives other than those set by the cloud service customer".

## Assessment, certification and evidence

- **There is no standalone accredited certificate for either standard.** The 27017 revision draft puts conformity assessment out of scope; 27018 is titled "Guidelines" and its extended control set is informative. Certification bodies deliver them as an **extension of an ISO/IEC 27001 audit**: the controls are declared applicable in the [Statement of Applicability](../../templates/statement-of-applicability.md), audited in the same visit, and reflected on the certificate or on a separate statement of verification.
- **Scope wording is the thing customers actually read.** A certificate that says "ISO/IEC 27001 with ISO/IEC 27017 and 27018" but whose scope statement covers one product line is not assurance for the rest of the estate. Check scope, edition year and the SoA, not the logo. See [audit-preparation](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).
- **Edition drift is the live audit risk through 2026–2027.** Certificates and SoAs referencing 27017:2015 or 27018:2019 point at superseded editions. No publisher-set transition deadline for the 27017 second edition was identifiable **(verify with your certification body and accreditation body)**; plan the re-mapping into the next surveillance audit rather than waiting for one.
- **Adjacent assurance routes** for the same audience: CSA STAR Level 1 (self-assessment against the Cloud Controls Matrix via CAIQ v4, published on the STAR registry), STAR Level 2 **Certification** (ISO/IEC 27001 management system plus the CCM, assessed together by an accredited body) or STAR Level 2 **Attestation** (SOC 2 plus the CCM). See [soc2-tsc.md](soc2-tsc.md).
- **Evidence that satisfies an assessor** is mostly contractual and operational: the shared-responsibility matrix per service, the cloud service agreement and SLA, the provider's sub-processor list and change-notification terms, admin-access and privileged-operation logs, tenant segregation design, key-management ownership, deletion/return-of-data records at exit, and PII breach-notification runbooks. See [control-testing](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Re-baseline the control register onto the 2022 taxonomy.** Replace 2013-era clause references and `CLD.*` identifiers with ISO/IEC 27002:2022 numbering, then re-run the mapping into the SoA. See [control-mapping](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md) and [framework-gap-assessment](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md).
2. **Decide which side of 27017 you are on — usually both.** Most organizations are a CSC for the platforms they consume and a CSP for the service they sell. Document the two control sets separately; the same clause reads differently in each direction.
3. **Write the shared-responsibility matrix down, per service.** 27017's central move is allocating each control between CSC, CSP and cloud service partner. An undocumented allocation is the recurring root cause of "we assumed the provider did that".
4. **Bind the allocation into contracts and SLAs.** Roles and responsibilities, sub-processor change notice, log and evidence access, incident notification timing, exit and data-return, and deletion assurance belong in the cloud service agreement — not only in an internal policy. See [third-party-risk-assessment](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [vendor-onboarding](../../workflows/vendor-onboarding.md).
5. **Treat 27018 as processor-side accountability evidence, never as legal cover.** It supports Art. 28-style processor diligence and customer audit rights; it does not substitute for a data processing agreement, transfer mechanism or lawful basis. See [dpia-privacy-assessment](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
6. **Watch the account-data carve-out.** Where the provider processes customer account data for its own purposes it may be a controller, outside 27018's scope — a gap that shows up in privacy reviews of telemetry, billing and support data.
7. **Test tenant isolation and administrator activity, not just policy.** Virtual-environment segregation, privileged-operation logging and monitoring of cloud services (the subject of a dedicated 27017 annex) are where assessors probe hardest, and they need technical evidence rather than a policy statement.
8. **Cover shadow cloud.** Detecting unauthorized cloud service use only works with a maintained inventory of sanctioned services; pair 27002:2022 control 5.23 with asset and software inventory discipline. See [cis-controls-v8.md](cis-controls-v8.md).
9. **Update customer-facing assurance artefacts at the same time.** Trust-centre pages, questionnaires and certificates that still cite 27017:2015 or 27018:2019 create avoidable diligence friction. See [vendor-security-questionnaire.md](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).

## Interplay

- **ISO/IEC 27001/27002** — the base. 27017 and 27018 are guidance layers, selected through the risk assessment and recorded in the SoA; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control **A.5.23** (information security for use of cloud services) is the hook that makes cloud acquisition, use, management and exit auditable in the ISMS itself. See [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md) and [iso27001-readiness](../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md).
- **ISO/IEC 27701** — where 27018 gives a public-cloud processor a control set, 27701:2025 gives controllers and processors a certifiable privacy **management system**. Running 27018 without a PIMS is common; the two are complementary, not alternatives. See [iso-27701-privacy-management.md](iso-27701-privacy-management.md).
- **CSA CCM / STAR** — the same problem space with a different unit of analysis: CCM domains and control IDs, a public registry and a customer-facing questionnaire. Useful when the buyer wants a filled questionnaire rather than a certificate. Mappings between CCM and the ISO cloud standards are published but directional — coverage analysis, not equivalence. See [csa-ccm-star.md](csa-ccm-star.md) and [framework-crosswalk.md](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md).
- **SOC 2** — a point-in-time or period attestation report with an auditor's opinion and tested controls, versus an ISO certificate attesting a management system. Large providers publish both. See [soc2-tsc.md](soc2-tsc.md).
- **Other ISO cloud standards** — ISO/IEC 22123-1:2023 (cloud computing vocabulary, a normative reference of 27017), the ISO/IEC 27036 series on information security for supplier relationships (which includes a part on cloud services), and the ISO/IEC 19086 series on the cloud service level agreement framework. These are the natural companions when writing cloud contract and SLA schedules; confirm the current part numbers and editions in the ISO catalogue before citing them.
- **Sectoral and regional regimes** — cloud outsourcing and exit provisions in EU financial-sector rules ([dora.md](../regulations/dora.md)), cloud switching and portability duties under the EU Data Act ([eu-data-act.md](../regulations/eu-data-act.md)), HIPAA business-associate obligations for cloud processors ([hipaa.md](../regulations/hipaa.md)), and the European certification framework ([eu-cybersecurity-act.md](../regulations/eu-cybersecurity-act.md)) all sit on top of, and are not satisfied by, 27017/27018 conformity.
