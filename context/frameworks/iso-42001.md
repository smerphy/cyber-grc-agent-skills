# ISO/IEC 42001:2023

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Owner / publisher | ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 (artificial intelligence); published jointly by ISO and IEC |
| Current version | ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (first edition, December 2023) |
| Structure | Management system clauses 4-10 (harmonized structure, same skeleton as ISO 27001) + Annex A reference controls (9 objective areas, commonly counted as 38 controls — verify against the purchased text) + Annexes B-D guidance |
| Certifiable? | Yes — the first certifiable AI management system (AIMS) standard. Accredited certification is still maturing; ISO/IEC 42006 (requirements for bodies auditing AIMS) was published in 2025 to anchor accreditation — verify a certificate's accreditation status |
| Companions | ISO/IEC 22989 (AI concepts/terminology), ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management guidance), ISO/IEC 42005 (AI system impact assessment guidance), ISO/IEC 42006 (certification body requirements) |
| Typical use | Certifiable AI governance for AI providers and enterprise deployers, structural support for EU AI Act readiness, customer assurance for AI products |
| Cost | Standard text is paid (purchase from ISO/IEC or national bodies) |

## What it is

ISO/IEC 42001 defines requirements for an **AI management system (AIMS)**: the organizational machinery — policy, roles, risk process, impact assessment, lifecycle controls, audit, review — for developing or using AI responsibly. Like ISO 27001, it certifies the *management system*, not any individual model or product. "ISO 42001 certified" does not mean a specific AI system is safe, fair, or accurate; it means the organization operates a conforming system for managing AI within the certificate's scope.

It applies to any organization in any role — provider, deployer, or both — and scope can be drawn around specific products, uses, or business units. As with 27001, the scope statement is where the assurance value is decided.

## Harmonized structure: clauses 4-10

ISO 42001 uses the same harmonized management-system skeleton as ISO 27001:2022 — this is the single most useful fact for integration planning:

| Clause | Title | AI-specific additions on top of the familiar skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Context of the organization | Determine the organization's role(s) with respect to AI systems (provider, deployer, user, etc.); AIMS scope |
| 5 | Leadership | AI policy required as a distinct document; top-management accountability for the AIMS |
| 6 | Planning | AI risk assessment and treatment; **Statement of Applicability** against Annex A (same mechanism as 27001); **AI system impact assessment** as a distinct planning requirement — see below |
| 7 | Support | Resources, competence (AI-specific skills), awareness, communication, documented information |
| 8 | Operation | Operational planning; performing AI risk assessments and impact assessments at planned intervals and on significant change; controlling the AI system lifecycle |
| 9 | Performance evaluation | Monitoring/measurement, internal audit, management review — same machinery as 27001 clause 9 |
| 10 | Improvement | Nonconformity/corrective action, continual improvement |

Because clauses 4-10 mirror ISO 27001 (see [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md)), an organization with a working ISMS can extend rather than rebuild: one document-control regime, one internal audit program, one management review with an AI agenda item, one corrective-action log. Building the AIMS as a standalone parallel bureaucracy is the most common and most expensive implementation mistake.

## Annex A: reference controls

Annex A is a reference control set applied via risk assessment and justified in a Statement of Applicability — the same not-individually-mandatory logic as 27001 Annex A. It is organized into **nine control objective areas** (numbered from A.2 in the published text); the total is commonly cited as 38 controls, but verify the exact count and numbering against the official text before building a checklist:

| Area | Theme |
|---|---|
| Policies related to AI | An AI policy, aligned with other org policies, reviewed at intervals |
| Internal organization | Roles, responsibilities, and reporting of concerns for AI |
| Resources for AI systems | Documentation of data, tooling, system/compute, and human resources and their provenance |
| Assessing impacts of AI systems | Impact assessment process covering individuals, groups, and societies |
| AI system life cycle | Objectives for responsible development; design, verification/validation, deployment, operation and monitoring, technical documentation, event logging |
| Data for AI systems | Data acquisition, provenance, quality, preparation for AI purposes |
| Information for interested parties | System documentation and information for users, external reporting channels, incident communication |
| Use of AI systems | Responsible-use processes, intended-use alignment, objectives for responsible use |
| Third-party and customer relationships | Allocating responsibilities across the AI supply chain; supplier and customer obligations |

**Annex B** provides implementation guidance for every Annex A control (the 27002 role, bound inside the same document). **Annex C** lists potential AI-related organizational objectives and risk sources — useful seed material for the risk assessment. **Annex D** discusses using the AIMS across domains and sectors.

A starter control catalog mapped to these areas is maintained in [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md) (references/ai-control-set.md).

## AI system impact assessment vs. DPIA

ISO 42001 requires an **AI system impact assessment**: assessing the consequences of an AI system for individuals, groups, and society — fairness, safety, autonomy, environment, fundamental rights — across the lifecycle. ISO/IEC 42005 provides methodology guidance.

This is *not* a DPIA and one does not substitute for the other:

| | AI system impact assessment (42001) | DPIA (GDPR Art. 35) |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | AI systems within AIMS scope, per the organization's criteria | Processing likely to result in high risk to natural persons |
| Lens | All impacts of the AI system — fairness, safety, societal, environmental — regardless of personal data | Risks to rights and freedoms arising from *personal data processing* |
| Legal status | Management-system requirement | Statutory obligation with regulator consultation duty |

For AI touching personal data, run both and cross-reference — procedure in [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md). The EU AI Act's fundamental-rights impact assessment (Art. 27, certain high-risk deployers) is a third, distinct instrument; a well-built 42001 impact assessment can feed it but does not automatically satisfy it.

## Relationship to the EU AI Act

ISO 42001 certification does **not** create a presumption of conformity with the EU AI Act — the Act's presumption mechanism runs through harmonized European standards (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 work), not directly through ISO standards, although the European standards draw heavily on ISO/IEC material. What 42001 does deliver against the Act:

- The quality-management-system obligation for high-risk providers (Art. 17) maps well onto AIMS clauses 4-10.
- Annex A lifecycle, data, documentation, and transparency controls cover much of the Arts. 8-15 requirement stack at the management level.
- The impact assessment, monitoring, and improvement loops support post-market monitoring and incident duties.

Treat 42001 as scaffolding that makes AI Act compliance operable and evidenceable, not as compliance itself. Statutory detail: [../regulations/eu-ai-act.md](../regulations/eu-ai-act.md); voluntary-framework counterpart: [nist-ai-rmf.md](nist-ai-rmf.md).

## Integrated certification with ISO 27001

Most early 42001 adopters certify on top of an existing ISO 27001 program, and certification bodies offer combined audits. What is shareable and what is not:

**Shared machinery (implement once):**

- Context and interested-party analysis (extend the existing one with AI roles and AI-relevant parties)
- Leadership commitment and policy governance structure
- Document control and records management
- Competence and awareness processes
- Internal audit program and auditor pool
- Management review (add AIMS agenda items)
- Nonconformity and corrective-action handling
- Supplier management skeleton (extend with AI supply-chain questions)

**AI-specific and not shareable:**

- The AI policy content itself
- AI risk assessment — risks to *stakeholders from AI behavior*, not just to information assets; a different risk universe than the ISMS register even if the methodology is shared
- The AI system impact assessment and its criteria
- Annex A lifecycle, data, and transparency controls and their evidence
- AI-specific competence (model evaluation, bias testing, AI incident triage)

Practical notes:

- Run one integrated internal audit with distinct AIMS coverage; auditors will check that AI-specific requirements were actually audited, not waved through under ISMS findings.
- Two Statements of Applicability (one per standard) even under combined audit.
- Security of AI systems (model theft, poisoning, prompt injection) sits naturally in the ISMS; *responsible behavior* of AI systems sits in the AIMS. Draw the line explicitly in both scope statements.
- Gap assessment mechanics: [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md); ISMS baseline: [../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md](../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md).

## Position in the SC 42 family

ISO 42001 sits atop a growing family from ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42; the ones that matter operationally:

- **ISO/IEC 22989** — AI concepts and terminology; the vocabulary 42001 assumes.
- **ISO/IEC 23894** — AI risk management guidance (ISO 31000 applied to AI); the methodology layer for the clause 6 risk assessment.
- **ISO/IEC 42005** — AI system impact assessment guidance; the methodology layer for the impact assessment requirement.
- **ISO/IEC 42006:2025** — requirements for bodies auditing and certifying AIMS; the accreditation anchor.
- **ISO/IEC 38507** — governance-of-IT implications of AI for governing bodies; useful for the clause 5 leadership conversation.

None of these are certifiable themselves; certification is against 42001 only — the same pattern as 27001 vs. 27002.

## Certification mechanics

Standard accredited-certification machinery applies (Stage 1 documentation review, Stage 2 implementation audit, annual surveillance, year-3 recertification; major vs. minor nonconformities) — see the fuller treatment in [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md), which transfers directly. AIMS-specific expectations before Stage 2 is realistic:

- An AI system inventory with classification/criticality actually populated
- At least one completed AI system impact assessment on a real system
- The AI risk assessment performed and treatments underway
- One internal audit covering AIMS-specific requirements and one management review with AI on the agenda
- Operating evidence for the selected Annex A controls (lifecycle documentation, data provenance records, logging) — not just approved policies

## Certification market maturity

The 42001 certification market is **young** — certificates only began issuing in 2024, and accredited certification is still stabilizing:

- ISO/IEC 42006:2025 (requirements for bodies certifying AIMS) anchors accreditation; accreditation bodies have been onboarding certification bodies since, with transition arrangements for certificates issued before accreditation was available. Verify the current state before relying on any certificate.
- Early certificates vary widely in scope rigor and auditor AI competence. Check: the accreditation mark, the scope statement, and which products/uses are actually covered.
- Expect the market to follow the 27001 trajectory: early scarcity value, then scope-scrutiny as certificates commoditize.

## Who should pursue it

- **AI providers under EU AI Act pressure** — high-risk system providers need an Art. 17 QMS anyway; building it as a certifiable AIMS yields a market-facing credential from mandatory work.
- **GPAI/model providers and AI-product vendors** answering enterprise security questionnaires — 42001 is becoming the standard ask alongside 27001/SOC 2.
- **Enterprise deployers wanting demonstrable governance** — regulated industries (finance, health, HR tech) deploying consequential AI, where "we govern our AI" needs third-party evidence.
- **Weaker cases:** organizations with incidental AI use only, or with no ISMS foundation — clause-level machinery (audit, review, document control) dominates the cost, and standing it up for AI alone rarely pays. Do 27001 first or together.

## Using this in assessments

- **Read the certificate scope, always** — same rule as 27001. An AIMS certificate scoped to one product line says nothing about the vendor's other AI. Ask for the SoA version/date and the list of AI systems in scope.
- **Gap-assess on two axes:** clause conformity (4-10) and Annex A control implementation. Organizations with a mature ISMS typically pass the clause axis quickly and fail on the AI-specific artifacts: no AI inventory, no impact assessments, no lifecycle documentation, no data-provenance records.
- **The impact assessment is the highest-signal artifact.** In vendor or internal reviews, request a (redacted) AI system impact assessment. Its absence, or a DPIA relabeled as one, tells you the AIMS is paperwork.
- **Check the risk universe.** An "AI risk assessment" that only contains security risks to AI assets is an ISMS extension, not an AIMS — look for risks *to people and society from system behavior* (bias, unsafe output, over-reliance).
- **In TPRM**, ask AI vendors: 42001 certificate (accredited? scope?), or if none, their AI RMF practice evidence — route through [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) with the AI question set from [../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md](../../skills/ai-governance/SKILL.md).
- **Evidence granularity per control:** policy/standard reference, owner, and operating evidence — identical discipline to 27001 Annex A; see [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md) and [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).
