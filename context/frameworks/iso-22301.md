# ISO 22301:2019

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Owner / publisher | ISO/TC 292 (security and resilience); published by ISO |
| Current version | ISO 22301:2019 (second edition, October 2019; replaced the 2012 first edition) |
| Structure | Management system clauses 4-10 (harmonized structure, same skeleton as ISO 27001) — no Annex A control catalog; the substantive requirements live in clause 8 |
| Certifiable? | Yes — the certifiable business continuity management system (BCMS) standard; accredited certification on the standard 3-year cycle |
| Companions | ISO 22313 (implementation guidance), ISO/TS 22317 (BIA), ISO/TS 22318 (supply chain continuity), ISO 22398 (exercises), ISO/IEC 27031 (ICT readiness for business continuity) |
| Typical use | BCMS certification, customer/regulator assurance of continuity capability, backbone for operational-resilience obligations (DORA, NIS2), structuring BIA/BC plan/exercise programs |
| Cost | Standard text is paid (purchase from ISO or national bodies) |

## What it is

ISO 22301 defines requirements for a **business continuity management system (BCMS)**: the capability to continue delivering products and services at acceptable predefined levels through disruption. Two structural facts distinguish it from ISO 27001:

1. **Same clause 4-10 skeleton** (context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement) — so it integrates with an existing ISMS on shared machinery: document control, internal audit, management review, corrective action.
2. **No Annex A.** There is no control catalog and no Statement of Applicability. The substantive requirements are embedded directly in **clause 8 (Operation)**, which carries the analytical and delivery weight of the whole standard.

## Clauses 4-10, with clause 8 unpacked

| Clause | Title | BCMS-specific emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Context | Products/services, interested parties (customers, regulators, suppliers), legal/regulatory continuity requirements, BCMS scope |
| 5 | Leadership | Top-management commitment, BC policy, roles — continuity is an executive accountability, not a facilities function |
| 6 | Planning | BC objectives, risks/opportunities to the BCMS itself (distinct from the operational risk assessment in 8.2) |
| 7 | Support | Resources, competence, awareness, communication plans, documented information |
| 8 | Operation | The core — see below |
| 9 | Performance evaluation | Monitoring, internal audit, management review — including evaluation of BC capability, not just the paperwork |
| 10 | Improvement | Nonconformity/corrective action, continual improvement — post-exercise and post-incident actions land here |

### Clause 8: the operational core

- **8.1 Operational planning and control** — including control of outsourced processes relevant to continuity.
- **8.2 Business impact analysis and risk assessment** — the analytical core (below).
- **8.3 Business continuity strategies and solutions** — identify and select options (before, during, after disruption) based on BIA/RA outputs, with resource requirements and risk-mitigation measures. Selection must be justified against the recovery objectives — a strategy that cannot meet the RTO is a nonconformity in waiting. Typical solution families:
  - Workarounds and documented manual procedures
  - Alternate sites, split operations, and remote-work arrangements
  - Workforce solutions: cross-training, succession for key roles, mutual aid
  - ICT recovery: dual-site/active-active, restore-from-backup, cloud failover (delivered per ISO/IEC 27031 practice)
  - Inventory/buffer stock and alternate logistics
  - Alternate or dual suppliers for critical dependencies
  - Insurance — finances recovery but restores nothing; never a standalone "solution"
- **8.4 Business continuity plans and procedures** — three layers auditors expect to see distinctly:
  - A **response structure**: teams with defined membership, competence, and the authority to invoke, escalate, and stand down; defined invocation criteria
  - **Warning and communication** procedures: staff, customers, suppliers, authorities, and media; contact data kept current
  - **Continuity and recovery plans** that are actionable under stress — trigger criteria, roles by function not by name, resource and dependency lists, concrete action steps, and interim-operation guidance toward the MBCO. A plan that reads like a policy fails the "usable at 3 a.m." test
- **8.5 Exercise programme** — exercises and tests, below.
- **8.6 Evaluation of business continuity documentation and capabilities** — periodic evaluation, and evaluation after incidents and significant change.

## BIA and risk assessment: the analytical core

The BIA determines *what matters and how fast*; the risk assessment determines *what could cause the disruption*. Everything downstream — strategy, plans, ICT recovery architecture — must trace to these two, and auditors test that traceability.

**BIA process** (ISO/TS 22317 gives the detailed method):

1. Identify the activities that deliver the in-scope products and services.
2. Assess impacts over time of not performing each activity — financial, legal/regulatory, reputational, contractual; "over time" is the point: impact curves, not single scores.
3. Set prioritized timeframes for resumption from the impact curves (MTPD, then RTO with margin).
4. Identify the dependencies each activity needs at resumption: people, sites, ICT and data, information, suppliers, equipment.
5. Get business-owner sign-off on the values — IT-invented RTOs are a standing audit finding.

**Core parameters** (definitions per the ISO 22300/22301 vocabulary — phrasing varies slightly across editions, but these are the working meanings):

| Term | Meaning | Practical test |
|---|---|---|
| **MTPD** (maximum tolerable period of disruption) | The time after which the viability of the organization is irreparably threatened if a product/service is not resumed | Set by the business, not IT; the ceiling nothing else may exceed |
| **RTO** (recovery time objective) | Target time to resume an activity/system after disruption | Must be < MTPD, with margin; per activity and per supporting system |
| **RPO** (recovery point objective) | Maximum tolerable data loss measured in time (state to which data must be restored) | Drives backup/replication frequency; a 4-hour RPO with nightly backups is a finding |
| **MBCO** (minimum business continuity objective) | Minimum acceptable level of products/services during disruption | Defines "degraded but acceptable" — plans should target MBCO first, full recovery second |

**Risk assessment (8.2.3):** identify and analyze risks of disruption to the prioritized activities and their dependencies, and select treatments. This is narrower than an enterprise risk assessment — it is disruption-scenario focused (site loss, ICT loss, people loss, supplier loss, denial of access) — but should use the organization's standard scales; see [../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [../risk-scoring.md](../risk-scoring.md).

## Exercising and testing

Clause 8.5 requires a **programme** — not an annual event. Requirements auditors check: exercises consistent with BC objectives and based on appropriate scenarios; defined aims and objectives per exercise; taken together over time, they validate the *whole* of the continuity arrangements; post-exercise reports with outcomes, and actions feeding clause 10 improvement.

Typical maturity ladder:

| Level | Exercise type | What it validates |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plan walkthrough / tabletop | Plan comprehension, role awareness, obvious gaps |
| 2 | Scenario simulation with live communications | Decision-making, escalation, notification mechanics |
| 3 | Technical failover/restore tests | Actual RTO/RPO achievement per system — measured, not asserted |
| 4 | Full or partial live interruption tests | End-to-end capability including workarounds at MBCO level |
| 5 | Supplier-inclusive exercises | Critical third-party response and joint recovery |

An exercise programme that only ever tabletops, never technically tests, and never rotates scenarios is a classic minor-nonconformity pattern; RTO/RPO claims with no test evidence behind them are worse — they convert the BIA into fiction.

## Links to ISO 27001 and ICT readiness

- **ISO 27001 Annex A 5.29** (information security during disruption) and **5.30** (ICT readiness for business continuity, new in 2022) are the bridge controls: 5.30 explicitly expects ICT continuity requirements derived from a BIA and tested recovery capability — i.e., a slice of ISO 22301 practice inside the ISMS. See [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md).
- **ISO/IEC 27031** provides guidance on ICT readiness for business continuity (IRBC) — the ICT-specific layer (DR architecture, failover, restore) that delivers the RTOs/RPOs the BIA set.
- Integrated ISMS/BCMS certification is common and cheap at the margin: shared clauses audited once, with the BCMS adding BIA, strategy, plans, and exercises. Availability risk work in the ISMS and disruption risk work in the BCMS should share one risk register, not duplicate.

## Regulatory overlap: DORA and NIS2

ISO 22301 is voluntary, but two EU regimes now impose continuity-and-testing obligations it maps directly onto:

- **DORA** (financial entities): ICT business continuity policy, ICT response and recovery plans with defined RTOs/RPOs, backup and restoration requirements, crisis communication, and a digital operational resilience **testing** programme up to TLPT for designated entities. A working BCMS covers much of the continuity substance; DORA adds ICT-specific testing depth, statutory incident reporting, and RTS-level detail a BCMS alone will not satisfy. See [../regulations/dora.md](../regulations/dora.md).
- **NIS2** (essential/important entities): the cybersecurity risk-management measures explicitly include business continuity — backup management, disaster recovery, and crisis management — plus incident-handling and testing expectations. A certified BCMS is strong (not conclusive) evidence for that measure. See [../regulations/nis2.md](../regulations/nis2.md).

In both regimes, map obligations to existing BCMS artifacts first and close deltas, rather than building a parallel resilience program — the same integration rule as everywhere else in this library.

## Common failure patterns

The recurring findings across BCMS assessments, roughly in order of frequency:

- **RTOs asserted, never measured** — recovery objectives in the BIA with no test evidence demonstrating they are achievable; often discovered to be off by an order of magnitude at first real failover test.
- **RPO/backup mismatch** — a stated RPO tighter than the actual backup or replication frequency delivers.
- **Plans keyed to named individuals** — single points of failure in the response structure; the plan dies with the author's departure or unavailability during the incident.
- **Stale dependency data** — BIA dependencies and call trees that predate a cloud migration, reorganization, or supplier change.
- **DR-only programs** — ICT runbooks with no business-side workarounds, no MBCO definition, and no crisis communication layer.
- **Supplier blind spot** — critical third-party dependencies identified in the BIA but no continuity requirements in the contract and no evidence the supplier can meet the assumed recovery times ([../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md)).
- **Exercise theater** — the same benign tabletop scenario annually, findings never tracked to closure.

## Certification

Standard accredited-certification mechanics apply (Stage 1 documentation review, Stage 2 implementation audit, annual surveillance, year-3 recertification; major vs. minor nonconformities) — see the fuller treatment in [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md), which transfers directly. BCMS-specific expectation: at least one exercise cycle completed with evidence before Stage 2, alongside the usual internal audit and management review.

**Evidence auditors typically request:**

- BCMS scope statement and BC policy
- BIA records: prioritized activities, dependencies, MTPD/RTO/RPO/MBCO values, and business sign-off on those values
- Disruption risk assessment and selected treatments
- Documented strategy selection rationale — options considered and why the chosen one meets the objectives
- BC plans and the response-structure definition with team rosters and invocation authority
- Call trees or notification-tool test evidence with currency checks
- Exercise programme schedule, per-exercise aims and reports, and closed post-exercise actions
- Records of actual incident invocations and post-incident reviews (real disruptions count as capability evidence — and must feed improvement)
- Supplier continuity requirements in contracts for critical dependencies, and any supplier test participation
- Internal audit and management review records covering BC *capability*, not just documentation

Preparation mechanics: [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).

## Using this in assessments

- **Trace the golden thread first.** BIA → RTO/RPO/MTPD → strategy → plan → exercise evidence. Any break (plans not derived from a BIA; RTOs never tested; a BIA older than the current operating model) is the finding that matters most; polish elsewhere cannot compensate.
- **Check BIA currency against organizational change.** Re-performed or reviewed at planned intervals and after significant change (new products, migrations to cloud, site changes, M&A). A pristine 3-year-old BIA is a nonconformity in substance even where the interval was met.
- **In vendor reviews**, ask for: certificate scope (which sites/services), the RTO/RPO commitments for *your* service and the latest test evidence for them, and how you the customer feature in their notification procedures. An ISO 22301 certificate without service-specific RTO evidence answers the wrong question — route through [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **Distinguish BC, DR, and crisis management** in gap work: DR (ICT recovery) is a subset delivering the technical RTOs; crisis management is the leadership/communication layer; BC is the whole capability. Programs frequently have DR runbooks and nothing else — that is an ICT capability, not a BCMS.
- **Gap assessments run on the clause skeleton** (no Annex A/SoA axis): clause 8 substance first (BIA, strategy, plans, exercises), then the management-system wrapper. Mechanics: [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **Cross-check BIA outputs against the ISMS.** Where an ISMS coexists, the availability requirements in the information security risk assessment and the RTO/RPO values in the BIA must agree — divergent numbers for the same system mean at least one is fiction. Annex A 5.30 evidence should cite the BIA directly.
- **Use companion technical specifications for method disputes.** When an auditee's BIA method is contested, ISO/TS 22317 is the reference arbiter for BIA practice and ISO 22398 for exercise design — cheaper than arguing from first principles.
- **For DORA/NIS2-regulated entities**, use the BCMS as the base layer and gap the statutory deltas (reporting clocks, testing depth, register/contract requirements) explicitly — obligations are compliance items with deadlines, not roadmap aspirations.
