# ISO/IEC 27001:2022

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Owner / publisher | ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27; published jointly by ISO and IEC |
| Current version | ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (third edition, October 2022), incl. Amendment 1:2024 (climate action considerations added to clause 4 context) |
| Structure | Management system clauses 4-10 (mandatory) + Annex A: 93 controls in 4 themes |
| Certifiable? | Yes — the flagship certifiable information security standard. Accredited certification bodies audit against it; certificates run a 3-year cycle |
| Companion | ISO/IEC 27002:2022 — implementation guidance for the Annex A controls (not certifiable itself) |
| Typical use | ISMS certification, customer assurance (esp. EU/UK/APAC markets), structural backbone for a security program, contract prerequisite |
| Cost | Standard text is paid (purchase from ISO/IEC or national bodies) |

## What is actually mandatory

Two distinct layers, frequently confused:

1. **Clauses 4-10** — the management system requirements. All are mandatory for certification; none can be excluded.
2. **Annex A** — a reference control set. Controls are *not* individually mandatory. The organization determines necessary controls via risk assessment, then compares against Annex A to check nothing was missed, and justifies inclusions/exclusions in the **Statement of Applicability (SoA)**. Excluding a control is fine; failing to justify the exclusion is a nonconformity.

## Management system clauses 4-10

| Clause | Title | Core requirements |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Context of the organization | Internal/external issues (incl. climate change per Amd 1:2024), interested parties and their requirements, ISMS scope definition |
| 5 | Leadership | Top management commitment, information security policy, roles and responsibilities assigned and communicated |
| 6 | Planning | Risk assessment and risk treatment processes; Statement of Applicability; information security objectives; (6.3, new in 2022) planning of changes to the ISMS |
| 7 | Support | Resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information control |
| 8 | Operation | Execute the plans: operational planning, perform risk assessments at planned intervals, implement the risk treatment plan |
| 9 | Performance evaluation | Monitoring and measurement, internal audit program, management review |
| 10 | Improvement | Nonconformity and corrective action, continual improvement |

Key documented artifacts auditors always request: scope statement, information security policy, risk assessment methodology and results, risk treatment plan, SoA, objectives, internal audit records, management review minutes, corrective action log. See [audit-preparation](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md) and [iso27001-readiness](../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md).

## Annex A: the 2022 restructure

The 2013 edition had 114 controls in 14 domains (A.5-A.18). The 2022 edition reorganizes to **93 controls in 4 themes**, numbered to match ISO/IEC 27002:2022:

| Theme | Numbering | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational controls | 5.1 - 5.37 | 37 |
| People controls | 6.1 - 6.8 | 8 |
| Physical controls | 7.1 - 7.14 | 14 |
| Technological controls | 8.1 - 8.34 | 34 |
| **Total** | | **93** |

The reduction from 114 to 93 came from merging controls (57 controls merged into 24), not from dropping requirements — plus 11 genuinely new controls. One 2013 control was split. Every 2013 control maps forward; ISO 27002:2022 Annex B provides the official bidirectional mapping.

### The 11 controls new in 2022

| Control | Title | What auditors look for |
|---|---|---|
| 5.7 | Threat intelligence | Collection and analysis of threat info (strategic/tactical/operational); feeds into risk assessment and detection |
| 5.23 | Information security for use of cloud services | Cloud acquisition, use, management, and exit processes; shared-responsibility clarity |
| 5.30 | ICT readiness for business continuity | ICT continuity requirements derived from BIA; tested recovery capability |
| 7.4 | Physical security monitoring | Surveillance of premises (CCTV, alarms, guards) with legal-basis awareness |
| 8.9 | Configuration management | Defined, enforced, monitored secure configurations/hardening baselines |
| 8.10 | Information deletion | Deletion when no longer required — systems, devices, and cloud; supports privacy retention obligations |
| 8.11 | Data masking | Masking/pseudonymization/anonymization aligned to policy and regulation |
| 8.12 | Data leakage prevention | DLP measures on systems, networks, endpoints for sensitive information |
| 8.16 | Monitoring activities | Anomaly detection on networks, systems, applications; response to events |
| 8.23 | Web filtering | Managing access to external websites to reduce malicious content exposure |
| 8.28 | Secure coding | Secure coding principles applied to software development |

These 11 are where organizations transitioning from 2013-era ISMS programs most often have genuine gaps — prioritize them in readiness work.

### Control attributes (27002:2022)

ISO 27002:2022 tags every control with five attribute types, useful for filtering and mapping:

- **Control type:** Preventive / Detective / Corrective
- **Information security properties:** Confidentiality / Integrity / Availability
- **Cybersecurity concepts:** Identify / Protect / Detect / Respond / Recover (deliberately aligned to NIST CSF functions — pre-dates CSF 2.0, so no Govern tag)
- **Operational capabilities:** e.g., governance, asset management, identity and access management, threat and vulnerability management
- **Security domains:** governance and ecosystem, protection, defence, resilience

## Relationship to 27002 and the 27000 family

- **27001** states *that* controls from its Annex A must be considered; **27002** explains *how* to implement each (purpose, guidance, attributes). Certification is against 27001 only.
- Sector extensions add controls on top: **27017** (cloud security), **27018** (PII in public cloud), **27701** (privacy information management, extends 27001/27002 — being revised to a standalone certifiable standard). **27005** covers information security risk management methodology.

## Certification cycle

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 audit | Documentation/readiness review: scope, SoA, risk methodology, mandatory documents. Identifies areas of concern before Stage 2 |
| Stage 2 audit | Full implementation audit: evidence that the ISMS operates as documented. Certificate issued on closure of any major nonconformities |
| Surveillance audits | Annually (years 1 and 2 of the cycle), sampled scope |
| Recertification audit | Year 3, full re-audit; new 3-year cycle begins |

- **Nonconformities:** *major* (systemic failure or absence of a required element — blocks certification until corrected) vs. *minor* (isolated lapse — corrective action plan accepted, verified at next audit). Auditors may also raise observations/opportunities for improvement.
- Certification bodies should themselves be accredited (e.g., against ISO/IEC 17021) by a national accreditation body — check the accreditation mark; unaccredited certificates carry little assurance value.
- An ISMS typically needs ~3 months of operating evidence (internal audit + management review completed at least once) before Stage 2 is realistic.

### Transition from 2013

The transition window closed: all ISO/IEC 27001:2013 certificates had to transition to 2022 by **October 31, 2025**. Any certificate encountered now should be against the 2022 edition; treat a 2013 certificate date as a red flag in third-party reviews (see [third-party-risk-assessment](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md)).

## Using this in assessments

- **Read the certificate scope, always.** Certification can cover a single product, site, or team. A vendor's "ISO 27001 certified" claim means little until you confirm the scope statement covers the services you consume, and the certificate is current and accredited.
- **Request the SoA version and date** alongside the certificate in vendor reviews — exclusions tell you more than the certificate does. Vendors often redact justifications; the inclusion/exclusion list alone is still informative.
- **Gap assessments have two axes:** clause conformity (4-10) and control implementation (Annex A). Immature programs fail on clauses (no risk process, no internal audit, no management review) more often than on technical controls. Assess clauses first.
- **Use control attributes for crosswalks.** The cybersecurity-concepts attribute gives a coarse ISO→CSF mapping for free; for finer mappings see the [framework crosswalk](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md) and [control-mapping](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md).
- **Evidence granularity:** for each in-scope Annex A control, expect (a) a policy/standard reference, (b) an implementation owner, (c) operating evidence. A control with a policy but no operating evidence is a finding. See [control-testing](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
- **ISO 27001 vs SOC 2:** ISO certifies the management *system*; SOC 2 Type II reports on control *operating effectiveness* over a period with per-control test results. Many customers want both; overlap is high — build the control set once and map. See [soc2-tsc](soc2-tsc.md) and [soc2-readiness](../../skills/soc2-readiness/SKILL.md).
