# NIST Risk Management Framework (SP 800-37 Rev. 2) with SP 800-39 and SP 800-30 Rev. 1

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Instruments | SP 800-37 Rev. 2, *Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations: A System Life Cycle Approach for Security and Privacy* (December 2018); SP 800-39, *Managing Information Security Risk: Organization, Mission, and Information System View* (March 2011); SP 800-30 Rev. 1, *Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments* (September 2012) |
| Publisher | NIST Joint Task Force (JTF), Computer Security Division; all free of charge from csrc.nist.gov / nvlpubs.nist.gov |
| Legal anchor (US federal) | FISMA (44 U.S.C. § 3551 et seq., P.L. 113-283); OMB Circular A-130 requires agencies to implement the RMF. Not applicable to national security systems without approval of the relevant authorities |
| Who uses it | Mandatory process for US federal executive-branch systems and their contractors; NIST positions it for "any type of organization regardless of size or sector," including IoT and control systems |
| Structure | 7 steps — Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, Monitor — decomposed into 47 tasks (P-1…P-18, C-1…C-3, S-1…S-6, I-1…I-2, A-1…A-6, R-1…R-5, M-1…M-7) |
| Companion standards | FIPS 199 (categorization, February 2004); FIPS 200 (minimum requirements, March 2006); SP 800-53 Rev. 5 / 800-53B / 800-53A Rev. 5 (controls, baselines, assessment); SP 800-60 Vol. 1 & 2 Rev. 1 (information types, August 2008); SP 800-137 (ISCM, September 2011); SP 800-18 Rev. 2 (system plans, June 2026) |
| Output / "certification" | No certificate. The output is an **authorization decision** — authorization to operate (ATO), authorization to use (ATU), or common control authorization — issued by an authorizing official who explicitly accepts residual risk; the decision can also be a denial |
| Assessment model | Independent or self-assessment of controls per SP 800-53A procedures; results in a security/privacy assessment report and a plan of action and milestones (POA&M); ongoing authorization via continuous monitoring |
| Machine-readable | OSCAL (XML/JSON/YAML) for catalogs, profiles, system plans, assessment plans and results; SP 800-53 Rev. 5 controls published in OSCAL |
| Status (Sep 2026) | SP 800-37 Rev. 2, SP 800-39 and SP 800-30 Rev. 1 remain the current editions; no public draft of a Rev. 3 / Rev. 2 located on CSRC. Neighbouring documents moved in 2025–2026 (see Timeline) |

## What it is

The RMF is the process layer of NIST's federal information security architecture: FIPS 199 tells you how bad a compromise would be, FIPS 200 and SP 800-53 tell you which controls to consider, SP 800-53A tells you how to assess them, and SP 800-37 tells you how to run all of that as a system life-cycle process ending in an accountable, explicit risk-acceptance decision. SP 800-39 sits above it as the enterprise risk-management model (three tiers, four components), and SP 800-30 Rev. 1 sits beside it as the detailed method for the risk assessments that Prepare, Categorize, Select and Authorize all consume.

Rev. 2 (2018) made four substantive changes over Rev. 1: it added an organization-wide **Prepare** step; it integrated **privacy** risk management (privacy plans, privacy control assessments, a senior agency official for privacy role); it aligned the RMF with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (each task table cites CSF constructs — note these are **CSF v1.1** identifiers, e.g. ID.RA-6, DE.CM) and with systems security engineering (SP 800-160); and it incorporated supply chain risk management. It also formalized the push from static, point-in-time reauthorizations to **ongoing authorization** built on continuous monitoring, and the maximal use of common controls, cloud/shared services and organizationally tailored baselines to shrink the number of authorizations an organization must carry.

## Who it covers / Scope

- **Federal agencies:** mandatory under FISMA and OMB A-130 for federal information systems other than national security systems. SP 800-37 Appendix F restates the FISMA and OMB rule that external providers that process federal information or operate systems on behalf of the federal government meet the same security and privacy requirements as agencies; ownership/operation type (government-owned/contractor-operated, contractor-owned/contractor-operated, nonfederal) is recorded in the system description (Task C-1).
- **Cloud and shared services:** SP 800-37 Appendix F treats a provisional authorization issued by the General Services Administration under FedRAMP as a valid authorization to operate that customer agencies leverage through an **authorization to use**, rather than re-authorizing from zero. See [fedramp.md](fedramp.md).
- **Non-federal use:** entirely voluntary. Common adopters are defense and civilian contractors, state and local government, critical infrastructure operators and regulated firms whose regulators accept NIST-based risk assessments. SP 800-30's risk model and scales are widely borrowed even where the ATO construct is not.
- **Scope unit:** the **authorization boundary** (Task P-11; Appendix G) — all components of a system authorized for operation by one authorizing official, excluding separately authorized connected systems. Boundary definition drives everything downstream; NIST devotes an appendix to getting it right.

## Structure and requirements

### The seven steps and their tasks (SP 800-37 Rev. 2, Chapter 3 and Appendix E)

| Step | Tasks | What must be produced |
|---|---|---|
| **Prepare — organization level** | P-1 Risk management roles; P-2 Risk management strategy (incl. risk tolerance); P-3 Organization-wide risk assessment; P-4 Organizationally tailored control baselines / CSF profiles; P-5 Common control identification; P-6 Impact-level prioritization; P-7 Continuous monitoring strategy | Named roles, risk strategy and tolerance statement, enterprise risk assessment, published tailored baselines, catalogue of inheritable common controls, ISCM strategy |
| **Prepare — system level** | P-8 Mission/business focus; P-9 Stakeholders; P-10 Asset identification; P-11 Authorization boundary; P-12 Information types; P-13 Information life cycle; P-14 System-level risk assessment; P-15 Requirements definition; P-16 Enterprise architecture placement; P-17 Requirements allocation; P-18 System registration | Boundary statement, information-type inventory, system risk assessment, security and privacy requirements, system registration record |
| **Categorize** | C-1 System description; C-2 Security categorization; C-3 Categorization review/approval | FIPS 199 categorization (C/I/A impact levels, high-water mark) approved by the authorizing official |
| **Select** | S-1 Control selection; S-2 Tailoring; S-3 Allocation (common / hybrid / system-specific); S-4 Documentation of planned implementations; S-5 System-level continuous monitoring strategy; S-6 Plan review and approval | Approved security and privacy plans (system plans) listing every control, its allocation and its monitoring frequency |
| **Implement** | I-1 Control implementation; I-2 Update control implementation information ("as-implemented" state) | Implemented controls; plans updated to reflect reality |
| **Assess** | A-1 Assessor selection; A-2 Assessment plan; A-3 Control assessments; A-4 Assessment reports; A-5 Remediation actions and reassessment; A-6 Plan of action and milestones | Approved assessment plan, security/privacy assessment reports, POA&M |
| **Authorize** | R-1 Authorization package; R-2 Risk analysis and determination; R-3 Risk response; R-4 Authorization decision; R-5 Authorization reporting | Authorization package; documented AO risk determination; ATO / ATU / common control authorization or denial with terms, conditions and termination date or authorization frequency |
| **Monitor** | M-1 System and environment changes; M-2 Ongoing assessments; M-3 Ongoing risk response; M-4 Authorization package updates; M-5 Security and privacy reporting; M-6 Ongoing authorization; M-7 System disposal | Change impact analyses, ongoing assessment results, updated plans/POA&M, posture reports (event-driven, time-driven or both), disposal strategy |

Executing the RMF is not linear in practice: Prepare tasks are revisited whenever risk tolerance, common controls or boundaries change, and Monitor feeds back into every earlier step.

### Categorization inputs (FIPS 199, FIPS 200, SP 800-60)

| Instrument | Content used by the RMF |
|---|---|
| FIPS 199 (Feb 2004) | Three security objectives (confidentiality, integrity, availability). Impact levels: **Low** = limited adverse effect; **Moderate** = serious adverse effect; **High** = severe or catastrophic adverse effect. Information types are categorized per objective; the system category is the **high-water mark** across its information types and objectives (e.g. `SC = {(C, MODERATE), (I, MODERATE), (A, LOW)}`) |
| FIPS 200 (Mar 2006) | Minimum security requirements in **17 security-related areas** (AC, AT, AU, CA, CM, CP, IA, IR, MA, MP, PE, PL, PS, RA, SA, SC, SI) and the rule that agencies select the SP 800-53 baseline matching the FIPS 199 category. Still listed as **Final** on CSRC with no withdrawal or supersession notice as of September 2026 |
| SP 800-60 Vol. 1 & 2 Rev. 1 (Aug 2008) | Method and catalogue of federal information types with provisional impact levels; the reference for Task P-12/C-2. Still the current edition on CSRC; no Rev. 2 draft located as of September 2026 |
| SP 800-53B (Sep 2020; Release 5.2.0 Aug 2025 with no baseline changes) | Low / Moderate / High security baselines plus the privacy baseline that Task S-1 starts from before tailoring |

### Control allocation and authorization concepts (SP 800-37 Rev. 2, Appendix F and glossary)

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| Common / hybrid / system-specific control | Common: provided by a common control provider and inherited by many systems; system-specific: implemented by one system; hybrid: partly each. Common controls get their own **common control authorization** by a separate authorizing official |
| Authorization package (minimum content) | Executive summary, system security plan, privacy plan, security control assessment, privacy control assessment, and relevant POA&Ms (definition adopted from OMB A-130); may be produced by an automated reporting tool |
| Initial authorization | Zero-based review of all system-level controls plus the status of inherited common controls |
| Ongoing authorization | Follow-on risk determinations at documented **time-driven or event-driven** frequencies, dependent on a robust continuous monitoring program; the authorization termination date may be replaced by an authorization frequency |
| Reauthorization | Static point-in-time re-decision; under ongoing authorization it becomes mostly event-driven (risk exceeds the level previously accepted) and may be a full or targeted review |
| Authorization to use (ATU) | Customer organization accepts risk based on an existing package produced by another organization — the cloud/shared-service pattern |
| Type authorization | One package for an archetype system deployed identically at multiple sites; site AO issues an ATU leveraging it |
| Facility authorization | Common control authorization for controls in a defined environment (physical/environmental, boundary protection, facility contingency/IR, staff screening) that resident systems inherit |
| Traditional vs joint authorization | One AO accountable, or multiple AOs sharing the decision and responsibility |

### Roles (SP 800-37 Rev. 2, Appendix D)

Head of agency; risk executive (function); senior accountable official for risk management; chief information officer; senior agency information security officer; senior agency official for privacy; chief acquisition officer; authorizing official and designated representative; common control provider; control assessor; enterprise architect; security or privacy architect; information owner or steward; mission or business owner; system owner. Task A-1 lets organizations self-assess or engage an independent control assessor; the level of assessor independence required is a deliberate decision that the authorizing official's confidence in the results rests on.

### SP 800-39 — the enterprise model the RMF plugs into

- **Three tiers:** Tier 1 organization (governance, risk executive function, risk management strategy, investment strategy); Tier 2 mission/business processes (including enterprise architecture and information security architecture); Tier 3 information systems (where the RMF runs).
- **Four components, applied across all tiers with bidirectional information flows:** **Frame** (assumptions, constraints, risk tolerance, priorities and trade-offs), **Assess**, **Respond**, **Monitor**.
- **Risk response types:** accepting, avoiding, mitigating, sharing, transferring.
- The risk frame is where an organization must document its **risk tolerance**; SP 800-37 Task P-2 and the authorizing official's risk determination (R-2) both cite it, so an undocumented tolerance is a structural gap, not a paperwork one.

### SP 800-30 Rev. 1 — the risk assessment method

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Process (Chapter 3) | **1 Prepare** (Tasks 1-1 purpose, 1-2 scope, 1-3 assumptions and constraints, 1-4 information sources, 1-5 risk model and analytic approach); **2 Conduct** (2-1 threat sources, 2-2 threat events, 2-3 vulnerabilities and predisposing conditions, 2-4 likelihood, 2-5 impact, 2-6 risk determination); **3 Communicate and share** (3-1, 3-2); **4 Maintain** (4-1 monitor risk factors, 4-2 update the assessment) |
| Risk model factors | Threat (threat sources → threat events), vulnerability, predisposing condition, likelihood, impact. Threat source types: hostile cyber/physical attacks; human errors of omission or commission; structural failures of organization-controlled resources; natural and man-made disasters beyond the organization's control |
| Assessment approaches | Quantitative, qualitative, or **semi-quantitative** (bins or 1–10 scales that translate to qualitative levels); analysis approaches are threat-oriented, asset/impact-oriented, or vulnerability-oriented |
| Tiers | Assessments at Tier 1, 2 and 3 with different scope and granularity; system-tier assessments feed RMF Tasks P-14 and R-2 |
| Appendix scales (Tables G-2…G-5, H-3, I-2, I-3) | Five qualitative levels with semi-quantitative equivalents: **Very High 96–100 (10)**, **High 80–95 (8)**, **Moderate 21–79 (5)**, **Low 5–20 (2)**, **Very Low 0–4 (0)**. Overall likelihood combines likelihood of initiation/occurrence with likelihood of adverse impact (Table G-5) |
| Appendices | D threat sources (adversarial: individual/group/organization/nation-state characteristics of capability, intent, targeting; non-adversarial: range of effects); E threat events; F vulnerabilities and predisposing conditions; G likelihood; H impact; I risk determination; J informing risk response; K risk assessment reports (essential elements of information); L summary of tasks |

Level of risk, Table I-2 (rows = likelihood that the threat event occurs *and* results in adverse impact; columns = impact):

| Likelihood \ Impact | Very Low | Low | Moderate | High | Very High |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very High | Very Low | Low | Moderate | High | Very High |
| High | Very Low | Low | Moderate | High | Very High |
| Moderate | Very Low | Low | Moderate | Moderate | High |
| Low | Very Low | Low | Low | Low | Moderate |
| Very Low | Very Low | Very Low | Very Low | Low | Low |

SP 800-30 deliberately does not prescribe an algorithm for combining factors; Table I-2 is an example, and organizations are expected to document their own risk model before assessing. See [../risk-scoring.md](../risk-scoring.md) for how this compares with FAIR-style quantification and with generic 5×5 matrices.

## Assessment, certification and evidence

- **No certification scheme.** The credential is the authorization decision document, signed by the authorizing official, stating terms and conditions, the termination date or ongoing-authorization frequency, and (for common controls) the impact level supported. Denials are also formal outcomes.
- **Assessment procedures — SP 800-53A Rev. 5 (January 2022; Release 5.2.0, August 2025):** assessment objectives and assessment methods per control (the basis IR 8011 uses to decide which controls can be tested automatically); the Assess step (A-1…A-6) requires an approved assessment plan, an assessment report and a POA&M for every unsatisfied control. See [nist-800-53.md](nist-800-53.md) and [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
- **Continuous monitoring — SP 800-137 (September 2011):** six-step ISCM process — define strategy, establish program, implement, analyze data and report findings, respond to findings, review and update. SP 800-137A (May 2020) adds an ISCM program assessment method and NISTIR 8212 (March 2021) supplies the free ISCMAx tool. IR 8011 (Vol. 1 Rev. 1 initial public draft February 2025) defines which SP 800-53 controls can be tested by automation.
- **System plans — SP 800-18 Rev. 2 (June 2026):** the system security plan, system privacy plan and cybersecurity supply chain risk management plan are treated as one set of "system plans," with essential elements correlated to RMF tasks. Older SSP templates predate this and should be re-based.
- **Evidence expectations:** categorization record and approval; boundary diagram and inventory; tailored baseline with tailoring rationale; inheritance agreements for common controls; assessment plan, raw assessment evidence, report; POA&M with milestones and risk-based prioritization; authorization decision letter; monitoring results and posture reports at the defined frequency. OSCAL can carry all of these as structured data; NIST publishes the 800-53 catalog and baselines in OSCAL.
- **Reciprocity:** an ATU built on another organization's package is only as strong as that package's assessment — the accepting AO still owns the risk.

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Decide whether the RMF is mandatory or a chosen method.** Federal systems, FedRAMP offerings and many DoD/civilian contracts require it; everyone else is adopting its vocabulary voluntarily and can scope it (e.g., SP 800-30 only). See [../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
2. **Run Prepare before anything else:** named roles (P-1), a written risk management strategy with risk tolerance (P-2), and a common-control catalogue (P-5). Missing these is the most frequent root cause of slow, inconsistent authorizations. Use [../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md](../../skills/policy-authoring/SKILL.md) for the strategy document.
3. **Categorize honestly and record the high-water mark reasoning** (FIPS 199, SP 800-60); the category fixes the baseline and therefore the cost of every later step.
4. **Conduct and maintain SP 800-30 assessments at all three tiers** using a documented risk model and scales — [../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md), [../risk-scoring.md](../risk-scoring.md), [../../templates/risk-register-guide.md](../../templates/risk-register-guide.md).
5. **Tailor the baseline with written rationale** and track every control not fully implemented as a POA&M item with owner, milestones and risk rating; treat accepted deviations as formal exceptions — [../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md](../../skills/exception-management/SKILL.md).
6. **Assess with SP 800-53A procedures and an assessor independent of the system**; keep raw evidence, not just conclusions — [../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md), [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).
7. **Move to ongoing authorization deliberately:** an ISCM strategy (P-7, S-5) with per-control monitoring frequencies, posture reporting to the AO, and defined event-driven reauthorization triggers — feed results into [../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md).
8. **Handle inherited and third-party controls explicitly:** inheritance agreements for common controls, ATU decisions for cloud services based on the provider's package, and supply chain risk in the system plans — [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
9. **Re-base templates on 2025–2026 editions:** SP 800-53 5.2.0 control text and SP 800-18 Rev. 2 system-plan structure; plan for OSCAL if tooling requires machine-readable packages.

## Interplay

- **SP 800-53 Rev. 5:** the control catalog the Select/Implement/Assess steps operate on; the RMF is the process, 800-53 is the content. See [nist-800-53.md](nist-800-53.md).
- **NIST CSF 2.0:** SP 800-37 Rev. 2 maps tasks to CSF **v1.1** categories (e.g. ID.RA-6, ID.SC-4, DE.CM); CSF 2.0 reorganized functions (adding Govern), so refresh any RMF-to-CSF mapping rather than reusing the 2018 tables. CSF profiles can serve as organizationally tailored baselines under Task P-4. See [nist-csf-2.md](nist-csf-2.md).
- **ISO/IEC 27001, 31000 and 27005:** see [iso-31000-27005-risk-management.md](iso-31000-27005-risk-management.md) for the ISO risk-process analogue of SP 800-39/800-30. ISO 27001's risk assessment, risk treatment and Statement of Applicability requirements have direct analogues in SP 800-30 and the tailored security plan; an authorization decision is functionally a documented risk-acceptance record. See [iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md) and [../../templates/statement-of-applicability.md](../../templates/statement-of-applicability.md).
- **SP 800-171 Rev. 3 (May 2024):** recommended requirements for protecting CUI confidentiality in nonfederal systems, imposed through contracts and assessed with SP 800-171A; there is no authorizing official or ATO construct. See [nist-800-171-cmmc.md](nist-800-171-cmmc.md).
- **FISMA and FedRAMP:** FISMA and OMB A-130 make the RMF the federal baseline process, and agencies consume FedRAMP packages via authorization to use (SP 800-37 Appendix F). See [../regulations/us-fisma-federal-cyber.md](../regulations/us-fisma-federal-cyber.md) and [fedramp.md](fedramp.md).
- **AI systems:** NIST's COSAiS project is developing SP 800-53 overlays for AI use cases, which would be consumed as tailored baselines in the Select step; the AI RMF covers the governance side. See [nist-ai-rmf.md](nist-ai-rmf.md).
- **SOC 2, PCI DSS:** attestation and validation regimes, not risk-acceptance regimes; RMF artifacts (SSP, assessment report, POA&M) map onto their evidence requests but the AO decision has no equivalent. See [soc2-tsc.md](soc2-tsc.md), [pci-dss-4.md](pci-dss-4.md) and the [framework crosswalk](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md).
- **Privacy regimes:** Rev. 2's privacy plan and privacy control assessment overlap with DPIA-style obligations; see [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
