# UK NCSC assurance schemes: Cyber Essentials / Cyber Essentials Plus and the Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF)

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Publisher | National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC, part of GCHQ). Cyber Essentials is delivered under licence by IASME as the NCSC's Cyber Essentials Delivery Partner, managing a network of 400+ UK cyber security organisations that advise applicants and certify them |
| Two distinct things | **Cyber Essentials** — a certifiable baseline of five technical controls for organisations of any size. **CAF** — a non-certifiable, outcome-based assessment framework for organisations running essential functions. They share a publisher and nothing else structurally |
| Cyber Essentials current text | *Requirements for IT Infrastructure* **v3.3** (April 2026), effective for applications started from **27 April 2026**; v3.2 had been in effect since **28 April 2025** and still governs assessment accounts created before the cut-over (IASME: 6 months to finish under the old version) |
| CAF current version | **v4.0**, released **4 August 2025** (previous: v3.2 15 April 2024, v3.1 11 April 2022, v3.0 30 September 2019) |
| CAF structure | 4 objectives (A–D) → 14 principles → **41 contributing outcomes**, each with an Indicators of Good Practice (IGP) table scored achieved / partially achieved (where offered) / not achieved |
| Cyber Essentials structure | 5 technical control themes → requirements, assessed by a question set derived from the Requirements document; certification must be renewed annually |
| Certifiable? | Cyber Essentials: yes — verified self-assessment signed off by a board member or equivalent and marked by an assessor. Cyber Essentials Plus: same five controls plus independent technical testing. CAF: no certification; expert-judgement assessment against a regulator-set CAF profile |
| Cost | Cyber Essentials tiered from **£320 + VAT** by organisation size; Cyber Essentials Plus quoted on network size and complexity. CAF itself is free |
| Mandatory where | Central government departments, executive agencies, NDPBs and NHS bodies must require CE or CE+ (or demonstrated equivalent controls) from suppliers on higher-risk contracts (PPN 014). CAF underpins GovAssure for government organisations, the CAF-aligned NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit, and NIS-regulator supervision |

## What it is

The UK has no single cyber standard; it has a layered set of NCSC products. At the bottom sits **Cyber Essentials**, launched in 2014 as a government-backed minimum: five technical controls that block the commodity, internet-facing attacks that make up most incidents. It is deliberately narrow — no governance, risk-management or incident-response requirements — and deliberately cheap, so that it can be pushed down supply chains. At the top sits the **Cyber Assessment Framework**, written for organisations whose failure would disrupt the country: it specifies outcomes rather than controls, is assessed by judgement against IGP tables, and is designed to be interpreted by an oversight body (a NIS competent authority, the Cabinet Office, NHS England) that sets a target profile.

The two are complementary rather than nested. Cyber Essentials is a point-in-time pass/fail snapshot — the April 2026 scheme changes make explicit that the "point in time" is the date the certificate is issued, and that systems must be supported at that date — while a CAF assessment is a graded maturity picture with an improvement plan attached. A CAF-regulated operator will normally hold Cyber Essentials Plus and cite it against a handful of contributing outcomes (typically B2.c privileged user management, B4.b secure configuration, B4.d vulnerability management), not as a substitute for the assessment. IASME names each annual question set; the set accompanying v3.3 is the **Danzell** question set, published on 13 February 2026.

Around these sit the NCSC's assurance schemes delivered through IASME: Cyber Advisor (NCSC-assured practitioners helping smaller organisations implement the five controls), Cyber Incident Exercising, Cyber Incident Response, IASME Cyber Assurance, IASME IoT Cyber, Maritime Cyber Baseline, the CAA's ASSURE scheme and the MOD's Defence Cyber Certification.

## Who it covers / Scope

**Cyber Essentials — organisational scope.** Certification should cover the whole IT infrastructure used to run the business, or "a well-defined and separately managed sub-set". The scope boundary must name the business unit managing it, the network boundary and the physical location, and must be agreed with the certification body before assessment. A scope that excludes end-user devices is not acceptable. Since v3.3, **cloud services cannot be excluded from scope** where they host the organisation's data or services, and exclusions must be justified to the assessor with an explanation of how excluded networks are segregated. From April 2026 applicants must also list every legal entity in scope (name, address, company number), describe out-of-scope areas (not published) and may buy per-entity certificates within a wider scope.

**Cyber Essentials — technical scope.** All in-scope devices and software that can accept inbound connections from internet-connected devices, establish outbound connections over the internet, or control data flow between those devices and the internet. Covers servers, desktops, laptops, tablets, mobile phones, firewalls and routers, plus IaaS, PaaS and SaaS; BYOD, home and remote working, wireless devices, third-party accounts and devices, and application development each have their own scoping rules. For cloud, the applicant is always accountable for every control even where the provider implements it; the requirements document sets out a control-by-control split across IaaS/PaaS/SaaS.

**CAF.** The common core is sector-agnostic and aimed at any organisation responsible for essential functions. Applicability comes from the body that mandates it, not from the CAF: NIS competent authorities for operators of essential services and relevant digital service providers, the Cabinet Office for government organisations under GovAssure, NHS England through the CAF-aligned DSPT. Oversight bodies may issue sector-specific profiles, sector-specific interpretations of outcomes or IGPs, and additional sector-specific outcomes.

## Structure and requirements

### Cyber Essentials — the five technical controls (v3.3)

| # | Control | What the requirements actually say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Firewalls | Every in-scope device protected by a correctly configured firewall or firewall-capable network device; change default administrative passwords or disable remote administration; no internet-facing administrative interface unless there is a clear documented business need protected by MFA or an IP allow list with managed passwords; block unauthenticated inbound connections by default; inbound rules approved, documented with the business need, and removed when obsolete; software firewall on devices used on untrusted networks |
| 2 | Secure configuration | Remove or disable unused user and administrative accounts; change default or guessable passwords; remove unnecessary software, utilities and network services; disable auto-run; authenticate users before access to organisational data or services; device unlocking credential (biometric, password or PIN) with brute-force protection — throttling allowing no more than 10 guesses in 5 minutes, or lockout after no more than 10 attempts — and a minimum 6-character unlock PIN/password |
| 3 | Security update management | All in-scope software licensed and supported; unsupported software removed or placed in a defined sub-set with no internet traffic; automatic updates enabled where possible; updates and vulnerability fixes installed **within 14 days** of release where the vendor rates the issue critical or high risk, where the CVSS v3 base score is 7.0 or above, or where the vendor gives no severity detail |
| 4 | User access control | Accounts assigned to authorised individuals only and to the least access needed; accounts removed or disabled when no longer required; **MFA implemented wherever available — authentication to cloud services must always use MFA**; separate accounts for administrative activity only; privileges removed on role change. MFA password element at least 8 characters; password quality met by MFA, or a 12-character minimum, or an 8-character minimum plus automatic blocking of common passwords via a deny list; no maximum length, **no forced expiry, no complexity rules**; passwordless methods (passkeys/FIDO2, biometrics, security keys, push, one-time codes) recognised, FIDO2 authenticators counting as MFA |
| 5 | Malware protection | An active mechanism on every in-scope device: anti-malware software (Windows/macOS) updated per vendor guidance and configured to prevent malware and malicious code running and to block connections to malicious sites; or application allow listing restricted by code signing, with applications actively approved before deployment, a current approved list, and no user installation of unsigned or invalidly signed applications |

v3.3 changes, per the document's own "What's new in this version": a definition of "cloud service"; the passwordless-authentication definition extended to include FIDO2; a definitive statement that cloud services cannot be excluded from scope; the Software Security Code of Practice introduced in the software development section (publicly available commercial web applications are in scope by default, bespoke and custom components are not); scope criteria that no longer refer to "untrusted connections"; and backup guidance moved earlier to emphasise it. IASME adds that "untrusted" and "user-initiated" were both dropped as qualifiers for internet connections, and that the user access control section was rewritten to highlight passwordless methods such as passkeys.

### CAF — objectives, principles and outcomes (v4.0)

| Objective | Principles | Contributing outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| A — Managing security risk | A1 Governance, A2 Risk Management, A3 Asset Management, A4 Supply Chain | 9 (A1.a–c, A2.a Risk Management Process, A2.b Understanding Threat, A2.c Assurance, A3.a, A4.a, A4.b Secure Software Development and Support) |
| B — Protecting against cyber attack | B1 Service protection policies/processes/procedures, B2 Identity and Access Control, B3 Data security, B4 System security, B5 Resilient networks and systems, B6 Staff awareness and training | 20 (B1.a–b, B2.a–d, B3.a–e, B4.a–d, B5.a–c, B6.a–b) |
| C — Detecting cyber security events | C1 Security monitoring, C2 Threat Hunting | 7 (C1.a–f, C2.a) |
| D — Minimising the impact of incidents | D1 Response and recovery planning, D2 Lessons learned | 5 (D1.a–c, D2.a–b) |

Each contributing outcome carries an IGP table with an "achieved" (green) column — normally all indicators must be present — a "not achieved" (red) column where any one indicator is enough to fail, and, where offered, a "partially achieved" (amber) column that must deliver real security benefit, not merely "something vaguely relevant". IGPs are indexed as `[4.0]A1.a.A.4` (version, objective, principle, contributing outcome, column, indicator). The NCSC states that an assessment showing all 41 outcomes achieved indicates security "some way beyond the bare minimum basic cyber hygiene level" — which is why profiles, not full achievement, are the normal target.

**What changed in v4.0** (record of changes, 4 August 2025): three new contributing outcomes — A2.b Understanding Threat (threat-actor capabilities, methods and techniques feeding risk decisions), A4.b Secure Software Development and Support (maximising use of secure, supported software, internally built or sourced), and C1.f Understanding User's and System's Behaviour, and Threat Intelligence, which absorbs indicators from v3.2's C1.d and C2.a and adds a new indicator on sharing threat intelligence with the defender community; v3.2's A2.b Assurance renumbered to A2.c; the old principle C2 Proactive Security Event Discovery (outcomes C2.a and C2.b) removed and replaced by a new principle C2 Threat Hunting with the single outcome C2.a; and new board-level indicators at A1.a (the board has the information and understanding needed to discuss security and resilience, and security is recognised as an enabler).

## Assessment, certification and evidence

| Element | Cyber Essentials | Cyber Essentials Plus | CAF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Method | Verified self-assessment against the question set, board-signed, marked by an assessor. Two routes: self-led, or supported by a certification body | The same self-assessment plus an independent technical audit that tests the controls on a random sample of devices | Self-assessment or independent review of 41 outcomes against IGPs, scored against a target profile |
| Timing | Point in time = date of certificate issue; systems must be supported at that date; annual renewal | Must follow a finalised verified self-assessment; the self-assessment may **not** be amended after CE+ testing begins (scheme terms, April 2026) | Periodic, on the cycle set by the oversight body |
| Failure handling | From April 2026 the MFA-for-cloud question and the two 14-day update questions (A6.4 operating systems and router/firewall firmware, A6.5 applications and their files/extensions) are **auto-fail** regardless of other answers | Failed update-management sampling requires remediation and a retest covering the original sample **and a new random sample**; a second failure revokes the verified self-assessment certificate | No pass/fail; outcomes not achieved feed a targeted improvement plan |
| Evidence typically requested | Firewall rule documentation and approvals, build standards, patch/update reports with dates, account and privilege reviews, MFA configuration exports, malware/allow-list configuration, asset and cloud service inventories | All of the above, plus the assessor's own scan and test results | Policies, board minutes, risk registers, threat assessments, supplier assurance, logging and monitoring evidence, exercise reports, post-incident reviews |

CAF profiles work by selecting a subset of the 41 outcomes and the level required for each — some at achieved, some at partially achieved, some marked not applicable. The profile, not the CAF, is the compliance yardstick; confirm with the relevant oversight body which CAF version and which profile apply before scoping an assessment.

**GovAssure** (Government Security Group, Cabinet Office) assesses government-critical systems against the CAF using the WebCAF platform: stage 3 self-assessment, stage 4 independent assurance review by an approved reviewer, stage 5 targeted improvement plans, against published Government CAF profiles and supported by the Government Cyber Security Policy Handbook.

**NHS DSPT.** The Data Security and Protection Toolkit is an annual self-assessment, contractually required through the NHS Standard Conditions, with a submission deadline of **30 June** each year. The larger-organisation version is CAF-structured — its outcomes carry CAF references (for example B2.c privileged user management, C1.e monitoring tools and skills, D1.a response plan) alongside an NHS-specific Objective E on lawful use and sharing of patient information (E1.a privacy and transparency information). CAF-aligned DSPT guides and independent-assessor material for NHS trusts, CSUs, ICBs and ALBs were published for the 2024-25 year. For 2026-27 the expected achievement levels for B2.c, C1.e, D1.a and E1.a rise for "Standards Exceeded". For organisation types outside the NHS trust/ICB/ALB/CSU/Genomics/OES-designated group, holding a current **Cyber Essentials Plus** certificate recorded in the organisation profile turns a "Standards Met" result into "Standards Exceeded" — a direct, documented link between the two schemes.

Pending as of September 2026: the Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill is **not yet an Act** — it cleared the Commons on 16 June 2026, completed Lords Grand Committee on 7 September 2026 and is listed for Lords report stage on 26 October 2026. It will put more of the UK's regulated cyber regime on a statutory footing, and CAF profiles are the instrument regulators are expected to use, so track the Bill rather than the CAF for scope changes. No CAF release later than v4.0 and no Cyber Essentials requirements release later than v3.3 had been published as at September 2026; Cyber Essentials is updated annually on an April cycle, so expect a further requirements revision in April 2027.

## Key obligations for security/GRC teams

1. **Pin the version before you assess or bid.** Certificates issued under v3.2 and v3.3 are not interchangeable in substance; CAF v3.2 and v4.0 differ by three new outcomes, a renumbered Assurance outcome and a replaced C2 principle. Record version and effective date on every certificate and assessment artefact. See [framework-gap-assessment](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md).
2. **Treat the three auto-fail questions as production controls, not assessment answers.** MFA on every cloud service where it exists, and 14-day installation of critical/high-risk updates across operating systems, firmware and applications, decide the outcome of the whole assessment from April 2026. Measure patch latency and MFA coverage continuously. See [control-testing](../../skills/control-testing/SKILL.md).
3. **Fix scope before the certification body does.** Whole-organisation scope is worth more commercially (and is a condition of the insurance); sub-set scopes now require documented justification, segregation evidence and a list of in-scope legal entities.
4. **Map Cyber Essentials into the control set you already run** rather than maintaining it separately — it is a thin subset of ISO 27001 Annex A and CIS IG1. See [control-mapping](../../skills/control-mapping/SKILL.md) and the [framework crosswalk](../crosswalks/framework-crosswalk.md).
5. **Use CE/CE+ as the supplier-tier baseline** in third-party risk: it is cheap, verifiable against the public certificate search, and PPN 014 already obliges much of the public sector to ask for it. See [third-party-risk-assessment](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md) and [vendor-security-questionnaire](../../templates/vendor-security-questionnaire.md).
6. **For CAF-regulated entities, assess against the profile, not the whole framework**, and keep the evidence per contributing outcome with named owners — CAF assessments are judgement-based and stand or fall on evidence quality. See [audit-preparation](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md).
7. **Close the v4.0 gaps first:** threat understanding feeding risk decisions (A2.b), secure software development and supported software (A4.b), threat hunting (C2.a), board information and understanding (A1.a). These are new demands, not re-worded ones. See [risk-assessment](../../skills/risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
8. **Run the NHS DSPT and GovAssure cycles off the same CAF evidence base** — one outcome library, multiple consumers, with the DSPT's Objective E handled as a privacy overlay. See [regulatory-applicability](../../skills/regulatory-applicability/SKILL.md).
9. **Report both metrics to the board:** certificate status and expiry dates for CE/CE+ across group entities, and outcome-level achievement against the CAF profile with the improvement plan. See [grc-metrics-reporting](../../skills/grc-metrics-reporting/SKILL.md).

## Interplay

- **UK NIS / Cyber Security and Resilience Bill** — the CAF is the assessment instrument competent authorities use to judge "appropriate and proportionate" measures; see [uk-nis-cyber-security-resilience.md](../regulations/uk-nis-cyber-security-resilience.md).
- **EU NIS2** ([nis2.md](../regulations/nis2.md)) — outcome-based CAF supervision versus NIS2's prescriptive Article 21 measures and management-body liability. Group-level programmes need one control set mapped to both.
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** ([iso-27001-2022.md](iso-27001-2022.md)) — certification-grade management system; Cyber Essentials covers a fraction of Annex A but is the faster supply-chain signal. CAF's Objective A duplicates much of ISO's clause 4–10 governance, but CAF demands outcome evidence, not documented procedure. See also [iso27001-readiness](../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md).
- **NIST CSF 2.0** ([nist-csf-2.md](nist-csf-2.md)) — the closest structural analogue to the CAF (functions/categories versus objectives/principles); CSF profiles and CAF profiles serve the same purpose, and CSF's Govern function maps to CAF A1/A2.
- **CIS Controls v8/v8.1** ([cis-controls-v8.md](cis-controls-v8.md)) — IG1 is the nearest international equivalent to Cyber Essentials' five controls, and a good bridge for suppliers who hold one and are asked for the other.
- **PCI DSS v4.x** ([pci-dss-4.md](pci-dss-4.md)) — overlapping technical ground (firewalls, configuration, patching, MFA, malware) at much greater depth and with its own scoping rules; a CE scope is not a CDE scope.
- **UK GDPR / DPA 2018** ([uk-data-protection.md](../regulations/uk-data-protection.md)) — Cyber Essentials and CAF outcomes are evidence towards the Article 32-equivalent security duty, not compliance with it; neither scheme addresses lawfulness, transparency or data subject rights.
