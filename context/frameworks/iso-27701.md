# ISO/IEC 27701

## At a glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Owner / publisher | ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27; published jointly by ISO and IEC |
| Current version | ISO/IEC 27701:2025 — revised as a **standalone certifiable management system standard**. The 2019 first edition (an extension to ISO 27001:2013/27002:2013) remains widely held under transition arrangements — verify edition and transition deadlines with the certification body |
| Structure | 2019 edition: clause extensions to 27001/27002 + Annex A (controller controls) and Annex B (processor controls). 2025 edition: own harmonized clauses 4-10 plus privacy controls, certifiable without a 27001 certificate — verify structural details against the purchased text |
| Certifiable? | Yes. Under the 2019 edition only as an extension of an ISO 27001 certificate; the 2025 edition is designed for standalone certification (still commonly combined with 27001) |
| Typical use | Privacy information management system (PIMS) certification, processor/controller assurance to customers, operationalizing GDPR-style accountability, structuring a privacy program |
| Cost | Standard text is paid (purchase from ISO/IEC or national bodies) |

## What it is

ISO/IEC 27701 defines requirements for a **privacy information management system (PIMS)**: the management-system machinery for protecting personally identifiable information (PII), covering both **PII controllers** and **PII processors** (deliberately vendor-neutral terms that align with, but are not identical to, GDPR's controller/processor).

Its core move: take the ISMS discipline of [ISO 27001](iso-27001-2022.md) — scope, risk assessment, applicability statement, audit, review, improvement — and extend it with privacy-specific requirements and controls. Security asks "is the data protected?"; the PIMS additionally asks "should we have this data at all, on what basis, for what purpose, for how long, and what can the person do about it?"

## The 2019 edition: extension architecture

ISO/IEC 27701:2019 could not stand alone — it bolts onto ISO 27001:2013 and 27002:2013:

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| Clause 5 | PIMS-specific extensions to ISO 27001 requirements — notably extending the clause 4 context analysis (role determination: controller, processor, or both; applicable privacy legislation) and requiring privacy risk assessment alongside security risk assessment |
| Clause 6 | PIMS-specific guidance extending the ISO 27002 controls (e.g., what classification, access control, cryptography, and incident management mean when PII is in scope) |
| Clause 7 | Additional guidance for **PII controllers** |
| Clause 8 | Additional guidance for **PII processors** |
| Annex A | PIMS-specific reference control objectives and controls for **controllers** — selected via the SoA mechanism |
| Annex B | PIMS-specific reference control objectives and controls for **processors** |
| Further annexes | Mappings to ISO/IEC 29100, to the GDPR's articles, and to ISO/IEC 27018/29151 — verify annex lettering against the text before citing |

Certification consequence: a 27701:2019 certificate exists only as an extension of (or combined with) a 27001 certificate — there was no standalone PIMS certificate. Scope follows the ISMS scope plus the declared role(s): controller, processor, or both. Annex A vs. Annex B applicability is driven by that role determination.

### Controller-side themes (2019 Annex A)

Four objective groups:

- **Conditions for collection and processing** — purpose identification and documentation, lawful basis determination, consent mechanics where consent is the basis, records of processing (RoPA), privacy impact assessment triggers.
- **Obligations to PII principals** — notice/transparency, handling subject requests (access, correction, erasure, objection), information about automated decision-making, complaint handling.
- **Privacy by design and by default** — minimization at collection, accuracy, retention limits and enforced disposal, de-identification/pseudonymization, temporary-file handling.
- **PII sharing, transfer, and disclosure** — bases for transfers between jurisdictions, recording of disclosures to third parties (including authorities), joint-controller and recipient documentation.

### Processor-side themes (2019 Annex B)

- Processing only on the documented instructions of the customer; flagging instructions that would breach applicable law.
- Supporting the customer's own compliance obligations — subject-rights assistance, breach notification support on contractual clocks.
- Sub-processor engagement controls — customer authorization, flow-down of obligations, change notice.
- Transfer and disclosure limits — including handling of legally binding government/authority disclosure requests (notify the customer unless prohibited; disclose the minimum).
- Return, transfer, or disposal of PII at contract end, with evidence.

These track the shape of GDPR Art. 28 processor-contract duties closely — which is why the annex works well as a processor due-diligence checklist even outside certification.

## The 2025 revision: standalone standard

The revision published in 2025 restructures 27701 as a **full management system standard with its own harmonized clauses 4-10**, so a PIMS can be certified without an underlying ISO 27001 certificate. Key shifts to understand (and to verify against the official text — the edition is recent and secondary sources still conflate it with the 2019 design):

- **Decoupling from 27001**, while remaining designed for integration: organizations can now certify privacy alone (relevant for privacy-mature organizations whose security assurance runs on SOC 2, and for privacy office-led programs), or run 27001 + 27701 as an integrated ISMS/PIMS as before. Information security requirements the PIMS depends on are addressed within the standard's own structure rather than by reference to a certified ISMS — check how the edition handles this before scoping.
- **Alignment to the 2022-generation control catalog** (27001:2022/27002:2022) rather than the withdrawn 2013 texts, resolving the awkward period where 27701:2019 formally referenced 2013-numbered controls against 2022-certified ISMSs.
- **Transition arrangements**: existing 2019-based certification extensions transition on a timetable set through IAF/accreditation-body arrangements (multi-year windows are typical for ISO management system transitions). Do not assume a deadline — confirm with the accreditation body and certification body; during the window, both editions will be encountered in vendor reviews, so always record *which edition* a certificate is against.

### 2019 vs 2025 at a glance

| | 2019 edition | 2025 edition |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Extension document — modifies 27001/27002:2013 clauses and controls | Standalone management system standard with its own clauses 4-10 |
| Certifiable alone? | No — only as an extension of an ISO 27001 certificate | Yes — standalone PIMS certification possible; combined with 27001 remains common |
| Underlying control generation | 27001/27002:2013 numbering | Aligned to the 2022-generation catalog |
| Controller/processor split | Clause 7 + Annex A (controller); clause 8 + Annex B (processor) | Role-based requirements retained; verify placement in the published text |
| Encountered in the wild (2026) | Still the majority of existing certificates, transitioning | New certifications; edition mix during the transition window |

## Terminology: PII vs personal data

The standard uses ISO vocabulary (from ISO/IEC 29100): **PII**, **PII principal**, **PII controller**, **PII processor**. These map closely to GDPR's personal data / data subject / controller / processor but are not legally identical — the standard is written to work under any privacy regime (GDPR, LGPD, APPI, PIPEDA, US state laws — see [../regulations/us-state-privacy.md](../regulations/us-state-privacy.md) and [../regulations/other-jurisdictions.md](../regulations/other-jurisdictions.md)). In multi-jurisdiction programs this neutrality is the point: one PIMS, with jurisdiction-specific legal requirements captured through the clause 4 context analysis rather than hard-coded into the system.

## Relationship to GDPR: accountability evidence, not compliance

27701 is deliberately law-agnostic, but it is engineered around GDPR-shaped obligations, and this is its main commercial use:

- **Art. 5(2) / Art. 24 accountability**: the PIMS produces exactly the artifacts a controller needs to *demonstrate* compliance — documented roles, lawful-basis records, risk assessments, RoPA, training, audits, reviews.
- **Art. 28 processor obligations**: Annex B (2019) / the processor control set maps closely onto mandatory processor-contract terms, making a 27701-certified processor materially easier to contract with.
- **Art. 32 security**: inherited from the ISMS layer.

Say this plainly in any assessment output: **a 27701 certificate is not a GDPR compliance certificate.** Specifically:

- It is not an approved certification mechanism under GDPR Arts. 42-43 (no such ISO-based mechanism has been approved — verify current status, as EDPB-approved schemes evolve).
- It does not validate the substance of lawful-basis decisions, transfer mechanisms, or proportionality judgments — those are legal determinations outside a management-system audit's competence.
- A certified organization can still process unlawfully; the certificate evidences that a *system for managing* privacy obligations exists and operates.
- Regulators treat it as an accountability positive — relevant to demonstrating the "appropriate measures" posture, potentially relevant to enforcement tone — nothing more.

Statutory detail: [../regulations/gdpr.md](../regulations/gdpr.md).

## What the PIMS operationalizes

The practical value is turning legal obligations into operated, auditable processes:

- **RoPA (records of processing activities)** — maintained as controlled documented information with owners and review cadence, not a one-off spreadsheet. Minimum useful fields per activity: purpose, lawful basis, PII categories, principal categories, recipients, transfers and their mechanism, retention rule, security-measure reference, controller/processor role.
- **Purpose limitation and minimization** — purpose specification at collection, compatibility checks on new uses, minimization and retention rules enforced through the control set, disposal evidence.
- **Consent management** — capture, granularity, withdrawal as easy as giving, records demonstrating validity where consent is the basis.
- **Subject rights handling** — a request process with identity verification, clocks, fulfillment evidence, and escalation; processors get a mirror-image "assist the controller" process.
- **Privacy risk assessment** — extending the ISMS risk process to risks *to PII principals*, which is also the hinge into DPIA work: the PIMS decides when a DPIA is triggered and houses its outputs — methodology in [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **Breach handling** — privacy-specific assessment and notification duties wired into the security incident process; timelines in [../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md](../crosswalks/breach-notification-timelines.md) and [../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md](../../skills/incident-regulatory-reporting/SKILL.md).

## Integrated audits with 27001

Where both certificates are held (still the dominant pattern), audits run combined: one audit team, one visit, integrated findings — with privacy competence required on the team. Shared machinery (document control, internal audit, management review, corrective action) is audited once.

PIMS-specific evidence auditors expect on top of the ISMS trail:

- Role determination — controller, processor, or both, documented *per processing activity*, with applicable privacy legislation identified
- Privacy risk assessment (risks to PII principals) distinct from, but integrated with, the security risk assessment
- SoA covering the privacy control set with justified inclusions/exclusions per declared role
- RoPA under document control, with owners and review dates
- Subject-request log with timestamps against statutory clocks and fulfillment evidence
- Consent records where consent is a relied-upon basis
- Processor/sub-processor management evidence: contracts with privacy terms, authorization records, flow-down verification
- Breach-assessment records — including assessments that concluded "no notification required," with reasoning
- Retention schedule *plus* disposal execution evidence

Preparation mechanics: [../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md](../../skills/audit-preparation/SKILL.md) and [../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md](../../skills/iso27001-readiness/SKILL.md).

## Using this in assessments

- **Record the edition and the role scope.** A 27701 certificate means little until you know:
  - Edition: 2019-extension (and against which underlying 27001 certificate) or 2025-standalone
  - Role scope: controller controls, processor controls, or both
  - Processing scope: which activities/services the (ISMS+)PIMS scope statement actually covers — as with 27001, the scope statement is where assurance value is decided
  - Accreditation: certification body accredited for PIMS certification by a recognized accreditation body
  A processor certified only for controller controls (or vice versa) is a scoping red flag.
- **When to ask a processor for 27701 vs SOC 2 + DPA:**

  | Instrument | What it actually gives you | Native market |
  |---|---|---|
  | 27701 certificate | An accredited body certifies an operating privacy *management system*; maps directly onto Art. 28-shaped processor duties | EU / international |
  | SOC 2 with privacy criterion ([soc2-tsc.md](soc2-tsc.md)) | CPA attestation on control operating effectiveness over a period against defined criteria; privacy category is optional and often omitted — check the report scope | US |
  | DPA | The legally binding processing terms — instructions, sub-processors, assistance, breach notice, return/deletion | Everywhere; mandatory under GDPR |

  Practical rule: for processors handling large-scale or sensitive EU personal data, 27701 (or credible progress toward it) is a reasonable ask *on top of* the DPA; for low-risk processors, a solid DPA plus SOC 2 and a completed privacy questionnaire is usually proportionate. A certificate never replaces the DPA — the contract carries the legal obligations; the certificate evidences the machinery behind them.
- **In vendor reviews, request the privacy SoA and the RoPA excerpt** relevant to your services — as with 27001, exclusions and the processing inventory tell you more than the certificate. See [../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/third-party-risk-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **Gap assessments:** organizations with a mature ISMS typically fail on the genuinely privacy-native elements — role determination per activity, lawful-basis records, retention enforcement (not just a schedule), and subject-rights operations under load. Assess those first; the shared clause machinery is usually already fine. Mechanics: [../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/framework-gap-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **During the 2019→2025 transition window**, treat edition currency like the 27001 2013→2022 transition: a vendor still on the old edition late in the window signals program neglect. Confirm the actual deadline before flagging — see the verification note.
- **Highest-signal artifacts in a gap review:** the RoPA (does it exist, is it current, does it cover the activity in question) and the subject-request log (are clocks met under real volume). Programs fake policies convincingly; they cannot fake an operated request queue or a live processing inventory.
- **Watch the DPIA hinge.** The PIMS should contain documented criteria for when a DPIA is triggered and evidence of DPIAs performed or explicitly ruled out per activity. A PIMS with zero DPIA decisions recorded is either processing nothing risky or not operating — find out which. See [../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md](../../skills/dpia-privacy-assessment/SKILL.md).
- **Adjacent standards to keep straight** — neither substitutes for 27701:
  - ISO/IEC 27018 — PII protection in public cloud; a code of practice (control overlay) for cloud processors, not a management system standard
  - ISO/IEC 29100 — privacy framework and vocabulary; the terminology base, with nothing to certify
